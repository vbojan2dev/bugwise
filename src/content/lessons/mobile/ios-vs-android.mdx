A food-delivery startup builds its app on iPhones. Every tester owns an iPhone, every demo runs on an iPhone. Launch day goes well, until the Android reviews arrive: *"Pressing Back closes the whole app."* *"I can't get rid of the menu at the bottom."* *"It asked for notifications and then said they were on, but nothing ever came."*

None of these bugs existed on iOS. They came from **building one design and shipping it to two platforms** that work differently. Your job as a tester is to know those differences well enough to spot them, so let's learn them.

## Why it matters

iOS and Android are not just "two skins" of the same thing. They have different **navigation models**, different **UI components**, different **permission rules**, different **release processes**, and very different **device landscapes**.

- **Users notice.** People expect apps to behave like the rest of their phone. An Android user presses Back dozens of times a day; if your app ignores it, the app feels broken.
- **The code is often different.** Even cross-platform apps (React Native, Flutter, Kotlin Multiplatform) use platform-specific code for navigation, permissions and notifications. A fix on one platform does not fix the other.
- **Bugs hide on the platform nobody tests.** Teams usually have a "home" platform. The other one gets less attention and collects bugs.

<Callout type="tip" title="Tester mindset">
Never write "works on mobile" in a test report. Write **which platform, which device and which OS version** you tested on. "Passed on iPhone 15, iOS 26" says something; "passed on mobile" doesn't.
</Callout>

## The big differences at a glance

| Area | iOS | Android |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Going back** | Back chevron in the navigation bar + swipe from the left edge | System Back (edge swipe or ◁ button) on *every* screen, plus the arrow in the app bar |
| **Main navigation** | Tab bar at the bottom | Navigation bar (bottom), navigation rail or drawer on big screens |
| **Menus of actions** | Action sheet with a separate Cancel button | Bottom sheet or menu, dismissed by swipe, tap outside or Back |
| **Confirmations** | Centered alert, must tap a button | Material dialog, usually dismissible by Back or tapping outside |
| **Quick messages** | No built-in toast; banners or inline messages | Toasts and snackbars |
| **Permissions** | System prompt shown **once**; after that, only Settings | Can ask again; after two denials (Android 11+) the dialog stops appearing |
| **Notifications** | Always needs permission | Needs permission on Android 13+; users can mute **channels** |
| **System font** | SF Pro | Roboto in Material, but makers ship their own fonts |
| **Beta testing** | TestFlight | Play Console testing tracks, Firebase App Distribution |
| **Devices** | A few dozen iPhone and iPad models | Thousands of models from hundreds of makers |

## Navigation: the biggest source of bugs

### iOS: back chevron and edge swipe

On iOS, screens are stacked on top of each other. Each new screen slides in from the right and shows a **back chevron** (‹) with the previous screen's title. Users can also **swipe from the left edge** to drag the screen away. There is no system-wide Back button.

What breaks:

- **The swipe-back gesture is disabled**, often by accident when developers customize the navigation bar or hide the back button. Users swipe and nothing happens.
- A swipe-back on a screen with **unsaved changes** discards the data without asking.
- A half-finished swipe (start swiping, then let go) leaves the screen in a weird state.

### Android: system Back everywhere

Android has a **system Back** that works in every app: a swipe in from the left *or right* edge in gesture navigation, or the ◁ button in 3-button navigation. Your app must handle it on **every screen and every state**:

<Steps>

1. **Keyboard open?** Back closes the keyboard first.
2. **Dialog, sheet or menu open?** Back closes it, like tapping Cancel.
3. **On a deeper screen?** Back goes to the previous screen.
4. **On the start screen of the app?** Back leaves the app (the user lands on the home screen).

</Steps>

Newer Android versions add **predictive back**: when the user starts the back gesture, apps that support it show a preview of where they'll land (the previous screen or the home screen), and the user can cancel by sliding back. Since Android 15 these previews appear for apps that opted in; for apps that target Android 16 they're on by default. Test that the preview matches where you really end up.

<Callout type="bug" title="The classic cross-platform bug">
An Android app copied from an iOS design shows a "‹ Back" chevron on screen, but the developer only wired up the chevron. **System Back closes the app** instead of going back, or does nothing in a dialog. Always test system Back, never only the on-screen arrow.
</Callout>

For swipes, long presses and other touch input, see [Gestures](/mobile/gestures).

### Tabs vs bottom navigation

Both platforms put the main sections at the bottom, but they behave differently:

- **iOS tab bar:** tapping the active tab again usually scrolls to the top or returns to that tab's first screen. Each tab keeps its own history. In iOS 26, Apple's Liquid Glass design makes the tab bar float over the content, and apps can let it shrink as you scroll.
- **Android navigation bar:** the active destination gets a pill-shaped indicator. Pressing Back from another tab normally returns to the start tab before leaving the app.

## UI conventions that differ

| Component | iOS | Android (Material 3) | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Alert / dialog** | Centered, buttons side by side or stacked; destructive in red | Rounded dialog, left-aligned text, text buttons bottom-right | Back / tap outside counts as Cancel on Android, never as "Confirm" |
| **Menu of actions** | Action sheet with a Cancel button | Modal bottom sheet with a drag handle | No iOS action sheet in an Android app |
| **Switch** | Green pill (<Term id="toggle-switch">toggle switch</Term>) | Material switch, theme color, optional check icon | State announced by the <Term id="screen-reader">screen reader</Term> |
| **Date & time picker** | Compact field, inline calendar or scrolling wheels | Calendar dialog with a typing mode; clock-dial time picker | The native picker for each platform, correct first day of week |
| **Share** | iOS share sheet | Android Sharesheet | The system sheet opens with the right content |
| **Short feedback** | Banner or inline message (no native toast) | <Term id="toast">Toast</Term> or snackbar | Message isn't hidden behind the keyboard or nav bar |
| **Font** | SF Pro, scales with <Term id="dynamic-type">Dynamic Type</Term> | Roboto or the maker's font, scales with Font size (up to 200% on Android 14+) | No clipped text at the largest size on either |
| **Haptics** | Precise, consistent Taptic Engine | Vibration quality varies a lot between devices | Haptics feel right and can be turned off |

</Callout>

## Permissions: same idea, different rules

Both platforms ask the user before an app can use the camera, location, microphone, photos, contacts or notifications. These are <Term id="app-permissions">app permissions</Term> (see the [App permissions](/features/app-permissions) feature lesson), and the rules differ in ways that create real bugs.

<Tabs labels={['iOS', 'Android']}>
<Tab>

- The system prompt appears **only once per permission**. If the user taps **Don't Allow**, the app can never show it again. The only way back is the **Settings** app.
- Location offers **Allow Once**, **Allow While Using App** and **Don't Allow**, plus a **Precise** on/off switch.
- Photos can be shared as **limited access** (only selected photos).
- The text under the title is the app's own **purpose string**. Apple requires it, and App Review rejects vague ones.
- Good apps show their own explanation screen *before* triggering the one-shot system prompt.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- Permissions have been asked at **runtime** since Android 6. The app can ask again after a denial.
- Since Android 11, if the user denies the same permission **twice**, Android stops showing the dialog (it behaves like "Don't ask again"). The app must then send the user to Settings.
- Location, camera and microphone offer **Only this time** (Android 11+). The permission is revoked soon after the user leaves the app.
- Location offers **Precise** or **Approximate** (Android 12+). Photos can be shared partially ("Select photos", Android 14+).
- Android may **auto-reset permissions** of apps that haven't been used for a few months.

</Tab>
</Tabs>

What to test on both: every answer (allow, one-time, deny), revoking the permission in Settings while the app is in the background, and the "permanently denied" state, where the app must explain how to turn it back on instead of failing silently.

## Notifications

<Term id="push-notification">Push notifications</Term> look similar on both platforms, but the setup differs:

- **iOS** always needs the user's permission before showing alerts. The prompt appears once. Users can also choose how notifications appear (banners, lock screen, summary) and turn on Focus modes.
- **Android 13 and newer** require the `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` runtime permission, so notifications are off for a new install until the user allows them. On Android 12 and older, notifications are allowed by default.
- **Android notification channels** (Android 8+) group notifications by type, like "Order updates" and "Promotions". Users can mute one channel and keep the others.

Test that the app's own notification toggle **matches the real system permission** (more in [Push notifications](/features/push-notifications)). A switch that says "On" while the OS blocks notifications is one of the most common bugs in this area.

## Keyboards and autofill

- **Android has many keyboards**: Gboard, Samsung Keyboard, SwiftKey and others. Each one handles autocorrect, suggestions and emoji differently. Test with at least two.
- **iOS number pads have no Return key.** If the app doesn't add a Done button or tap-outside handling, the user can't close the keyboard and can't reach the button below it.
- **The keyboard covers content.** Check that the focused field and the main button stay visible on short screens.
- **Autofill:** iOS offers Password AutoFill and one-time codes from SMS above the keyboard; Android's Autofill framework works with Google Password Manager and other <Term id="password-manager">password managers</Term>. Fields must be marked correctly (username, new password, one-time code), or autofill won't appear.

## Distribution, review and beta testing

How a <Term id="build" /> reaches users is different too, and testers are often in charge of the beta builds (more in [Beta distribution](/mobile/beta-distribution)).

| | App Store (iOS) | Google Play (Android) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Beta testing** | <Term id="testflight">TestFlight</Term>: internal testers from your team, external testers after a quick Beta App Review; builds expire after 90 days | Testing tracks: **internal** (up to 100 testers, available within minutes), **closed** and **open** |
| **Other options** | <Term id="firebase-app-distribution">Firebase App Distribution</Term> for ad-hoc builds | Firebase App Distribution, or sharing APKs directly (sideloading) |
| **Review** | Every version is reviewed by Apple; most are reviewed within a day | Automated plus manual checks; usually hours, sometimes several days |
| **Gradual release** | Phased release over 7 days for automatic updates | Staged rollout to a percentage of users, can be halted |
| **Rollback** | No rollback: you must ship a new version | No rollback either, but you can halt a staged rollout |

<Callout type="warning" title="Why this matters for testing">
On both stores you can't instantly undo a bad <Term id="release" />. A serious bug means a new build, a new review and days of waiting. That's why the final regression run before submission matters so much more on mobile than on the web.
</Callout>

## OS updates and hardware variety

- **iOS users update fast.** Most iPhones move to the newest major iOS within a few months, so teams can drop old versions sooner. Apple also supports devices for many years, so a 6-year-old iPhone may still run the newest iOS.
- **Android is spread across many versions.** Updates depend on the phone maker and carrier, so at any time your users run five or more different Android versions. See [Device fragmentation](/mobile/device-fragmentation) for how to deal with this.
- **Hardware varies a lot more on Android:** screen sizes, cameras, chips, RAM, foldables and makers' own skins (Samsung One UI, Xiaomi HyperOS). Every <Term id="os-version">OS version</Term> and skin can change how permissions, notifications or background work behave.

## What to test

Group your platform checks like this:

- **Navigation:** system Back in every state (Android), edge swipe on every pushed screen (iOS), tabs keep their state, deep links land on the right screen with a sensible back path.
- **Platform UI:** native pickers, sheets and dialogs on each platform; dialogs dismiss correctly; text fits with large fonts.
- **Permissions:** allow, one-time, deny, permanently deny, revoke in Settings, then return to the app.
- **Notifications:** first-time prompt, denied state, the app's toggle vs the real system setting, Android channels, tapping a notification opens the right screen.
- **Keyboard and autofill:** keyboard can always be dismissed, fields stay visible, password and one-time-code autofill appear.
- **Lifecycle:** [backgrounding](/mobile/background-and-foreground), <Term id="interruption">interruptions</Term> like calls, and the OS killing the app. Both platforms do this, but differently.
- **Release:** install the TestFlight / internal-track build, and test <Term id="upgrade-testing">upgrading</Term> from the version in the store.

## Tools and how-to

<Tabs labels={['iOS', 'Android']}>
<Tab>

- **Xcode Simulator:** test layouts, permissions and navigation on many iPhone and iPad models. The edge swipe works with a mouse drag from the left edge. It's a <Term id="simulator" />, so camera, some sensors and real performance need a <Term id="real-device" />.
- **Reset permissions:** on a device, delete and reinstall the app, or use Settings › General › Transfer or Reset iPhone › Reset › Reset Location & Privacy. On the simulator: `xcrun simctl privacy booted reset all <bundle-id>`.
- **Test push on the simulator:** drag an `.apns` payload file onto the simulator window, or run `xcrun simctl push booted <bundle-id> payload.apns`.
- **TestFlight:** install the TestFlight app, accept the invite, and always note the build number you tested.
- **Large text:** Settings › Accessibility › Display & Text Size › Larger Text.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- **Android Studio Emulator:** create devices with different Android versions. Switch between gesture and 3-button navigation in Settings › System › Navigation mode (the exact path varies by phone maker).
- **Revoke a permission with <Term id="adb" />:** `adb shell pm revoke <package> android.permission.POST_NOTIFICATIONS`, or use Settings › Apps › your app › Permissions.
- **Read crash logs:** `adb logcat` shows <Term id="logcat" /> output; filter by your app's package name.
- **Notification channels:** long-press a notification, or open Settings › Apps › your app › Notifications to mute single channels.
- **Internal testing:** join via the opt-in link from Play Console, then install from the Play Store. Note the version code.
- **Font size:** Settings › Display › Display size and text (called Font size on some phones).

</Tab>
</Tabs>

<KeyTakeaways>

- iOS and Android differ in **navigation, UI components, permissions, notifications, releases and devices**. Always test both, and always say which one you tested.
- The number one cross-platform bug: **Android system Back not handled**. On iOS, check that the **edge swipe** works.
- Each platform should use **its own** dialogs, sheets, pickers and share sheet. iOS patterns in an Android app (or vice versa) are valid findings.
- iOS shows each permission prompt **once**; Android stops asking after **two denials**. Test the denied path and make sure the app's toggles match the real OS setting.
- Android 13+ needs the **notification permission**; Android channels let users mute types of notifications.
- Releases can't be undone instantly, so test the **TestFlight / internal-testing build** before it goes out.

</KeyTakeaways>
