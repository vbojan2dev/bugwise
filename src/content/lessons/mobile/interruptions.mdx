Maya is paying for concert tickets in her banking app. She types €25.00, adds a note, taps **Send**, and her phone rings. It's her mom. She declines, goes back to the app, sees the spinner still turning and the form looking oddly empty. A minute later she gets two "Payment sent" notifications. **She paid twice.**

Nobody on the team had ever tested "phone call during a payment". On a desk with a test phone, nobody calls you. In real life, people get calls, messages and alarms in the middle of everything.

## Why it matters

An <Term id="interruption" /> is anything that pulls the user, the screen or the audio away from your app for a moment. Your app doesn't choose when that happens, and it can't stop it. It can only **react well**.

Interruption bugs are nasty because:

- **They lose real things:** a typed form, workout progress, a video position, sometimes money.
- **They don't show up in normal testing.** You have to cause them on purpose.
- **Users blame the app, not the phone call.** "The app deleted my message" ends up in a one-star review.

The good news: once you know the list of interruptions and the four things that should happen, testing them is quick and very rewarding.

## Types of interruptions

Not all interruptions are equal. Some only cover part of the screen, some take over the audio, some send the app to the background completely.

| Interruption | What the phone does | What a well-behaved app does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Incoming call (ringing)** | Full-screen call UI or a banner. Ringtone plays. | Pause video and audio. Keep the screen state. |
| **Call accepted** | Call audio takes over. The app goes to the background. | Stay paused. Save drafts. Don't time out the session too early. |
| **Call declined / missed** | You're back in the app within seconds. | Everything exactly as it was. No double actions. |
| **SMS / notification banner** | A banner slides over the top for a few seconds. | **Nothing.** The app keeps running. Content stays usable. |
| **Alarm or timer** | Full-screen alarm and loud sound. | Pause media. Resume cleanly when the alarm is stopped. |
| **Low battery alert / Low Power Mode** | A system alert. Low Power Mode limits background work. | Keep working. Reduce heavy work if needed. Never reset the screen. |
| **Headphones connect / disconnect** | Audio switches between headphones and speaker. | **Pause** playback when headphones are removed. Don't auto-play when they connect. |
| **Siri / Google Assistant** | Assistant UI and audio take over. | Pause audio like for a call. |
| **Screen lock (power button or auto-lock)** | Screen turns off. The app goes to the background. | Pause video. Keep timers correct. Restore the screen after unlock. |
| **OS update or permission prompts** | A system dialog appears on top. | Keep state. Continue after the dialog closes. |
| **Another app's audio** | Music or a podcast is already playing. | Decide on purpose: pause the other audio, lower it ("ducking"), or mix with it. |

<Callout type="info" title="Small interruptions vs big ones">
A **banner** or the **notification center** covers part of the screen, but the app stays in the foreground (pulling down the notification center makes it briefly "inactive" on iOS). A **call you accept**, the **lock screen** or switching apps sends it to the **background**, which is where most bugs live. The full story is in [Background and Foreground](/mobile/background-and-foreground).
</Callout>

## What should happen

Whatever the interruption is, a well-behaved app does the same four things. Use them as your test oracle: if one fails, you've found a bug.

<Steps>

1. **Pause what should pause.** Video, music, games and voice guidance stop when a call, alarm or assistant takes over. They must never play *under* a phone call.
2. **Save what the user typed.** Form drafts, messages, unsaved edits and the current screen survive the interruption, even if the OS later kills the app in the background.
3. **Keep time correct.** Timers that measure real time (a workout, a parking meter, an OTP countdown) show the right value afterwards: no freezing, no jumping, no secretly restarting a timer the user paused.
4. **Resume cleanly.** After the interruption you're back exactly where you left off, with nothing done twice. A payment, an order or a message is sent **once**, not again "because the app resumed".

</Steps>

## What to test

Group your interruption testing by **what the user is doing** when it hits. The most valuable moments are the ones where losing state hurts.

### During media

- Video or music playing → incoming call, alarm, Siri, headphones removed.
- After the interruption: is it paused at the same position? Does it resume only when the user asks (or automatically, if that's the spec)?
- With Spotify already playing → start your app's video. What happens to Spotify?

### During input

- Half-filled form (sign-up, address, chat message, review) → call, then decline; call, then accept and talk for a minute; lock screen.
- Is every field still filled? Is the keyboard or focus in a sensible place?

### During something that counts time

- Workout, cooking timer, parking session, OTP code countdown, quiz with a time limit.
- Lock the phone for 2 minutes, then unlock: does the timer show the **real** elapsed time? Is a paused timer still paused?

### During a transaction

- Payment, order placement, booking, file upload, sending a message.
- Interrupt **while the spinner is showing**. Afterwards: one charge, one order, one message. The screen shows the final result, not an endless spinner. See <Term id="double-submit" /> and <Term id="idempotency" />.

### During audio or video calls inside your app

- If your app makes calls itself (WhatsApp-style VoIP, video consultation, ride-hailing driver calls), test a normal phone call arriving during them. Which call wins? Can the user switch?

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Media plays under a call** | Video audio continues while you're talking on the phone | Medium |
| 2 | **Form draft lost** | Back from a call, the fields you typed are empty | Medium–High |
| 3 | **Double transaction** | Payment or order sent again after returning from a call or unlock | Critical |
| 4 | **Timer wrong after interruption** | Workout froze while locked, jumped after unlock, or a paused timer is running again | Medium–High |
| 5 | **Audio blasts from the speaker** | Headphones disconnect and the video keeps playing out loud | Medium |
| 6 | **Auto-play on connect** | Headphones connect and the app starts playing without being asked | Low–Medium |
| 7 | **Blank screen or crash after unlock** | White screen, frozen UI or a <Term id="crash" /> when you return | High–Critical |
| 8 | **Endless spinner** | A request finished while you were away, but the app never shows the result | High |
| 9 | **Session expired during a short call** | A 2-minute call logs you out and loses your progress | Medium |
| 10 | **Banner breaks the layout** | A notification banner shifts content or blocks a button permanently | Low |

<Callout type="bug" title="Why bug #3 happens">
Many apps run some code every time they come back to the screen (for example "if a payment is pending, send it"). If that code doesn't check whether the request **already went out**, every return from an interruption sends another one. The fix on the server side is an **idempotency key**: the same payment ID sent twice is processed only once.
</Callout>

## Tools & how-to: triggering interruptions

You don't need special hardware. A second phone and the built-in emulator tools cover almost everything.

<Tabs labels={['Real device', 'Android Emulator', 'iOS Simulator']}>
<Tab>

The most realistic option, and the only one for some interruptions.

- **Calls:** call your test phone from a second phone (or ask a colleague). Test **ringing → decline**, **ringing → accept, talk 30 s, hang up**, and **missed** (let it ring out). Also try a WhatsApp or FaceTime call, since VoIP apps use the same system call screen.
- **SMS and notifications:** text yourself, or send a push from your team's test tool or Firebase console.
- **Alarms and timers:** open the Clock app, set a **timer for 10 seconds**, switch to your app and start the action you want to interrupt. For alarms, set one for the next minute.
- **Low battery:** test on a phone below 20%, or turn on **Low Power Mode** (iOS: Settings › Battery) / **Battery Saver** (Android: Settings › Battery).
- **Headphones:** pair Bluetooth earbuds, start playback, then take them out of your ears or put them back in the case. Also try turning Bluetooth off.
- **Siri / Assistant:** hold the side button (iOS) or say "Hey Google" during playback.
- **Lock:** press the power button, wait, unlock. Also let **auto-lock** kick in by not touching the phone.

</Tab>
<Tab>

Open the **Extended controls** with the **⋯** button on the emulator's side toolbar.

- **Phone:** enter any number and click **Call device** to simulate an incoming call (you can answer, decline and hang up from the emulator). **Send message** delivers a fake SMS.
- **Battery:** set the charge level (for example 15%), unplug the charger and change the battery status.
- **Power button** on the side toolbar locks and unlocks the screen.

The same with <Term id="adb" /> commands, handy for repeating a test:

```bash
adb emu gsm call 5551234          # incoming call
adb emu gsm cancel 5551234        # caller hangs up
adb emu sms send 5551234 "Hi!"    # incoming SMS
adb shell dumpsys battery unplug
adb shell dumpsys battery set level 15
adb shell dumpsys battery reset   # back to normal
```

</Tab>
<Tab>

The iOS <Term id="simulator" /> **cannot** receive real phone calls or SMS, and it has no real battery, Bluetooth or alarm sound. Use it for what it can do, and test the rest on a <Term id="real-device" />.

- **Lock:** Device › Lock (⌘L). **Home:** Device › Home (⇧⌘H).
- **Siri:** Device › Siri.
- **Push notifications:** drag an `.apns` file onto the Simulator, or run `xcrun simctl push booted com.example.app payload.apns`.
- **Battery icon:** `xcrun simctl status_bar booted override --batteryLevel 15` only **changes the icon** (handy for screenshots). It does not trigger low-battery behavior.

</Tab>
</Tabs>

<Callout type="warning" title="Emulators are not enough">
An <Term id="emulator" /> call doesn't route audio like a real call, and the iOS Simulator can't do calls at all. Plan at least one round of interruption testing on real phones (one iPhone, one Android) before every release that touches media, forms or payments.
</Callout>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Interruptions (calls, notifications, alarms, low battery, headphones, assistants, screen lock) happen to every user, every day. Test them **on purpose**.
- A well-behaved app does four things: **pause** what should pause, **save** what the user typed, keep **time correct**, and **resume cleanly** without doing anything twice.
- The most valuable moments to interrupt: **during media, input, timers and transactions**.
- Use a **second phone** and the **Clock app** on real devices, **Extended controls** or `adb emu` on the Android Emulator. The iOS Simulator can't do calls.
- Interruptions often push the app to the background. Continue with [Background and Foreground](/mobile/background-and-foreground).

</KeyTakeaways>
