Maria is booking a hotel for a weekend in Porto on the train. She fills in her name, email and the guests, then turns the phone sideways to watch the hotel's video tour. The video starts from the beginning. She turns the phone back to finish the booking. The form is **empty**. She sighs, closes the app and books on a competitor's site instead.

Nobody on the team had rotated the phone in the middle of the form. On their test devices the screen looked fine in both orientations. It just forgot everything in between.

## Why it matters

Users rotate their phones all the time: to watch a video, to see a wide photo, to type on a bigger keyboard, or simply because they put the phone down on a table. They also use **many different screens**: a small iPhone SE, a huge Pro Max, an iPad, a foldable that turns from phone to tablet mid-session, or a split screen next to another app.

Every one of these changes the space the app has. Two kinds of bugs appear:

- **Layout bugs:** content hidden behind the notch or home indicator, buttons pushed off screen, text stretched into unreadable lines, dialogs cut off.
- **State bugs:** on Android, rotation **re-creates** the screen. Anything the app didn't save (a half-filled form, a scroll position, a playing video) is gone. This is a classic <Term id="state-persistence" /> bug.

## Portrait, landscape and rotation lock

<Term id="orientation">Orientation</Term> is simply how the user holds the device: **portrait** (upright) or **landscape** (sideways). What the app does about it depends on three settings:

| Setting | Who controls it | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rotation lock / Auto-rotate off** | The user (iOS Control Center, Android Quick Settings) | The screen stays in portrait no matter how the phone is held. Android shows a small rotate button in the navigation bar so users can rotate once. Landscape-only apps (some games, video players) still open in landscape |
| **Supported orientations** | The app (Info.plist on iOS, `screenOrientation` in the Android manifest) | Many phone apps are **portrait only**: banking, food delivery, social apps. Rotating the phone does nothing in those apps |
| **Upside down** | The app and OS | Face ID iPhones don't support upside-down portrait; many Android apps don't either. Tablets usually support all four orientations |

<Callout type="info" title="Portrait-only isn't always allowed">
iPad apps that support multitasking are expected to support all orientations. And starting with **Android 16**, apps targeting that version can no longer lock orientation or resizability on **large screens** (tablets, unfolded foldables): the system ignores the restriction. A "portrait-only" app suddenly shows up in landscape on a tablet, so those layouts need testing too.
</Callout>

## What happens when the phone rotates

The platforms handle rotation very differently, and that difference explains most rotation bugs.

<Tabs labels={['Android', 'iOS']}>
<Tab>

By default, rotating the device is a **configuration change**. Android **destroys the current screen (the Activity) and creates it again** with the new size. It's like closing and reopening the screen in a fraction of a second.

- Data the app keeps or saves for re-creation survives.
  <DevNote>Data kept in a **ViewModel** or saved with **onSaveInstanceState** (`rememberSaveable` in Jetpack Compose).</DevNote>
- Standard text fields restore their text automatically, but custom views, timers, video players and network calls started by the screen are lost or restarted unless the developer saved them.
  <DevNote>Text fields need an ID for this. Compose `remember` state is lost as well.</DevNote>
- The same re-creation happens for other configuration changes: **folding/unfolding**, entering **split screen**, changing **dark mode**, **font size** or **language**, and connecting a keyboard. One test, many triggers.

Some apps handle rotation themselves. That avoids re-creation but can cause layouts that never update.

<DevNote>They opt out with `android:configChanges`.</DevNote>

</Tab>
<Tab>

iOS does **not** re-create the screen. The same view controller stays alive, and the system tells it that its **size** and **size classes** changed. The layout is recalculated.

<DevNote>With Auto Layout or SwiftUI.</DevNote>

- Data loss is rare, but **layout** bugs are common: constraints that only work in portrait, scroll views that jump, keyboards and dialogs that don't fit.
- iOS describes space with **size classes**: *compact* or *regular* width and height. Most iPhones are compact width in portrait and compact **height** in landscape; the larger "Max/Plus" models get regular width in landscape. iPads are regular in both directions, until they are put into a split screen.
- The status bar is hidden in landscape on iPhone, and the safe area moves to the sides.

</Tab>
</Tabs>

## Safe areas: the parts of the screen you can't fully use

Modern screens aren't simple rectangles. They have **rounded corners**, a **notch** or **Dynamic Island** for the cameras, a **home indicator** at the bottom, and system bars. The <Term id="safe-area" /> is the rectangle where content won't be covered or clipped. Backgrounds and images can extend beyond it, but text and buttons should stay inside.

| Device and orientation | Typical safe-area insets |
| --- | --- |
| iPhone SE, portrait | Top 20 pt (status bar), nothing else |
| iPhone SE, landscape | None at all (status bar hidden, no home indicator) |
| iPhone 16 Pro Max, portrait | Top 62 pt (Dynamic Island + status bar), bottom 34 pt (home indicator) |
| iPhone 16 Pro Max, landscape | Left **and** right 62 pt, bottom 21 pt, top 0 |
| iPad with Face ID | Top 24 pt, bottom 20 pt, in both orientations |
| Android phone | Status bar on top (often 24–48 dp, more around a camera hole), navigation bar or gesture handle at the bottom; since Android 15, apps targeting it draw **edge-to-edge** and must handle these insets themselves |

Notice that in landscape the iPhone's island is on **one** side, yet iOS insets **both** sides, so content stays centered whichever way the phone is turned.

## Points, pixels and dp

Screens have very different pixel densities, so apps don't measure in pixels. They use **logical units** that look roughly the same size on every screen:

| Unit | Platform | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **px** (physical pixel) | All | One dot on the screen. A Pro Max has 1320 × 2868 of them |
| **pt** (point) | iOS | Logical unit. 1 pt = 2 px on @2x screens, 3 px on @3x screens. The Pro Max is 440 × 956 pt |
| **dp** (density-independent pixel) | Android | Logical unit. 1 dp = 1 px at 160 dpi; px = dp × (dpi ÷ 160) |
| **sp** (scale-independent pixel) | Android | Like dp, but also scales with the user's font size setting. Used for text |
| **CSS px** | Web | Logical unit in browsers, similar to pt and dp. See <Term id="viewport" /> |

For testers this matters because **requirements, designs and bug reports speak in logical units**. "The button is 40 pt tall" is a fact you can check against the 44 × 44 pt minimum <Term id="touch-target" />; "the button is 120 px tall" means nothing until you know the density.

Users can also change the logical size: **Display Zoom** on iPhone (Settings › Display & Brightness › Display Zoom › Larger Text) makes a big iPhone behave like a smaller one, and **Display size** on Android (Settings › Display) changes the dp width of the screen. Together with font size (<Term id="dynamic-type">Dynamic Type</Term> on iOS), these are some of the most common sources of cut-off text.

## Tablets, foldables and split screen

- **Tablets** have room for more: two panes (list + detail), sidebars, grids. A stretched phone layout on a tablet is a usability bug.
- **Foldables** change size **while the app is running**. A Galaxy Z Fold goes from a tall, narrow cover screen to an almost square inner screen. Flip-style phones have a tiny cover screen. The app must keep its state and switch layout, and avoid putting buttons right on the **hinge**.
- **Split screen and windows.** On iPad (Split View and Slide Over on older versions, Stage Manager, and the freely resizable, tileable windows of iPadOS 26) and Android (split screen, free-form windows on tablets), the app gets only part of the screen. A tablet app in a 50/50 split is suddenly phone-sized.

Android groups widths into **window size classes**: *compact* (under 600 dp: phones in portrait), *medium* (600–839 dp: tablets in portrait, unfolded foldables) and *expanded* (840 dp and up: tablets in landscape; newer guidance splits very wide windows further into *large*, from 1200 dp, and *extra-large*, from 1600 dp). Heights under 480 dp are *compact* (phones in landscape). These are good test buckets.

## The keyboard and video in landscape

Two features get very tight in landscape:

- **The keyboard.** In landscape the on-screen keyboard can cover more than half of the screen. The focused field must stay visible, dialogs must move up or scroll, and the action button (Send, Apply, Log in) must be reachable. Some Android keyboards switch to a full-screen editing mode in landscape, which hides your app entirely while typing.
- **Video.** Users expect a playing video to go **full screen** when they rotate to landscape and return when they rotate back, **without restarting** and without losing the playback position, subtitles or quality setting. Picture-in-picture and AirPlay/Cast add more transitions to try.

## What to test

**Rotation and state**

- Rotate on every screen with input, scroll position, selection, a playing video, an open dialog or menu, and during loading.
- Rotate several times quickly, and rotate while a network request is running (is it sent twice?).
- Change other configurations too: fold/unfold, split screen, dark mode, font size. See also [Background and Foreground](/mobile/background-and-foreground).

**Layout**

- Nothing important under the notch, Dynamic Island, camera hole, rounded corners or home indicator, in **both** landscape directions.
- Landscape uses the extra width sensibly; text lines stay readable (roughly 45–75 characters).
- Smallest supported phone, largest phone, tablet, foldable (folded and unfolded), split screen.
- Largest font size and Display Zoom / Display size, which together shrink the available space a lot.

**Keyboard, dialogs and media**

- With the keyboard open in landscape, the focused field and its buttons are visible or reachable by scrolling.
- Videos go full screen and keep playing across rotation.

**Locked orientation**

- With rotation lock on, rotating does nothing. In portrait-only apps, check that rotation can't sneak in (for example through a video player or a web view).

### Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **State lost on rotation** | Form fields empty, selection reset, wizard back to step 1 | High |
| 2 | **Content under the notch / island** | Title, back button or first list item hidden or untappable in landscape | Medium–High |
| 3 | **Content under the home indicator** | Bottom button overlaps the home indicator; swiping up to go home taps the button instead | Medium |
| 4 | **Video restarts or stays small** | Rotating jumps back to 0:00, or the video doesn't go full screen | Medium |
| 5 | **Stretched layout** | Phone layout scaled up to a tablet or landscape: 120-character lines, giant buttons, empty space | Low–Medium |
| 6 | **Keyboard hides input** | In landscape the focused field or dialog buttons are covered and can't be scrolled to | High |
| 7 | **Scroll position reset** | Rotate halfway down a long list and you're back at the top | Medium |
| 8 | **Duplicate requests** | Rotating re-sends a request (two orders, two messages) | High |
| 9 | **Cut-off dialogs and sheets** | A dialog is taller than the landscape screen and has no scrolling | Medium |
| 10 | **Crash on rotation** | The app closes when rotated during loading or with a dialog open | Critical |

## Tools and how-to

<Tabs labels={['iOS', 'Android', 'Web']}>
<Tab>

**Xcode Simulator**

- Rotate with **Device › Rotate Left / Rotate Right** (⌘← / ⌘→).
- Use **File › Open Simulator** (or **Window › Devices and Simulators**) to add the smallest and largest supported iPhones and an iPad. Check the app's minimum supported iOS version to know which devices matter.
- Test iPad multitasking: on iPadOS 26, resize and tile windows with the window controls in the top-left corner; on older versions, use the "…" multitasking control at the top center.
- Change **Display Zoom** and **Larger Text** in the Simulator's Settings app; **I/O › Keyboard › Toggle Software Keyboard** (⌘K) brings the on-screen keyboard back if a hardware keyboard hides it.

**Real devices**

- Toggle **Portrait Orientation Lock** in Control Center to test both modes.
- Rotate in both directions: landscape left and landscape right put the Dynamic Island on opposite sides.

</Tab>
<Tab>

**Android Emulator**

- Rotate with the toolbar buttons or **Ctrl+←/→** (⌘ on a Mac).
- Create a **Resizable** emulator, or the Pixel Fold and Pixel Tablet images, to switch between phone, foldable and tablet sizes and to fold/unfold from the extended controls.

**<Term id="adb" /> commands**

```bash
# Lock rotation and force landscape (0 = portrait, 1 = landscape, 3 = reverse landscape)
adb shell settings put system accelerometer_rotation 0
adb shell settings put system user_rotation 1

# Pretend to be a smaller or denser screen, then reset
adb shell wm size 1080x1920
adb shell wm density 480
adb shell wm size reset
adb shell wm density reset
```

**Developer options**

- **Don't keep activities** destroys every screen when you leave it, a quick way to find the same lost-state bugs as rotation.
- **Force activities to be resizable** lets you put even "portrait-only" apps into split screen.

</Tab>
<Tab>

Mobile websites have orientation and safe-area bugs too.

- In Chrome <Term id="devtools">DevTools</Term>, open the device toolbar (Ctrl/⌘+Shift+M) and use the **rotate** button next to the device size.
- On iPhone, a page only draws under the notch if it uses `viewport-fit=cover`; it must then pad content with `env(safe-area-inset-left)` and friends. Test in Safari on a real iPhone or the Simulator in both landscape directions.
- Rotation doesn't reload a web page, but `orientationchange` or `resize` handlers can reset forms, carousels or scroll position. See <Term id="responsive-design" />.

</Tab>
</Tabs>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Rotation is a **change of size**. On Android it also **re-creates the screen** by default, so test that input, scroll position, video and dialogs survive it.
- Keep content inside the **safe area**: notch, Dynamic Island, camera holes, rounded corners and the home indicator. Test **both** landscape directions.
- Think in **logical units** (pt, dp), not pixels. Display Zoom, display size and font size change the space too.
- Landscape, tablets, foldables and split screen each need a layout that **uses the space**, not a stretched phone screen.
- The keyboard and full-screen video are the classic landscape trouble spots.
- Always **change something, then rotate**. And repeat the same checks for folding, split screen and other configuration changes.

</KeyTakeaways>
