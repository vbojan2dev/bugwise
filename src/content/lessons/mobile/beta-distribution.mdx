Friday afternoon, a banking app team. The developer says "the transfer bug is fixed, can you check?" The tester opens the app, tries a transfer, and it fails again. Bug reopened. Developer: "Works for me." Twenty minutes of back-and-forth later they find the problem: the tester was still on **yesterday's build**. The fix was in a build that had arrived in TestFlight an hour earlier, which nobody had installed.

That's the whole topic in one story. On mobile you can't just refresh a page to get the latest code. Every change has to become a new <Term id="build" />, get delivered to your phone, and be installed. Knowing how that delivery works, and **always knowing which build you're holding**, is a basic tester skill.

## Why beta distribution exists

Developers can install an app straight from Xcode or Android Studio onto a phone plugged into their computer. That's fine for one person, but a team needs something better:

- **Real builds on real phones.** Testers need the same kind of build users will get (signed, optimized, with push notifications and payments working), installed on <Term id="real-device">real devices</Term>.
- **Many testers, no cables.** QA, product managers, designers and sometimes thousands of customers need to install new builds with one tap.
- **Before the store.** Once something is in the App Store or Google Play, every user gets it. Beta distribution is the last safety net before a <Term id="release" />.
- **Feedback and crash reports** from testers flow back to the developers, tied to the exact build.

There are three main channels you'll meet: **TestFlight** (iOS), **Google Play testing tracks** (Android) and **Firebase App Distribution** (mostly Android, also iOS).

## TestFlight (iOS)

<Term id="testflight" /> is Apple's beta service, part of App Store Connect. Testers install Apple's free **TestFlight** app from the App Store, and beta apps are installed through it.

| | Internal testers | External testers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who | Members of your App Store Connect team (with a role like Developer or Marketing) | Anyone with an email address |
| How many | Up to 100 | Up to 10,000 |
| How they join | Added in App Store Connect, invited by email | Email invitation or a **public link** |
| Review | None: builds are available as soon as they're processed | The first build of a version needs **Beta App Review** (usually quick) |
| Typical use | Daily QA builds | Wider beta with customers or other departments |

Things every TestFlight tester should know:

- **Builds expire after 90 days.** An expired build can't be opened anymore. If someone reports a bug on an expired build, it needs to be checked on a current one.
- **"What to Test" notes** come with every build. Read them first: they tell you what changed and what the team wants checked.
- **Version and build** are shown on every build, for example *Version 2.4.0 (318)*. Many builds can share one version; the number in brackets is the one that matters.
- **Previous builds.** In the TestFlight app you can install an older build of the same app (as long as it hasn't expired). Useful for checking "did this work before?" and for <Term id="upgrade-testing">upgrade tests</Term>.
- **Feedback with screenshots.** Take a screenshot while using a beta app, tap **Share Beta Feedback**, add a comment and send it. After a crash, TestFlight can ask you to share details. Both arrive in App Store Connect together with the device, OS and build number.
- **Installing over the store app.** A TestFlight build replaces the App Store version of the same app (and keeps its data). To go back, reinstall from the App Store.

<Callout type="tip" title="Turn on automatic updates, or don't">
TestFlight can update beta apps automatically. That's convenient, but it can also swap your build in the middle of a test session. Many testers turn automatic updates **off** and install new builds on purpose, after finishing what they were doing.
</Callout>

## Google Play testing tracks (Android)

Google Play has its own beta system built into the Play Console. The team uploads a build to a **track**, and testers who joined that track get it through the normal Play Store app, like a regular update.

| Track | Who can join | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Internal testing** | Up to 100 testers chosen by email | Fastest: available within minutes. Great for daily QA builds. |
| **Closed testing** | Invited testers (email lists or Google Groups) | For a bigger, controlled group. At the time of writing, new personal developer accounts must run a closed test with at least 12 testers for 14 days before they can publish to production. |
| **Open testing** | Anyone, from the app's Play Store listing | A public beta. Your app's store page shows a "Join the beta" option. |

Testers first **opt in** with a link from the team, then install or update the app from Google Play. To go back to the public version, they leave the testing program and reinstall.

## Firebase App Distribution

<Term id="firebase-app-distribution" /> sends builds **directly** to testers, without going through a store track. That makes it popular for very early builds, feature branches and debug builds.

<Steps>

1. **You get an email invitation** for the app and accept it in your browser.
2. **Android:** you install the **App Tester** app (the email guides you). It lists every build you've been given, with release notes. Because App Tester installs apps outside Google Play, Android asks you to allow **Install unknown apps** for it once.
3. **iOS:** you register your device through a web page (Firebase adds an App Tester web clip to your home screen). iOS builds have to be made for registered devices, so a newly added iPhone may have to wait for the next build.
4. **Install the build**, open it, and you're testing. New builds show up in App Tester with an email notification.

</Steps>

Developers can upload **APK** files, which install directly, or **AAB** (Android App Bundle) files, which Firebase delivers through Google Play's internal app sharing (that needs the Firebase project linked to Google Play). Firebase builds expire after 150 days.

<Callout type="warning" title="Only from links you trust">
Allowing "Install unknown apps" for App Tester is fine. Never allow it for a browser or messaging app just because someone sent you an APK file. Builds should only come from your team's distribution tool.
</Callout>

## Environment builds: dev, staging, prod

Teams often build the same app version for different <Term id="environment">environments</Term>:

| Build | Talks to | Usually looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dev** | Developer or test servers, often unstable | Icon with "DEV" badge, name "FreshCart DEV" |
| **Staging** | <Term id="staging" /> servers that copy production, with test data | Orange "STG" badge, staging banner in the app |
| **Production** | Real <Term id="production" /> servers, real money | The real icon and name, like the store version |

Different icons and names let you install several versions side by side and tell them apart at a glance. But they also cause classic mix-ups:

- A tester reports "test promo banner on the home screen", but it's **staging content**, not an app bug.
- A bug is "fixed" on staging but still happens on production, because the fix was **on the server** and wasn't deployed there yet.
- A tester uses a production build with a test card and the payment fails, because production rejects test cards.

## Always know which build you're testing

Before you report or retest anything, check three things:

1. **Version and build number**, for example 2.4.0 (318). Good apps show it in **Settings › About**; TestFlight and App Tester show it too. On Android you'll find it in **Settings › Apps › [app]**.
2. **Environment** (dev, staging or production). Look at the icon, the app name, a banner, or the About screen.
3. **How you got it** (TestFlight, Play internal track, Firebase), and whether it was a fresh install or an update.

Then copy them into every <Term id="bug-report" />. See [Writing bug reports](/tutorials/writing-bug-reports) and the version numbers section of [Install, Update and Uninstall](/mobile/install-update-uninstall).

## When a new build arrives

A new build in TestFlight or App Tester is your cue for a small routine:

<Steps>

1. **Read the release notes** ("What to Test"). What's new, what's fixed, what's a known issue, which environment it uses.
2. **Install it and confirm** the version, build number and environment in the About screen.
3. **Smoke test.** A quick <Term id="smoke-testing">smoke test</Term>: the app opens, you can log in, the main flows (search, cart, checkout) work. If it's badly broken, report that right away so nobody wastes time.
4. **Verify fixed bugs.** For every bug listed as fixed in *this* build, do <Term id="retesting" /> with the original steps. Close it, or reopen it with the new build number.
5. **Test the new features** the notes mention.
6. **Regression.** Check the areas around the changes (and your regular <Term id="regression-testing">regression</Term> set) for things that broke.

</Steps>

## Common mistakes

| # | Mistake | What happens | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Testing an old build** | Fixed bugs "still fail", new features "are missing" | High (wasted time, wrong reopen) |
| 2 | **"Build: latest" in reports** | Developer can't tell which code you tested | Medium |
| 3 | **Retesting before the fix is in the build** | Bug reopened although the fix was never delivered | Medium |
| 4 | **Staging vs production mix-up** | Test data or server-only issues reported as app bugs | Medium |
| 5 | **Ignoring release notes** | Known issues re-reported as duplicates; changed areas not tested | Low–Medium |
| 6 | **Bug reported on an expired build** | Nobody can install that build to reproduce it | Low |
| 7 | **Only testing fresh installs of betas** | Upgrade bugs from the store version are missed | High |

## Tools and how-to

<Tabs labels={['TestFlight', 'Google Play', 'Firebase']}>
<Tab>

**As a tester**

1. Install **TestFlight** from the App Store.
2. Open the invitation email (or public link) and tap **View in TestFlight**, then **Accept** and **Install**.
3. Open the app from TestFlight or the home screen; beta apps have an orange dot next to their name.
4. Feedback: take a screenshot in the beta app › tap the preview › **Share Beta Feedback**. Or open TestFlight › the app › **Send Beta Feedback**.
5. Older builds: TestFlight › the app › **Previous Builds**.

**The team** uploads builds from Xcode (**Product › Archive › Distribute App**) or a CI pipeline, then adds testers and "What to Test" notes in **App Store Connect › TestFlight**.

</Tab>
<Tab>

**As a tester**

1. Open the opt-in link the team sends you and tap **Become a tester** (use the Google account that's signed in to Play on your phone).
2. Install or update the app from the Play Store. It can take a little while to show up the first time.
3. To leave, open the same link and choose to leave the program, then reinstall the store version.

**The team** uploads an AAB in **Play Console › Testing › Internal testing / Closed testing / Open testing** and manages tester lists there. For quick one-off builds there's also **internal app sharing**, which gives a download link per upload.

</Tab>
<Tab>

**As a tester**

1. Accept the email invitation.
2. Android: install **App Tester**, sign in with the invited Google account, and allow **Install unknown apps** for it when asked. iOS: follow the steps to register your device and add the App Tester web clip.
3. Pick a build in App Tester, read its notes and tap **Download**/**Install**.

**The team** uploads builds in the **Firebase console › App Distribution**, with the Firebase CLI (`firebase appdistribution:distribute`) or Gradle/fastlane plugins, and picks testers or tester groups for each release.

</Tab>
</Tabs>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Beta distribution gets **real builds onto real phones** before a release: **TestFlight** for iOS, **Play testing tracks** and **Firebase App Distribution** for Android.
- TestFlight: **internal** (team, up to 100, no review) vs **external** (up to 10,000, email or public link, Beta App Review). Builds **expire after 90 days**.
- Play tracks: **internal** (fast, small team), **closed** (invited group), **open** (anyone). Firebase sends builds **directly**, via App Tester.
- Dev, staging and production builds look different on purpose. Always know **which environment** you're on.
- Before every report or retest, check **version, build number and environment** in the About screen.
- New build routine: **read notes → install and confirm → smoke test → verify fixes → test new features → regression**.

</KeyTakeaways>
