Daniel is booking a train ticket. Halfway through the passenger form, he switches to his email to copy a booking code. He replies to a message, checks a map, takes a photo. Ten minutes later he goes back to the train app, and it opens on the **home screen**. The form is empty. The seat he picked is gone. He books with a competitor instead.

On the developer's brand-new phone with nothing else running, the app always came back perfectly. On Daniel's three-year-old phone, the OS had **quietly killed it** to free memory. Nobody had tested that.

## Why it matters

People don't use apps in one long session. They jump between apps dozens of times an hour. Every time they leave, your app moves to the **background**, and from that moment the OS decides what happens to it: keep it, freeze it, or kill it.

Background and foreground bugs are some of the most common mobile bugs because:

- **Developers rarely see them.** Fast test phones with lots of memory almost never kill apps.
- **They depend on time.** "Come back after 5 seconds" and "come back after 2 hours" are completely different tests.
- **They touch privacy and security:** what's visible in the app switcher, and whether an expired session is handled.

## The app lifecycle

The <Term id="app-lifecycle" /> is the set of states an app moves through. You don't need to know the code, but you do need to know the states, because each one is a test idea.

| State | Visible? | Running code? | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Not running** | No | No | Never opened since the phone restarted |
| **Foreground, active** | Yes | Yes | You're scrolling the app |
| **Foreground, inactive** | Yes (partly) | Yes, but gets no touches | App switcher open, Control Center pulled down, an incoming call screen on top |
| **Background** | No | Briefly | You just pressed Home |
| **Suspended** | No | No, frozen in memory | A few seconds after leaving |
| **Terminated** | No | No, removed from memory | The OS killed it, or you swiped it away |

<Tabs labels={['iOS', 'Android']}>
<Tab>

- iOS names the states exactly like the table: **Not running → Inactive → Active**, then **Background → Suspended** when you leave.
- An app gets **a few seconds** in the background to save its work (up to about 30 seconds if it asks for extra time). Then it's suspended, unless it uses an approved background mode (audio, navigation, calls, background downloads).
- A suspended app gets **no warning** when it's terminated. That's why it must save state when going to the background.
- If the user **force-quits** an app from the app switcher, iOS throws away its saved screen state, and system background transfers for that app are canceled.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- An Android screen is created, started and resumed (visible and active), then paused and stopped when you leave.
  <DevNote>These are the **activity callbacks**: `onCreate` → `onStart` → `onResume`, then `onPause` → `onStop`.</DevNote>
- A stopped app's process is **cached**: kept in memory but not doing anything. When memory runs low, Android kills cached processes, starting with the least recently used.
- When the process is killed, the screen's **saved instance state** survives. When the user comes back, Android recreates the screen and the app should restore it as if nothing happened.
- **OEM battery savers** on some brands (Xiaomi, Huawei, OnePlus, Samsung "sleeping apps") kill background apps far more aggressively than stock Android.

</Tab>
</Tabs>

### Why does the OS kill apps?

- **Memory:** another app (often the camera or a game) needs RAM, so the OS frees it by killing background apps.
- **Battery:** iOS and Android limit background work to save power. Android's Doze and App Standby, plus manufacturer battery optimizations, can stop apps completely.
- **Updates and restarts:** an app update or a phone restart terminates everything.
- **Crashes in the background:** a <Term id="crash" /> while backgrounded looks to the user like "the app forgot everything".

<Callout type="info" title="What users expect">
Users don't know or care whether the app was suspended or killed. If they didn't swipe it away, they expect to come back to **the same screen, with the same data they typed**. Making that happen after a kill is called **state restoration**, and testing it is your job.
</Callout>

## Background tasks

Some work must continue after the user leaves. Each type has its own rules and its own bugs.

| Task | Example | How it keeps running | What to test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Downloads / uploads** | Offline maps, a podcast, uploading an ID photo | iOS background transfer session, Android WorkManager or a foreground service | Leave during transfer: does it finish? Is progress correct on return? What if the network drops? |
| **Location** | Running tracker, delivery driver app | Background location permission, usually a visible indicator | Lock the phone for 10 minutes: is the route complete, without gaps? |
| **Music and audio** | Spotify, audiobooks | Background audio mode / media notification | Lock screen controls, headphones, calls while playing |
| **Background refresh** | News app showing fresh articles on open | iOS Background App Refresh, Android WorkManager. Runs when the **system** decides | Turn Background App Refresh off: does the app still work and refresh on open? |
| **Push notifications** | "Your order has shipped" | Delivered by Apple / Google, not by the app | Tap them with the app in foreground, background and killed |

<Callout type="warning" title="Background work is never guaranteed">
Low Power Mode, Battery Saver, Doze, OEM battery optimizations and the user's own settings can all delay or stop background work. Test the app's behavior when background work **didn't** happen, not only when it did.
</Callout>

## What to test

### Leaving and coming back

<Steps>

1. **Switch apps** (via the app switcher, and via a notification from another app) and come straight back.
2. **Go Home and return after different gaps:** 5 seconds, 5 minutes, 1 hour, the next morning. Each gap catches different bugs.
3. **Kill the app from the app switcher**, then reopen. Expect a fresh start, but saved data (cart, login, settings) must still be there.
4. **Simulate the OS killing the app** (see the tools section) and reopen. Expect to land on the same screen with typed data restored.
5. **Use the phone heavily in between:** open the camera, a game and a few other apps, then come back. This creates real memory pressure.

</Steps>

### What must survive

- The **current screen** and the navigation back stack.
- **Typed data:** forms, search queries, half-written messages, filters. See <Term id="state-persistence" />.
- **Scroll position** in long lists.
- **Media position** and play/pause state.
- **Work in progress:** uploads, downloads, a payment that was being processed.

### Coming back after a long time

- **Stale data:** prices, stock, balances, chat messages and delivery status should refresh on return (or at least show when they were last updated).
- **Session expiry:** banking and health apps end the <Term id="session" /> after a few minutes in the background. On return, the app should ask you to log in **right away**, not show stale personal data and then fail on the first action.
- **Time-based content:** "Today" labels, countdowns, expired offers and OTP codes are correct after midnight or after hours away.

### Opening from outside the app

A <Term id="push-notification" /> or a <Term id="deep-link" /> must open the **right screen** in all three situations (more in [Push notifications](/features/push-notifications) and [Deep links](/features/deep-links)):

| App state when tapped | Expected result |
| --- | --- |
| Foreground | Navigates to the target (or shows an in-app banner first) |
| Background / suspended | Resumes and navigates to the target |
| Killed / not running | Cold starts **directly** on the target (after login if needed), with a sensible Back |

### Privacy in the app switcher

The app switcher shows a **snapshot** of each app's last screen. Anyone looking at the phone can see it. Banking, health, password and messaging apps should **blur it or cover it** with the app logo. Open the app switcher on every screen that shows personal data and look closely.

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **State lost after OS kill** | Back on the home screen with an empty form or empty cart | High |
| 2 | **Sensitive data in the app switcher** | Balance, card number or messages visible in the snapshot | High (privacy) |
| 3 | **Stale data on return** | Old balance, prices or stock after hours in the background | Medium–High |
| 4 | **Expired session not detected** | Logged-in screen with old data until an action fails with a vague error | High |
| 5 | **Notification opens the wrong screen** | Tapping "Your order shipped" opens the home screen when the app was killed | Medium |
| 6 | **Background upload or download stops** | Progress stuck at 40% forever, no error, no retry | High |
| 7 | **Crash on restore** | App crashes when reopened after being killed in the background | Critical |
| 8 | **Duplicate screens** | Each notification tap stacks another copy of the screen; Back has to be pressed many times | Low–Medium |
| 9 | **Location gaps** | A run or delivery route has straight lines where tracking stopped in the background | High (for that app) |
| 10 | **Media restarts from zero** | A podcast or video starts over after a warm resume | Medium |

## Tools & how-to

<Tabs labels={['Android', 'iOS']}>
<Tab>

**Simulate the OS killing your app (keeps saved state):**

<Steps>

1. Open the app, go to a screen with typed data, press **Home**.
2. Run `adb shell am kill com.example.app`. It only kills the app if it's in the background, just like the system would.
3. Reopen the app from the app switcher (Recents). It should restore the screen and the data.

</Steps>

**"Don't keep activities":** in **Settings › System › Developer options**, turn on **Don't keep activities**. Every screen is destroyed as soon as you leave it, so any screen that doesn't save its state shows the bug immediately. Also try **Background process limit › No background processes**. Turn both off again when you're done, as they make the whole phone behave strangely.

**Other useful commands:**

```bash
adb shell am force-stop com.example.app   # like "Force stop" in Settings (state is NOT kept)
adb shell am start -W -a android.intent.action.VIEW -d "shop://order/4521" com.example.app   # deep link
adb shell dumpsys deviceidle force-idle   # push the device into Doze
adb shell dumpsys deviceidle unforce      # leave Doze
```

Watch what happens with <Term id="logcat" /> in Android Studio while you test: you'll see the process die and restart.

</Tab>
<Tab>

**Simulate the OS killing your app (keeps saved state):** Apple's recommended way is to run the app from **Xcode**, press **Home** in the app so it goes to the background, then click **Stop** in Xcode. Relaunch it: state restoration should bring you back. Don't swipe it away in the app switcher for this test, because a force-quit deletes the saved state on purpose.

**Without Xcode (TestFlight builds):** open many heavy apps (camera, a few games, video) and come back after a while. Older iPhones with less memory kill background apps much sooner.

**Useful Simulator tools:**

- **Debug › Simulate Memory Warning** sends a memory warning to the app (it does not kill it, but badly built apps sometimes crash or drop data).
- **Push notification:** `xcrun simctl push booted com.example.app payload.apns`, or drag an `.apns` file onto the Simulator.
- **Deep link:** `xcrun simctl openurl booted "shop://order/4521"`.
- **Background App Refresh:** turn it off for your app in **Settings › General › Background App Refresh** to test the "nothing happened in the background" path.

</Tab>
</Tabs>

<Callout type="tip" title="Use a long timer, not just a quick switch">
Many bugs only appear after the session timeout (often 5–15 minutes in banking apps) or after midnight. Ask the team for the exact session timeout and test just before and just after it. A <Term id="real-device" /> left on your desk overnight is a great test tool.
</Callout>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Apps move through **not running, active, inactive, background, suspended and terminated**. Each transition is a test idea.
- The OS kills background apps to save **memory and battery**, without warning. Users expect to come back to **the same screen and data** anyway.
- Test **different gaps** (seconds, minutes, hours), **OS kill vs swipe away**, and **"Don't keep activities"** on Android.
- After a long absence: **refresh stale data** and **check the session** before showing personal data.
- Notifications and deep links must work from **foreground, background and killed** states.
- Check the **app switcher snapshot** on every screen with personal data. Related reading: [Interruptions](/mobile/interruptions).

</KeyTakeaways>
