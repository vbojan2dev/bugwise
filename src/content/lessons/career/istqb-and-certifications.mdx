Sooner or later someone will ask you: *"Do you have ISTQB?"* Maybe it's in a job ad, maybe it's a friend who works in IT, maybe it's a course trying to sell you exam prep. Is it a must, a nice extra or a waste of money?

The honest answer: **it depends on where you apply.** This lesson explains what the certificate is, when it helps, how the exam works and how far Bugwise gets you toward it.

## What ISTQB and CTFL are

The **ISTQB** (International Software Testing Qualifications Board) is a non-profit organization that defines a widely used scheme of software testing certifications. It publishes the syllabi and the exam rules; the exams themselves are run through national **member boards** and accredited **exam providers** in many countries.

The entry-level certificate is the **Certified Tester Foundation Level**, usually shortened to **CTFL** or "ISTQB Foundation". It checks that you know the shared vocabulary and core ideas of testing: why we test, how testing fits into development, static testing, test techniques, managing testing and tools.

The current version of the syllabus is **CTFL v4.0**, released in 2023. ISTQB occasionally publishes small updates, so check [istqb.org](https://www.istqb.org) for the version your exam provider uses.

<Callout type="tip" title="The syllabus is free">

You can download the full CTFL syllabus, the official glossary and sample exams for free from istqb.org. Paid courses and books are optional extras, not a requirement.

</Callout>

## The essentials

### Do you need it?

**Often not, but sometimes it helps a lot.**

- **It helps** in some countries and company types, for example consultancies and outsourcing companies, public-sector and regulated projects (banking, insurance, healthcare), and in markets where job ads commonly list it. Some employers there treat it as a basic filter.
- **It matters less** in many product companies and startups, which tend to care more about how you test in a practical task than about certificates.
- **It never replaces** practical skills. A certificate without a portfolio is weaker than a portfolio without a certificate in most interviews.

Look at 20 junior QA job ads in your city. If several mention ISTQB, it's worth considering.

### The exam, in general terms

The Foundation exam is a closed-book, **multiple-choice** exam, typically **40 questions in 60 minutes**, with a pass mark of **65%** (26 of 40). Candidates taking it in a language that isn't their native one can usually get extra time. There are no formal prerequisites, and the Foundation certificate does not expire. Always confirm the current rules with your exam provider.

### Cost

**Prices vary** by country, exam provider and format (online or in a test center), and courses cost extra. Check your national member board's website for current fees. Some employers pay for the exam after you join, so it's fair to ask.

### How Bugwise maps to it

Bugwise covers most of CTFL chapters 1, 2, 4 and 5 in practical form. Chapter 3 (static testing and reviews), the syllabus names for some chapter 5 techniques (estimation methods, testing quadrants) and chapter 6 (tool types) need extra study from the syllabus itself. The Detailed version has the full map.

## Do you need it? A closer look

There's no single answer, because hiring differs a lot between countries and company types.

| Situation | How much CTFL tends to matter |
| --- | --- |
| Job ads in your market often list ISTQB | Worth considering: it can get you past filters |
| Consultancies, outsourcing, public-sector or regulated projects | Often valued, sometimes required for client work |
| Product companies and startups | Usually a nice extra; practical skills decide |
| You have a strong portfolio and practice project | Optional; it adds vocabulary and confidence |
| You have nothing else to show yet | A certificate alone rarely wins; build projects too |

**What it gives you, beyond the line on your CV:**

- A **shared vocabulary**. Interviewers often use ISTQB terms (test basis, test condition, confirmation testing), and knowing them makes conversations smoother.
- A **structured overview** of testing, including topics beginners skip, like reviews and test management.
- **Evidence of commitment** for career changers: you studied and passed an exam on your own.

**What it doesn't give you:** practical skill. The exam tests whether you know and can apply the concepts in multiple-choice questions; it can't show how you'd test a real checkout. That's what your [portfolio](/career/cv-and-portfolio-without-experience) and the [Capstone](/capstone) are for.

<Callout type="warning" title="Be careful with promises">

Be wary of anyone who promises a job after a certification, or claims a certificate is "required everywhere". Neither is true. Some courses are excellent; some mainly sell exam vouchers.

</Callout>

## The exam, in more detail

These are the general rules for CTFL v4.0 at the time of writing. Exam providers publish the binding details, so check before you book.

| | Typical CTFL exam |
| --- | --- |
| Format | Multiple choice, one or more correct answers per question as stated |
| Questions | 40 |
| Time | 60 minutes; usually 25% extra for non-native speakers |
| Pass mark | 65% (26 points of 40) |
| Prerequisites | None |
| Validity | The Foundation certificate does not expire |
| Where | Test centers or online with remote proctoring, depending on provider |

Each learning objective in the syllabus has a **K-level** that tells you how deeply you need to know it:

- **K1 (remember):** recall a term or fact.
- **K2 (understand):** explain, compare, classify, give examples.
- **K3 (apply):** use a technique on a small example, such as deriving boundary values for a field.

Most exam questions are K1 and K2, but the K3 questions (mostly test techniques) are where practice pays off, and that's exactly what Bugwise trains.

### Cost and who pays

Exam fees **vary by country, provider and format**, and change over time, so we don't quote a number here. Check your national member board or the exam provider directly. Training courses are optional and usually cost more than the exam itself. Some employers pay for certification for new testers, so if you're offered a job, it's a reasonable question to ask.

### A realistic study plan

<Steps>

1. **Download the syllabus and glossary** from istqb.org. Skim the whole syllabus once to see its shape.
2. **Go chapter by chapter.** Use the map below: read the Bugwise lessons for practical understanding, then read the syllabus chapter for the exact terms.
3. **Pay extra attention to the "study from the syllabus" areas**, because Bugwise doesn't cover them deeply.
4. **Practice the techniques** (equivalence partitioning, boundary values, decision tables, state transitions, statement and branch coverage) on paper until they're automatic.
5. **Take the official sample exams** under time pressure. Review every wrong answer against the syllabus.
6. **Book the exam** when you pass the sample exams comfortably.

</Steps>

Many people study for a few weeks part-time, but it depends on your background and how much time you have. There's no need to rush.

| Syllabus topic | Where to learn it |
| --- | --- |
| Categories of tool support for testing | **Syllabus.** Real tools in practice: [Jira](/tutorials/working-with-jira), [DevTools](/web/devtools-for-testers), [Postman](/web/api-testing-with-postman), [proxy tools](/mobile/proxy-tools) |

Bugwise shows you how to use real tools; the syllabus asks you to classify tool types and list automation benefits and risks. Learn its lists.

## Beyond Foundation

Once you have CTFL and some experience, ISTQB offers further certificates: **Advanced Level** modules (for example for test analysts and test managers) and **specialist** modules on topics such as mobile application testing, test automation, performance, security and usability. They generally make more sense after a year or two of real work, when you know which direction you want to grow in. See [Your First 90 Days as a Tester](/career/your-first-90-days-as-a-tester) for how specializations develop.

Other organizations offer their own testing certificates too, and recognition varies by country. If a job ad names a specific one, that's the one that matters for that job.
