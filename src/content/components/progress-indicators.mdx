You're exporting a year of bank statements for your tax advisor. The bar shoots to 99% in ten seconds... and stays there. One minute. Five minutes. There's no Cancel, and the sheet covers the whole app. You force-quit, open the app again and start over. Later you learn the export had failed at the very first second: the bar was just a timer.

Progress indicators are tiny, and they're everywhere: behind every login, upload, payment and export. Their job is to answer two questions honestly: **"Is it still working?"** and **"How long will it take?"** When they lie, flash or never stop, users lose trust in everything else. Let's learn how to test them.

## What is it?

A progress indicator shows that the app is **busy with something the user is waiting for**. It comes in two kinds:

- A **spinner** (also called an activity indicator or loader) says: *"I'm working, I don't know how long it takes."*
- A **progress bar** says: *"I'm working, and I'm this far."*

It's one form of the <Term id="loading-state" />. Two close relatives have their own pages: the loading state **inside a button** is covered on [Button](/components/button), and **skeletons** for loading whole screens are on [Empty & error states](/components/empty-and-error-states). This page is about the indicators themselves and their timing.

### Determinate vs indeterminate

This is the most important distinction on the page:

| | Determinate | Indeterminate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Shows** | How much is done (38%, 3 of 8) | Only that something is happening |
| **Looks like** | A bar or ring that fills up | A spinning wheel or a looping bar |
| **Use when** | The app can measure progress: uploads, downloads, exports, installs | The app can't: waiting for a server answer, signing in |
| **Rule** | Must only move forward and match reality | Must end in seconds, not minutes |

An indeterminate indicator can **switch** to determinate once the app knows the size of the work: "Preparing…" with a spinner, then "Uploading 1 of 8" with a bar. The other way round (a bar that turns into an endless spinner at 99%) is a classic bug.

### Linear vs circular

The shape is a layout choice, not a meaning:

- **Linear** (a bar) fits in a row, a card, under a toolbar or at the top of a web page. It has room for a label and "2 min left".
- **Circular** (a spinner or ring) fits in small, square places: a button, a list row, the middle of an empty screen, a download icon.

Both shapes exist in both kinds: a determinate ring (like a download in an app store) and an indeterminate bar (like the thin moving line under a browser's address bar).

### How it differs from similar components

| Component | What it shows | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Progress bar / spinner** | The app is working right now | "Uploading 3 of 8 photos" |
| **Skeleton** | Content is coming, in its future layout | Grey rows on a feed while it loads |
| **Stepper / steps** | Where the **user** is in a multi-step task | "Step 2 of 4: Delivery" |
| **Meter / gauge** | A measured amount, not work in progress | Storage 80% full, password strength |
| **[Toast](/components/toast)** | The result, after the work is done | "Album exported" |

<Callout type="dev" title="Platform names">
On iOS: `UIActivityIndicatorView` (spinner) and `UIProgressView` (bar) in UIKit, and `ProgressView` in SwiftUI, which is a spinner without a value and a bar with one. On Android (Material 3): `LinearProgressIndicator` and `CircularProgressIndicator`, determinate when you pass a progress value; newer Material 3 Expressive versions add a "wavy" style. On the web: the native `<progress>` element, or any element with `role="progressbar"`. Don't confuse `<progress>` with `<meter>`, which is a gauge (disk space, a score), not work in progress.
</Callout>

## When to use it

Show a progress indicator when the user **asked for something and is waiting** for it:

- **Waiting for a server:** signing in, placing an order, searching, loading a screen.
- **Moving data:** uploading photos, downloading a file, syncing, backing up.
- **Long local work:** exporting a video, applying a filter, installing an update.

How long the wait is decides **what** to show. These limits come from decades of usability research (Jakob Nielsen's classic 0.1 s / 1 s / 10 s response times) and are still the best rule of thumb:

| Expected wait | What to show |
| --- | --- |
| **Under ~0.1 s** | Nothing. It feels instant. |
| **~0.1 to 1 s** | Usually nothing, or a subtle change (the button dims). A spinner would only flash. |
| **1 to 10 s** | A spinner or a skeleton, with a short label. |
| **Over ~10 s** | A **determinate** bar, what is happening, ideally time left, and **Cancel**. Let people keep using the app if possible. |

For every indicator, ask three questions: **"When does it appear?"**, **"Is what it shows true?"** and **"How does it end: success, error, timeout and Cancel?"** Then test with a fast network, a slow one and a broken one.

## How good progress indicators behave

### They don't flash

An indicator that appears and disappears within a few frames is worse than none: the screen flickers, layouts jump, and the app feels **slower**. Good apps use two timers together:

<Steps>

1. **Delay before showing (about 300–500 ms).** Start the work, but only show the spinner if it isn't finished yet after the delay. Fast answers never show an indicator at all. The action is still blocked during the delay, so a second tap can't send a second request (see <Term id="double-submit" />).
2. **Minimum display time (about 500 ms or more).** Once the spinner is visible, keep it for at least this long, even if the work finishes right after. People see a calm "it was loading, now it's done" instead of a flicker.

</Steps>

The exact numbers vary by team and design system. What you test is the **behavior**: no flashes on a fast network, an indicator within about a second on a slow one.

### Long work shows real progress, and can be canceled

For anything that can take more than about ten seconds:

- Use a **determinate** bar if the work can be measured, with a label ("Uploading 3 of 8 photos") and, ideally, time left.
- Offer **Cancel**, and make it real: it stops the upload or export on the device **and** the server, leaves no half-finished files, and says what happened ("Export canceled").
- Don't block the whole app if you don't have to. Let the export run while the user keeps browsing, and tell them when it's done.
- Check what happens if the user leaves: the **back** gesture, switching apps, locking the phone. Does the work continue, pause, or silently die? See [Background & foreground](/mobile/background-and-foreground).

### Progress tells the truth

A determinate indicator makes a promise. Test that it keeps it:

- It **only moves forward**. A percentage that jumps from 46% to 31% usually means the app divided "done so far" by a total that changed.
- It **reaches 100%** when the work is done, and the work is really done then (the file is in the folder, the order exists).
- It **doesn't race to 99% and wait**. Many apps fake the bar on a timer and only finish when the server answers. If that answer is an error, the bar sits at 99% forever.
- The **label matches the bar**: "7 of 12" next to 30% is a contradiction.
- **Time left** is roughly right and goes down, not "about 2 min left" for five minutes.

### It always ends, including on errors

Every indicator needs an exit for each outcome:

| Outcome | What the user should see |
| --- | --- |
| **Success** | The indicator goes away, the result appears, and it's announced |
| **Error** | The indicator **stops**, and an <Term id="error-message" /> explains what happened, with Retry |
| **No answer** | After a <Term id="timeout" />, the same as an error: "This is taking too long. Try again." |
| **Canceled** | The indicator goes away immediately, and the app says what was (not) done |

For long work, a good timeout watches for **no progress** rather than total time: a 30-second export that keeps moving is fine, an upload stuck at 35% for 8 seconds is not. Many apps first say "This is taking longer than usual…" and then give up with an error. The pages on [Empty & error states](/components/empty-and-error-states) and [Network conditions](/mobile/network-conditions) cover the error side in depth.

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Endless spinner** | On an error or no answer, it never stops; no message, no Retry | High |
| 2 | **Stuck at 99%** | A faked bar waits for the server; on failure it waits forever | High |
| 3 | **Flashing indicator** | A spinner or overlay blinks for a few frames on every fast action | Low–Medium |
| 4 | **Progress goes backwards** | 46% then 31%, or "time left" goes up | Medium |
| 5 | **No Cancel on long work** | A 2-minute export or upload with no way out but force-quitting | Medium–High |
| 6 | **Cancel doesn't cancel** | The bar disappears, but the upload continues or the order is placed anyway | High |
| 7 | **Spinner for minutes** | Long, measurable work with no progress, so users quit and retry | Medium |
| 8 | **Indicator blocks everything** | A full-screen overlay for a small background task | Medium |
| 9 | **Silent for screen readers** | Spinner has no label; "Done" or "Failed" is never announced | High (for affected users) |
| 10 | **Layout jump** | The page shifts when the indicator appears or disappears, so taps land on the wrong thing | Low–Medium |

<Callout type="bug" title="Why #2 is so common">
Real progress is hard to measure when the slow part happens on the server. So developers animate the bar on a timer and stop just short of 100% until the response arrives. That's fine **only** if errors and timeouts are handled. To catch it: start the long action, then make the final request fail with a <Term id="proxy-tool" /> (or airplane mode) and see whether the bar ever ends.
</Callout>

### Tools

- **Slow it down:** <Term id="network-throttling" /> (Network Link Conditioner on iOS, the emulator's network settings on Android, the "Fast 4G", "Slow 4G" and "3G" presets in Chrome DevTools) shows you the indicator at all. Fast office Wi-Fi hides most of these bugs.
- **Break it on purpose:** a [proxy tool](/mobile/proxy-tools) like Charles or Proxyman can return a `500`, add a long delay, or hold a request with a <Term id="proxy-breakpoint" /> so it never answers. That's how you find endless spinners and the 99% bug.
- **Catch flashes:** record the screen and step through frame by frame (QuickTime, the iOS screen recorder, `adb shell screenrecord`). A 60 ms flash is three or four frames.
- **Check it's real:** after "100%", check the result exists (the file in Files, the order in the history). After Cancel, check the proxy log: did requests really stop?
- **Web:** DevTools › Network shows request timing; the Performance panel's screenshots show flashes and layout shifts.

## Accessibility

- **Give it a name.** A spinner alone means nothing to a <Term id="screen-reader" />. Label it with what's happening: "Loading statements", "Uploading 3 of 8 photos".
  <DevNote>On the web, the native `<progress>` has the progressbar role built in; a custom bar needs `role="progressbar"`, a label (`aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`), `aria-valuemin`, `aria-valuemax` and `aria-valuenow`. Add `aria-valuetext` when a number isn't the clearest wording ("5 of 8 photos"). For an indeterminate bar, leave out `aria-valuenow`. `aria-busy="true"` on a list or region that's being updated tells assistive technology to wait until it's done.</DevNote>
- **Announce the result.** Screen readers usually don't read every change of a progress bar unless it has focus, and reading every percent would be unbearable anyway. Announce the **start**, maybe a few **milestones** for long work, and always the **end**: "Export complete", "Upload failed". This is <Term id="wcag" /> 4.1.3 Status Messages (AA).
  <DevNote>On the web use a live region (`role="status"`), on iOS an accessibility announcement, on Android an announcement or a live region.</DevNote>
- **Don't steal focus** to announce progress. Focus should stay where the user is.
- **<Term id="reduced-motion" />.** A small loading spinner is usually considered essential motion and may keep turning, but large or decorative motion (bouncing loaders, shimmering skeletons, confetti at 100%) should calm down. Watch out for the opposite bug: some sites switch off *all* animation with Reduce Motion, which freezes a CSS spinner. That's only OK if a text label still says "Loading…".
- **Contrast.** The bar and its track are UI parts: at least 3:1 against the background (WCAG 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast, AA). A pale grey bar on white fails. Any percentage text needs 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum), AA).
- **Cancel must be reachable** by keyboard, Switch Control and screen readers, not just by tapping a tiny "x". At least 44×44 pt on iOS and 48×48 dp on Android. On the web, WCAG 2.5.8 (AA) asks for at least 24×24 CSS px; 44×44 px (2.5.5, AAA) is the comfortable target.

## Mobile vs web

<Tabs labels={['iOS', 'Android', 'Web']}>
<Tab>

<DevNote>`UIActivityIndicatorView` (medium and large styles) hides itself when stopped by default; `UIProgressView` is the determinate bar. In SwiftUI, `ProgressView()` is a spinner and `ProgressView(value:total:)` a bar.</DevNote>

- There's no built-in determinate **ring** in UIKit; rings like a download button are custom-drawn, so test them carefully (start, 100%, error, Cancel).
- Long uploads and downloads should survive the app going to the background. Test by switching apps and locking the phone mid-transfer.
  <DevNote>That's a background `URLSession`.</DevNote>
- Test with VoiceOver: is the indicator labeled, and is the result announced?

</Tab>
<Tab>

- Material 3 linear and circular progress indicators: indeterminate by default, determinate when given a progress value. The linear determinate version has a gap and a small **stop indicator** dot at the end.
  <DevNote>`LinearProgressIndicator` and `CircularProgressIndicator`.</DevNote>
- Long work is often a **foreground service** with a progress **notification** (for example a download). Check the notification updates, offers Cancel, and ends with success or failure, not a frozen bar.
- Rotation and process death can restart the work or lose the indicator. Rotate mid-upload; try Developer options › *Don't keep activities*.
- Old apps may still use the legacy `ProgressBar` widget; check it in dark mode.

</Tab>

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Determinate** shows how far (bar or ring); **indeterminate** only says "working" (spinner or looping bar). Linear vs circular is just layout.
- Match the indicator to the wait: nothing under ~1 s, a spinner for a few seconds, a **determinate bar with Cancel** for anything over ~10 s.
- Don't flash: a **delay before showing** plus a **minimum display time**.
- Progress must tell the truth: **only forward**, 100% means done, no faking to 99%.
- Every indicator **ends**: on success, error, timeout and Cancel. Test with slow and broken networks.
- Label it, **announce the result** (WCAG 4.1.3), keep 3:1 contrast, and don't rely on motion alone.
