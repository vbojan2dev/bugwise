Open your orders in a shopping app: **Active · Delivered · Returns**. Tap *Delivered*, scroll down to last month's parcel, peek at *Returns*, come back... and you're at the top of the list again. Annoying? It's also a bug, and one of the most common ones in apps with tabs.

Tabs look like nothing more than three words and a line. But they decide **what the user sees**, they must **remember where the user was**, and they have to agree with swipes, links, badges, the Back button and the keyboard. Let's learn them properly.

## What are tabs?

Tabs are a **row of labels where each label shows a different section of the same screen**. Exactly one tab is selected at a time, and the selected tab is marked by an **active indicator** (usually a line under it). The sections are siblings: equally important, at the same level, and the user can jump between them in any order.

You'll usually see tabs used to:

- **Split a screen into sections**: *Overview / Specs / Reviews* on a product page.
- **Group the same kind of items by status**: *Active / Delivered / Returns* in orders, *Upcoming / Past* in a travel app.
- **Browse categories**: *Popular / Pizza / Sushi / Burgers…* on a restaurant menu (often scrollable).
- **Switch between the main areas of an app**: the **tab bar** at the bottom of an iPhone app (*Home, Search, Orders, Account*).

### How it differs from similar components

Tabs, segmented controls and bottom navigation all show "pick one of a few". The difference is **what changes when you pick**. Using the wrong one is a valid design finding.

| Component | Where | What it switches | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Tabs (top)** | Under the app bar | Sibling *sections* of one screen | Overview / Specs / Reviews |
| **<Term id="segmented-control">Segmented control</Term>** | Inside the content | *Views* or filters of the same data | List / Map, Day / Week / Month |
| **Bottom navigation / iOS tab bar** | Bottom of the screen | The app's *main areas*, each with its own history | Home / Search / Orders / Account |
| **Stepper / wizard** | Top or inline | Steps that must happen *in order* | Address → Delivery → Payment |

<Callout type="info" title="iOS vs Android: different habits">
Android (Material Design) uses **top tabs** under the app bar for sections of a screen. iOS has no real equivalent: Apple's guidelines use a **tab bar at the bottom** for the app's main areas, and a [segmented control](/components/segmented-control) where Android would put top tabs (Calendar's *Day · Week · Month*, App Store's *Free · Paid*). Cross-platform apps that copy Android tabs to iPhone, or the other way round, often feel "off". Mention it, especially when it confuses users.
</Callout>

### Fixed or scrollable?

- **Fixed tabs** share the width equally. They work for **2–4 short labels** and let users see all options at once. The risk: long labels and translations get **cut off**.
- **Scrollable tabs** keep each tab at its natural width and let the row scroll sideways. They suit **many** or **long** labels. The risks: users don't notice there's more, and the selected tab can end up **hidden off-screen**.

## When to use it

Tabs are a good choice when:

- The content splits into **2–6 sibling sections** that the user may visit in any order.
- Each section is **substantial** (a list, a page of details), not just a different filter of the same list.
- Labels are **short and clear** (one or two words) and still fit when translated.
- Users switch between the sections **often**, and want to see at a glance what exists.

## How good tabs behave

Most tab bugs aren't about colors. They're about **staying in sync** and **remembering things**. These are the rules to test against.

### The indicator always tells the truth

Whatever changes the content (a tap, a **swipe**, a **<Term id="deep-link">deep link</Term>**, a push notification, the Back button, a rotation), the selected tab, its indicator and the content below must **agree**. On Android, top tabs usually let you **swipe the content** sideways to reach the next tab; the indicator should follow your finger and land on the new tab. A swipe that changes the page but leaves the indicator behind is a classic bug.

### Each tab keeps its own state

Scroll *Delivered* halfway down, switch to *Returns*, come back: you should be exactly where you were. The same goes for filters, expanded rows, text typed in a search field and the screen you opened *inside* a tab. This is <Term id="state-persistence" />. Test it when switching tabs, after opening a detail screen and coming back, after **rotating** the device, and after the app returns from the background.

<Callout type="info" title="Bottom tab bars have their own history">
In an iPhone tab bar (and Android bottom navigation), each tab keeps its **own navigation stack**. Open an order in *Orders*, switch to *Home*, come back: the order should still be open. Tapping the **already selected** tab goes back to that tab's first screen or scrolls it to the top, which is a long-standing iOS convention.
</Callout>

### Badges stay current

A badge (*Orders* **2**) is a promise: "there are two things waiting here". When the number changes (an order is delivered, a message is read) the badge must update **immediately**, and disappear at zero. Test it while the app is open, after a <Term id="push-notification" />, and after coming back from the background.

### Deep links open the right tab

Emails, push notifications and web links often point to a specific tab: `shop://orders/returns`, or `shop.example.com/product/42?tab=reviews` on the web. The link must open **that** tab, with the indicator on it, both when the app is closed and when it's already open on another tab. On the web, the selected tab often lives in the URL (a <Term id="query-parameter" /> or `#reviews`), so **refreshing** the page or **sharing** the link keeps the tab.

### Back behaves the same way every time

Teams decide this, and the decision must be **consistent**:

- On Android, switching **top tabs** usually doesn't create history: Back leaves the screen rather than visiting the previous tab.
- With **bottom navigation**, Back usually returns to the start tab first, then exits the app.
- Back from a **detail screen** opened inside a tab returns to **that tab**, exactly where the user was, whether they used the app's back arrow, the system Back button or the Back gesture.
- On the web, decide whether each tab click adds a browser history entry. Either way, browser Back must never land on a broken or empty tab.

### Labels fit in every language

German and Finnish labels are often 30–50% longer than English. Fixed tabs then <Term id="truncation">truncate</Term> or clip labels. Good apps switch to **scrollable tabs**, shorten the copy, or wrap to two lines. Check with every supported language, a narrow phone, and the largest text size (<Term id="dynamic-type" /> on iOS, Font size on Android).

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Indicator out of sync** | After a swipe or deep link, the line is on *Active* but the content is *Delivered* | High |
| 2 | **Scroll or state reset** | Every tab switch jumps back to the top; filters and typed text are lost | Medium |
| 3 | **Deep link opens the wrong tab** | The "Track your return" email opens *Active* instead of *Returns* | Medium–High |
| 4 | **Truncated labels** | "Rücksendun" or "Techn…" in other languages or with large text | Medium |
| 5 | **Stale badge** | *Orders* **2** after both orders were delivered, or a badge that never clears | Medium |
| 6 | **Inconsistent Back** | The arrow returns to the right tab, System Back to the first one | Medium |
| 7 | **No keyboard support (web)** | Arrow keys do nothing, every tab is a Tab stop, focus is invisible | Medium–High |
| 8 | **Screen reader silence** | VoiceOver / TalkBack reads "button" with no "tab", "selected" or "2 of 3" | High (for affected users) |
| 9 | **Double loading** | Tapping the selected tab, or swiping back and forth, refetches everything | Low–Medium |
| 10 | **Selected tab off-screen** | In scrollable tabs, the selected tab is hidden past the edge | Low–Medium |

<Callout type="bug" title="Why #2 happens">
To save memory, developers sometimes **destroy and rebuild** a tab's content every time it's hidden. The list is created fresh, so it starts at the top. The fix is to keep each tab alive or save and restore its scroll position and state. To catch it, always **scroll down before switching**, then come back.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info" title="Automatic or manual activation?">
With **automatic** activation, the arrow keys move focus *and* show the tab's panel. That's best when panels appear instantly. With **manual** activation, arrows only move focus, and <kbd>Enter</kbd> or <kbd>Space</kbd> opens the tab. That's better when each tab loads data from the server, so keyboard users don't trigger a request on every arrow press. Both are correct; mixing them up within one product isn't.
</Callout>

### Tools

- **Deep links on Android:** `adb shell am start -a android.intent.action.VIEW -d "shop://orders/returns"` (see [ADB basics](/mobile/adb-basics)).
- **Deep links on the iOS Simulator:** `xcrun simctl openurl booted "shop://orders/returns"`.
- **State after process death (Android):** turn on *Developer options › Don't keep activities*, switch tabs, leave the app and come back.
- **Web:** check roles and `aria-selected` in the Elements and Accessibility panes of [DevTools](/web/devtools-for-testers), and test the URL: refresh, Back and a shared link should all keep the tab.

## Accessibility

- **Roles and state.** Each tab must be announced as a *tab*, with its label, its **selected** state and its **position** ("Reviews, tab, 2 of 3"). Native iOS and Android tabs do this; custom ones often read "button".
  <DevNote>On the web, use `role="tablist"`, `role="tab"` with `aria-selected` and `aria-controls`, and `role="tabpanel"` with `aria-labelledby` (this is the <Term id="aria" /> tabs pattern).</DevNote>
- **Keyboard (web).** <Term id="keyboard-navigation" /> works like this: <kbd>Tab</kbd> enters the row on the **selected** tab, <kbd>←</kbd> <kbd>→</kbd> move between tabs (wrapping at the ends), <kbd>Home</kbd> / <kbd>End</kbd> jump to the first / last, and the next <kbd>Tab</kbd> goes into the panel. Every tab being a separate Tab stop is a bug.
- **Badges need words.** A red "2" means nothing to a <Term id="screen-reader" />. The tab should be read as "Orders, 2 new" (or similar).
- **Don't rely on color alone.** The selected tab needs an indicator line or weight change, not just a slightly different text color. The indicator needs 3:1 contrast with its background, labels 4.5:1.
- **<Term id="touch-target">Touch targets</Term>:** at least 44×44 pt on iOS and 48×48 dp on Android, including small icon-only tabs. On the web, WCAG 2.5.8 (AA) asks for at least 24×24 CSS px; 44×44 px (2.5.5, AAA) is the comfortable target.
- **Swiping is optional.** Every tab must also be reachable by a tap or a key, because some users can't swipe.

</Callout>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Tabs switch between **sibling sections** of one screen. Use something else for steps, actions, or filters of one list.
- iOS puts main areas in a **bottom tab bar** and uses **segmented controls** where Android uses top tabs.
- The indicator must **always match the content**: after taps, swipes, deep links, Back and rotation.
- Each tab **keeps its own state**: scroll position, filters, typed text and opened screens.
- Always test **swipe**, **deep links**, **badges**, **Back**, **long translations**, the **keyboard** (web) and the **screen reader**.

</KeyTakeaways>
