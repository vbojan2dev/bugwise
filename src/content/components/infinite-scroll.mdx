The other 68 are gone, and so is your place. You try to find the Returns policy in the footer instead, but every time you get close, twelve more products push it away. You close the tab.

<Term id="infinite-scroll" /> feels effortless when it works. Underneath, it's a little machine: something **watches** the end of the list, **requests** the next page, **appends** it, and has to handle slow networks, errors, the end of the data, the Back button and people who don't use a mouse. Every part of that machine has its own bugs.

## What is infinite scroll?

Infinite scroll **loads the next batch of items automatically when the user nears the bottom** of a list. There's no "Next" button: scrolling *is* the action. You know it from social feeds, photo streams, news apps, shop categories and search results in many apps.

It's still pagination underneath. The app asks the server for a **page** of items (say 20), shows them, and asks for the next page when it's needed. The difference is only **who decides when**: the scroll position, not a tap.

### How it works

<Steps>

1. **A sentinel marks the end.** After the last loaded item sits an invisible element, the sentinel.
   <DevNote>On the web, an `IntersectionObserver` watches it; on iOS and Android, the list reports when its last rows are about to appear (`prefetchDataSource` or `willDisplay` in UIKit, `.onAppear` on the last row in SwiftUI, the Paging library on Android).</DevNote>
2. **The prefetch distance decides how early.** The observer can fire *before* the sentinel is visible, e.g. one screen ahead. Too small, and people stare at a spinner at the bottom. Too large, and the app loads pages nobody will see.
   <DevNote>On the web: `rootMargin: "0px 0px 800px 0px"`.</DevNote>
3. **One request asks for the next page.** Feeds usually use <Term id="cursor-pagination" />: "20 items **after** item r40" (`?limit=20&after=r40`), instead of "skip 40, take 20". The response contains the items and the cursor for the next page, or `null` when there's nothing more.
4. **The app appends and waits.** New items are added at the bottom, the sentinel moves down with them, and nothing happens until it comes close again. While a request is running, the app must **not** start another one.
5. **The list ends, or fails, clearly.** When the next cursor is `null`, the app stops loading and says so. When a request fails, it shows an error row with Retry, right where the new items would have appeared.

</Steps>

The page size matters too: **10–30 items** is typical. Tiny pages mean many requests and frequent waits; huge pages mean slow responses and lots of memory on cheap phones.

### How it differs from similar patterns

| Pattern | What triggers loading | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Infinite scroll** | Reaching the end of the list | Feeds, photo streams, browsing |
| **"Load more" button** | A tap or click | Shop lists, search results, comments |
| **Numbered [pagination](/components/pagination)** | Choosing a page | Shops, tables, results people come back to |
| **[Pull to refresh](/components/pull-to-refresh)** | Pulling down at the top | Loading **newer** items at the top |
| **<Term id="lazy-loading" /> (images)** | An image nearing the viewport | Saving data on long pages; the items already exist |
| **Virtualization** | Not loading at all: rendering only visible rows | Keeping long lists fast in memory |

## Designs & variants

- **Endless feed (list):** one column of posts or articles, newest first. Social apps, news.
- **Product or photo grid:** two to four columns. Each page fills whole rows, or the last row looks broken.
- **Reverse list (chat):** older messages load at the **top** when you scroll up, and the list must keep your place while they're inserted above you.
- **Hybrid "load more":** the first two or three pages load on scroll, then a **Load more** button appears. It keeps the footer reachable and gives keyboard users a stop.
- **With helpers:** a "Back to top" button after a long scroll, a "New posts" pill when fresh items wait above, or a result count ("312 results").

## When to use it

Infinite scroll fits when people **browse without a specific goal**:

- **Feeds and streams:** social posts, news, photos, short videos, activity.
- **Discovery lists:** "Inspiration", "For you", a category people scroll through for fun.
- **Mobile lists** where tapping a tiny "Next" would be awkward, and nothing important lives below the list.

## How good infinite scroll behaves

### One request per page, no duplicates or gaps

Only **one** request may run at a time. The classic bug: the trigger fires, the user keeps scrolling while the page loads, the trigger fires again (a throttle or debounce doesn't help if the request is slower than it), and the **same cursor** is requested twice. The page then shows the same items twice. Good apps keep an "already loading" flag and also merge results **by item ID**.

Data also changes while people scroll: new posts arrive, items sell out. With offset paging ("skip 40") that causes duplicates and skipped items; cursor paging avoids most of it. The [Pagination](/components/pagination#data-that-changes-between-pages) page has a live demo of both. Test it by adding or deleting items (with a second device or an admin tool) while you're halfway down the list.

### A small loading row, not a full-screen spinner

While the next page loads, a short row with a spinner at the bottom ("Loading more…") is enough. The items already on screen stay usable. With a good prefetch distance, people on a normal connection rarely see it at all. See [Progress & spinners](/components/progress-indicators) for indicator timing.

### Errors stay inline, with Retry

If a page fails, the loaded items **stay**, and an error row appears at the bottom: "Couldn't load more. Retry". Two common bugs are opposites of each other:

- **Silent dead end:** the spinner disappears, nothing more ever loads, and nothing says why. Users think they've reached the end.
- **Retry storm:** the app retries automatically on every scroll event and floods the server (and the battery). Automatic retries need limits and a growing delay (<Term id="retry-backoff" />).

### The end is an end

When the server says there are no more items, the spinner **stops** and a message says so: "You've seen all 45 recipes" or "You're all caught up". Without it, users keep scrolling and waiting. Test the end with a **short list** too: a filter or a category with only 7 items is the fastest way to reach it.

### Back returns to the same place

After opening an item and going **Back**, the list must look **exactly** as before: the same loaded items, the same scroll position, the same filters. On iOS and Android this usually works when the list stays in memory, and breaks after the OS kills the app in the background (<Term id="state-restoration" />), on rotation or when the app reloads the list "to be safe". On the web, the browser restores the position for normal page loads, but a single-page app that rebuilds the list from page 1 has to store the loaded pages and position itself.

### Deep links and refresh

Infinite lists are hard to link to. Good web apps write the position into the URL (`?page=4` or `?after=r80`) or an anchor for the item, so **Refresh**, **Back** and **shared links** land near the same spot instead of the top. In apps, a <Term id="deep-link" /> to an item opens the item, and Back goes somewhere sensible, not an empty list.

### The footer stays reachable

If a footer is below the list, people can't reach it: each time they get close, more items load. Fixes: switch to **Load more** after a few pages, put the footer links in a menu, or stop auto-loading at a fixed limit. As a tester, **try to click every footer link** on a page with infinite scroll.

### Thousands of items stay fast

A list that has loaded 2,000 items can get slow, janky and memory-hungry, especially on older phones. Native lists only create views for visible rows. On the web, apps use **virtualization**: only the rows near the viewport exist in the page, the rest are empty space. It's a performance fix with side effects: the browser's **Find in page** can't find rows that aren't rendered, and screen readers may not see the whole list. Test long sessions: scroll for a few minutes, then check speed, memory and that scrolling back up still shows the right items.

<DevNote>Native lists: `UITableView`, `UICollectionView`, SwiftUI `List`, `RecyclerView`, Compose `LazyColumn`.</DevNote>

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Duplicate page** | The same items appear twice after scrolling fast | High |
| 2 | **Lost position** | Back from an item returns to the top with only the first page | High |
| 3 | **Endless spinner at the end** | No "that's all" message; the loading row never goes away | Medium |
| 4 | **Silent dead end** | A failed request stops loading forever, with no message or Retry | High |
| 5 | **Unreachable footer** | Contact, Returns, Imprint run away as you scroll | Medium–High |
| 6 | **Focus jumps** | After new items load, keyboard focus goes back to the top | High (for affected users) |
| 7 | **Never loads** | Short screens or big monitors: the first page doesn't fill the screen, so no scroll happens and nothing more loads | Medium |
| 8 | **Retry storm** | Hundreds of requests per minute after an error | High |
| 9 | **Slow after a while** | Jank, memory warnings or a crash after a few hundred items | Medium–High |
| 10 | **Skipped items** | Items added or removed during scrolling are missing (offset paging) | Medium |

<Callout type="bug" title="Why #7 happens">
Many implementations only check the sentinel when the user **scrolls**. On a large monitor or a tablet in landscape, the first 10 items might not fill the screen, so there's nothing to scroll and the list stops at 10. Always test on a **big screen** and with a **small page size**: the app must keep loading until the screen is full.
</Callout>

### Tools

- **Network log:** DevTools › Network on the web, a [proxy tool](/mobile/proxy-tools) like Charles or Proxyman for apps. Filter by the list's API path and compare the cursor or page parameter of every request. Two identical requests are proof of the duplicate bug.
- **Throttle:** <Term id="network-throttling" /> ("3G" in Chrome DevTools, Network Link Conditioner on iOS, emulator settings on Android) makes loading rows, double requests and race conditions visible.
- **Break a single page:** use a proxy rule or <Term id="proxy-breakpoint" /> to fail only the second or third page request, then check the error row and Retry.
- **Force the end:** filter or search for something with very few results, or use a test account with little data.
- **State restoration:** on Android, Developer options › *Don't keep activities*; on iOS, put the app in the background and stop it from Xcode, then reopen. Rotate mid-scroll.
- **Performance:** scroll for several minutes. Watch memory in Xcode's Debug navigator, Android Studio's Profiler or DevTools' Performance monitor.

## Accessibility

Infinite scroll is one of the hardest patterns to make accessible. Check these:

- **Keyboard focus stays put.** When items are appended, focus must stay on the item the user was on. Re-rendering the whole list or moving focus to the top sends <Term id="keyboard-navigation">keyboard</Term> users back to the start (WCAG 2.4.3 Focus Order, A). See also <Term id="focus-order" />.
- **Offer "Load more".** A visible button lets keyboard, switch and screen reader users decide when to load, and lets them reach what's below the list. Many sites load the first pages automatically, then show the button.
- **Announce new items.** A polite live region ("12 more products loaded") tells <Term id="screen-reader" /> users something changed without moving focus (WCAG 4.1.3 Status Messages, AA). Announce the end and errors too.
- **Give it structure.** A feed or a plain list with a heading and a result count.
  <DevNote>On the web, the ARIA **feed** pattern fits: `role="feed"` on the container, each item an `article` with `aria-posinset` and `aria-setsize` (`-1` if the total is unknown), and `aria-busy="true"` on the feed while it loads. A plain list (`ul`/`li`) with a heading and a result count also works.</DevNote>
- **The footer and skip links.** Keyboard users must reach everything after the list. A "Skip to footer" link or footer links in the header avoid an endless Tab journey.
- **VoiceOver and TalkBack** swipe through items one by one. Check that new items are reached naturally when swiping past the last one, and that the loading row and errors are read out.
- **Virtualized lists** can confuse screen readers ("item 5 of 12" when there are 400). Check positions and counts are announced correctly.
- **Touch targets** for Retry and Load more: at least 44×44 pt on iOS and 48×48 dp on Android. On the web, WCAG 2.5.8 (AA) asks for at least 24×24 CSS px; 44×44 px (2.5.5, AAA) is the comfortable target.

<KeyTakeaways>

- Infinite scroll is **pagination triggered by scrolling**: a sentinel, a prefetch distance, a page size and usually a **cursor**.
- Test the three bottoms: a small **loading row**, an inline **error with Retry**, and a clear **end message**.
- **One request per page**: scroll fast and watch the network log for duplicate cursors.
- **Back must restore** the loaded items and the position; check rotation, process death and refresh too.
- Keep the **footer reachable**, keep **keyboard focus** in place, offer **Load more**, and **announce** new items.
- Long lists need **virtualization** or native recycling; test long sessions for speed and memory.
