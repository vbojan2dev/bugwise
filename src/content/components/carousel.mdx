You open a shopping app and start reading the banner: "Free delivery on orders over..." and it slides away. You tap a dot to bring it back and get a different slide. You try to scroll down past it and the page doesn't move. Meanwhile, the app has quietly downloaded 12 MB of pictures you'll never see.

A carousel is one of the most common components on the web and in apps, and one of the most argued about. For testers it's a goldmine: it moves by itself, reacts to **gestures**, loads **images**, and has to stay usable for keyboard and **screen reader** users.

## What is it?

A carousel shows **one slide (or a few) at a time** from a set, in a horizontal strip. Users move through it by **swiping**, clicking **arrows**, or tapping **dots**. Some carousels move by themselves (**autoplay**).

You'll meet three main kinds:

- **Hero carousels:** big rotating banners at the top of a home page ("Summer sale", "New collection").
- **Product image galleries:** several photos of one product, swiped on a product page.
- **Content rows:** a horizontally scrolling row of cards, like "Popular near you" in a food delivery app or "Continue watching" on a streaming site.

## When to use it

Carousels have a bad reputation, and part of it is earned. Be fair to both sides:

- **The critique:** hero banners get very little engagement. A widely quoted 2013 measurement from the University of Notre Dame website found that only about 1% of homepage visitors clicked a carousel slide, and most of those clicks (about 84%) went to the first slide. Usability studies (for example by the Nielsen Norman Group) also found users often skip banners because they look like ads, and get annoyed when text moves before they finish reading.
- **Where carousels do work:** when the **user is in control** and the slides are **the same kind of thing**: photos of a product, a row of restaurants or films, onboarding screens. Product image galleries are used heavily because people actively want to see more photos.

So carousels are a good choice when:

- The items are **similar and browsable** (photos, cards), and users choose to see more.
- Space is limited and **a peek** of the next item invites swiping.
- The first slide still works on its own, because many users never see the others.

## How good carousels behave

### Autoplay can always be stopped

If slides move by themselves for **more than 5 seconds**, users must be able to **pause, stop or hide** them. That's <Term id="wcag" /> success criterion **2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide** (Level A, the minimum level). In practice a good carousel:

- Has a **visible Pause/Play button**, not a hidden gesture.
- **Pauses on hover** (mouse) and while **keyboard focus is inside** it.
- **Stops for good** once the user takes control (swipes, clicks an arrow or dot).
- Gives each slide enough time to **read it**: at least 5 seconds, more for longer text.
- **Doesn't autoplay** when the user has asked for **reduced motion** (see below).

### Swipe, arrows and dots all work, on every input

- **Swipe** on touch, and **drag** or **arrows** with a mouse. A desktop user without a trackpad can't swipe, so arrows (or visible scroll buttons) are needed.
- A swipe needs a small threshold before it counts (the <Term id="touch-slop" />), so a slightly wobbly tap isn't a swipe.
- **Vertical swipes must scroll the page.** A carousel only owns horizontal gestures. If you can't scroll the page when your finger starts on the carousel, that's a bug (a classic <Term id="nested-scrolling" /> problem).
- **Dots** are tiny on purpose, but their **tap area** must not be. Aim for 44×44 pt (iOS) / 48×48 dp (Android); on the web, WCAG 2.2 asks for at least 24×24 CSS px (2.5.8, level AA). Tiny targets close together cause wrong taps.

### Looping or clear ends

Either works, but be consistent:

- **Looping** carousels go from the last slide straight to the first, *seamlessly*. A visible "rewind" through all slides, a flash or a duplicated slide is a bug.
- **Non-looping** carousels (product galleries) have clear ends: the Previous arrow is hidden or disabled on slide 1, Next on the last slide, and a swipe past the end bounces back.

### The counter and dots always tell the truth

"2 / 5", the active dot and the slide shown must agree after a swipe, an arrow, a dot tap, autoplay, rotation and coming back from another screen. Screen readers should hear the same: "slide, 2 of 5".

### Images load smart

Carousels are heavy: five 2 MB banners is 10 MB, often on a mobile connection.

- **<Term id="lazy-loading">Lazy-load</Term>** slides that aren't visible yet (load the next one just before it's needed).
- But **never lazy-load the first slide**: it's often the biggest thing on the page, and delaying it hurts the Largest Contentful Paint (one of the <Term id="core-web-vitals" />).
- Serve **sized images**: a phone doesn't need a 2400 px desktop banner.
  <DevNote>On the web that's `srcset`.</DevNote>
- Show a **placeholder** with the right size while an image loads, so the page doesn't jump.

### Aspect ratio and cropping

The same image is shown in very different shapes: wide on desktop, square on a phone. Images are usually **cropped to fill** the slide. Text "baked into" a banner image, or a product near the edge, gets cut off. Try it:

<DevNote>CSS `object-fit: cover`.</DevNote>

Things to check: is the important part (product, face, headline) visible at **every breakpoint**? Is the image **sharp** on high-density screens, or a small file stretched up? Do all slides have the **same height**, or does the page jump when slides change?

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Autoplay can't be paused** | Slides keep moving while you read, hover or tab into it | Medium–High (accessibility: fails WCAG 2.2.2) |
| 2 | **Tiny or overlapping dot targets** | Tapping a dot opens a different slide | Medium |
| 3 | **Vertical scroll blocked** | Page won't scroll when the swipe starts on the carousel | High |
| 4 | **Counter or dots out of sync** | "1 / 5" on every slide, wrong dot active | Low–Medium |
| 5 | **Visible loop jump** | Rewinds through all slides, flashes, or shows a slide twice | Low |
| 6 | **All images loaded up front** | Slow first load, many MB of data on mobile | Medium |
| 7 | **Bad crop or blurry image** | Headline cut off on phones, pixelated banner | Medium |
| 8 | **Focus lost in hidden slides** | Tab goes to links on invisible slides | Medium |
| 9 | **Swipe triggers a click** | Dragging a slide opens the promo link | Medium |
| 10 | **Layout jump** | Page content jumps when slides of different heights load | Low–Medium |

<Callout type="info" title="What reduced motion means">
Users who get dizzy or distracted by motion can turn on **Reduce Motion** (iOS: *Settings › Accessibility › Motion*; Android: *Settings › Accessibility › Remove animations*; macOS and Windows have similar settings). Websites see this as the CSS media query `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`. A good carousel then **doesn't autoplay** and replaces sliding animations with an instant change or a gentle fade. You can emulate it in Chrome DevTools: *More tools › Rendering › Emulate CSS media feature prefers-reduced-motion*.
</Callout>

### Tools

- **Real devices:** gestures, scrolling conflicts and tap accuracy only show up with a real finger. See [Gestures](/mobile/gestures).
- **DevTools › Network** (filter *Img*) with <Term id="network-throttling" />: count the requests and MB on first load. **Lighthouse** flags oversized images and a lazy-loaded LCP image. See [Performance basics](/web/performance-basics).
- **DevTools › Rendering:** emulate `prefers-reduced-motion`. **Elements:** inspect the dot buttons to see their real size.
- **Responsive mode** ([Responsive design testing](/web/responsive-design-testing)): check the crop at 360, 768 and 1440 px wide.
- **Screen readers:** VoiceOver (iOS/macOS), TalkBack (Android), NVDA (Windows).

## Accessibility

- **Name and role.** The container is a region announced as a carousel with a label. Each slide is a group announced as "slide" with its position ("2 of 5").
  <DevNote>For example `<section aria-roledescription="carousel" aria-label="Featured offers">`. This follows the WAI-ARIA Authoring Practices carousel pattern (<Term id="aria" />).</DevNote>
- **Pause control first.** The Pause/Play button should come first in the <Term id="focus-order" />, so keyboard and screen reader users can stop the motion before anything else.
- **Don't move focus unexpectedly.** Changing slides (by autoplay or a click) must not move keyboard focus. Links and buttons on **hidden slides** must not be focusable.
  <DevNote>Use `inert` or `tabindex="-1"` plus `aria-hidden`.</DevNote>
- **Announce changes only when the user caused them.** Otherwise screen readers interrupt the user every few seconds.
  <DevNote>Use `aria-live="polite"` on the slides while autoplay is off, and `aria-live="off"` while it runs.</DevNote>
- **Labeled controls.** "Previous slide", "Next slide", "Go to slide 3" (or the slide's name), "Stop automatic slide show".
- **<Term id="alt-text">Alt text</Term>** for every meaningful image, including text that is baked into a banner ("Summer sale: up to 40% off sandals").
- **<Term id="touch-target">Touch targets</Term>:** arrows and dots at least 44×44 pt / 48×48 dp. **Swiping is optional:** every slide must be reachable with a tap or a key.
- **Contrast:** text over images needs 4.5:1 against the *actual* pixels behind it, on every slide. A gradient overlay usually helps.

<KeyTakeaways>

- Carousels work when **users are in control** (product galleries, content rows). Auto-rotating hero banners get little engagement, so don't hide important info on later slides.
- Autoplay needs a **visible Pause button**, must **pause on hover and focus**, stop after interaction, and stay off with **reduced motion** (WCAG 2.2.2).
- A carousel owns only **horizontal** gestures: vertical swipes must still **scroll the page**.
- **Counter, dots and slide** must always match, for eyes and for screen readers ("2 of 5").
- Test **dot tap areas**, **loop behavior** and **ends**, with a real finger.
- Images: **lazy-load** later slides, never the first; serve **sized** images; check the **crop** at every width.

</KeyTakeaways>
