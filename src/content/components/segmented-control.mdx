Open the Calendar app on an iPhone and look at the top: **Day · Week · Month · Year**. Tap one and the view below changes. That small bar of connected buttons is a <Term id="segmented-control">segmented control</Term>, and you'll find it in almost every app: maps, banking, fitness, shopping filters.

It looks simple, and that's exactly why bugs slip through. Let's learn it properly.

## What is a segmented control?

A segmented control is a **horizontal set of 2–5 options shown as one connected control**. The options are <Term id="mutually-exclusive">mutually exclusive</Term>, so selecting one deselects the others. Think of it as radio buttons dressed up as a single button bar.

You'll usually see it used to:

- **Switch between views of the same content**: *List / Map* in a restaurant app, *Day / Week / Month* in a calendar.
- **Filter content**: *All / Unread* in a mail app, *Income / Expenses* in a banking app.
- **Pick a setting with a few options**: *Walk / Transit / Drive* in a maps app.

### How it differs from similar components

Beginners often mix these up. Knowing the differences helps you spot when the **wrong component** was used, and that's a valid finding too.

| Component | Choices | Selection | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Segmented control** | 2–5, short labels | Exactly one | Switch views or filter, right where the content is |
| **[Tabs](/components/tabs)** | 2–7+ | Exactly one | Navigate between *different* sections or screens |
| **[Radio buttons](/components/radio-button)** | ~2–6 | Exactly one | Forms, especially with long labels or descriptions |
| **[Toggle switch](/components/toggle-switch)** | 1 (on/off) | On or off | A setting that takes effect immediately |
| **[Dropdown](/components/dropdown)** | Many | One (or more) | Saving space when there are 5+ options |
| **[Chips](/components/chips)** | Any | One or many | Filters, tags, multiple selection |

<Callout type="dev" title="Platform names">
Apple calls it a **segmented control**. Google's Material Design calls it a **segmented button**. On the web it's often called a **button group** or **toggle group**. In bug reports, use the name your team uses.
</Callout>

## When to use it

A segmented control is a good choice when:

- There are **2 to 5 options**, and all of them fit with **short labels** (1–2 words).
- The options are **related and mutually exclusive**: you can only look at one time range at a time.
- The user will **switch often** and wants to see all options at once.
- The change applies **immediately** to content on the same screen, with no "Save" button.

If a design uses a segmented control where it doesn't fit (8 options, actions, or long text), raise it early as a **usability** finding. It's much cheaper to change a design than finished code.
</Callout>

## Common bugs

These are the bugs testers report most often for segmented controls. Learn the patterns and you'll spot them in seconds.

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **No default selection** | The screen loads with no segment highlighted, or the highlighted one doesn't match the content | Medium |
| 2 | **Content doesn't match selection** | "Day" is selected but the chart shows yearly data, often after fast tapping | High |
| 3 | **State is lost** | Selection jumps back to the first segment after rotating, going back or returning from the background | Medium |
| 4 | **Label truncation** | "Mon…" or clipped text in other languages or with large text | Medium |
| 5 | **Invisible selection** | In dark mode or high contrast, you can't tell which segment is active | Medium–High |
| 6 | **Double requests** | Tapping the selected segment again reloads data; fast taps fire many API calls | Low–Medium |
| 7 | **Tiny touch targets** | Segments smaller than 44 pt (iOS) / 48 dp (Android) are hard to hit and you tap the wrong one | Medium |
| 8 | **Screen reader silence** | VoiceOver or TalkBack doesn't say which segment is selected, or reads "button, button" | High (for affected users) |
| 9 | **Disabled segment still works** | A greyed-out option can still be tapped or selected with the keyboard | Medium |
| 10 | **Animation glitches** | The thumb lands between segments or flickers when you switch quickly | Low |

<Callout type="bug" title="Why #2 happens: race conditions">
When each tap triggers a network request, responses can arrive **out of order**. If the app shows whatever response arrives last, a slow "Year" response can overwrite a fast "Day" response. This is called a <Term id="race-condition">race condition</Term>. To catch it, tap segments quickly, ideally on a slow network.
</Callout>

## Accessibility

A segmented control has to work for people who can't see the screen, use large text, or navigate with a keyboard. Check these:

- **Screen readers** (<Term id="screen-reader">VoiceOver / TalkBack</Term>) must announce each segment's **label**, **position** ("2 of 4") and whether it's **selected**.
  <DevNote>On the web, that means `role="radiogroup"` with `role="radio"` and `aria-checked` on each segment, or `aria-pressed` for toggle buttons.</DevNote>
- **Icon-only segments** need a text label for assistive tech. Otherwise users just hear "button".
  <DevNote>`accessibilityLabel` / `aria-label`.</DevNote>
- **Don't rely on color alone.** The selected segment should differ in more than hue: a thumb, a checkmark or bolder text.
- **Contrast:** text needs at least 4.5:1 contrast with its background, and the selection indicator at least 3:1.
- **<Term id="touch-target">Touch targets</Term>:** at least 44×44 pt on iOS and 48×48 dp on Android. On the web, WCAG 2.5.8 (AA) asks for at least 24×24 CSS px; 44×44 px (2.5.5, AAA) is the comfortable target.
- **Keyboard (web):** <kbd>Tab</kbd> moves focus into the group (onto the selected segment), and arrow keys move between segments. In the radiogroup pattern the arrow keys also select, just like [radio buttons](/components/radio-button); with toggle buttons, each segment is its own Tab stop and <kbd>Space</kbd> or <kbd>Enter</kbd> selects it.

## Mobile vs web

<Tabs labels={['iOS', 'Android', 'Web']}>
<Tab>

<DevNote>Native component: `UISegmentedControl` (UIKit) or `Picker` with `.segmented` style (SwiftUI).</DevNote>

- Always single selection. The selected segment sits on a sliding thumb; its exact look differs between iOS versions and between light and dark mode.
- Supports **Dynamic Type**, so test at the largest accessibility sizes.
- Common in navigation bars and toolbars, and under large titles.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- Material 3 calls it a **segmented button**: an outlined pill with a checkmark on selected items. In the 2025 Material 3 *Expressive* update it is being replaced by the **connected button group**, which works the same way with a new look.
- Can be **single or multi-select**, so read the requirements carefully.
- Material's guidelines suggest 2–5 segments.
- Test with **font scale** and **display size** settings, plus TalkBack.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- Usually custom-built (HTML buttons + CSS) or from a UI library, so quality varies a lot.
- Check **keyboard navigation**, **focus styles** and ARIA roles in DevTools.
- Test **responsive** widths: does it shrink, wrap, or overflow on mobile browsers?
- Test across browsers (Chrome, Safari, Firefox), because CSS differences can break the layout.

</Tab>
</Tabs>

<KeyTakeaways>

- A segmented control offers **2–5 short, mutually exclusive options** that change content **immediately**.
- Use something else for actions, long labels, on/off settings, many options or multi-select (except Material's multi-select variant).
- The classic bugs: **no default selection, content out of sync, lost state, truncation, invisible selection, and double requests**.
- Always test **fast tapping**, **navigation and rotation**, **languages and large text**, **dark mode**, and **screen readers**.

</KeyTakeaways>
