Open any shopping, food delivery or news app and you'll see a wall of little boxes: a photo, a name, a price, a heart. Each box is a **card**. You scroll past hundreds of them a day without thinking about it.

For a tester, every card is a tiny screen of its own. It mixes **data** (names, prices, ratings), **images** that can be missing, **badges** like "Sold out", and **several things you can tap**. That mix is where the bugs live.

## What is a card?

A card is a **container that groups everything about one item** and is usually a doorway to more detail. Tap the card and you land on the product, restaurant or article page.

You'll usually see cards used for:

- **Collections of similar things**: products in a shop, restaurants in a delivery app, hotels in a travel app.
- **Feeds**: news articles, workouts, social posts, recommendations.
- **Summaries**: "Your run today", "This month's spending", an upcoming flight.

Cards almost never appear alone. They sit in **lists, grids and carousels**, so a lot of card testing is really testing how many cards behave together.

### How it differs from similar components

| Component | What it holds | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Card** | Rich, mixed content about one item (media, text, actions) | Products, restaurants, articles, summaries |
| **List row** | One line or two of text, maybe an icon | Settings, contacts, emails |
| **Tile** | Mostly an image or icon with a short label | Photo galleries, app launchers, categories |
| **Banner** | A message across the full width | Promotions, warnings, "You're offline" |
| **<Term id="modal">Modal</Term>** | Content that blocks the screen | Decisions, forms, confirmations |

<Callout type="dev" title="Platform names">
**Material Design** (Android) has an official card component in three styles: elevated, filled and outlined. **iOS** has no system "card" control. Apps build cards from views, often in the style of the App Store's Today tab. On the **web**, it's just a styled `div` or `article`, so every site invents its own. Teams may also say *tile* or *content card*.

### Images: ratio, cropping and missing photos

Images cause more card bugs than anything else. Things to know:

- **Fix the aspect ratio.** Product grids usually use **1:1** or **4:5**, restaurant and article cards **16:9** or **4:3**. If every photo keeps its own shape, the grid jumps around and the layout shifts while images load.
- **Crop, don't squash.** Photos should be cropped to fit, never stretched. A stretched shoe looks like a canoe.
  <DevNote>On the web that's `object-fit: cover`.</DevNote>
- **Plan for missing images.** A photo can be missing, slow or broken. A good card reserves the space and shows a **neutral placeholder**. A bad one shows a broken-image icon, a file name, or collapses to half its height.
- **Text on photos** (like a "Closed" label or a headline) needs a dark overlay so it stays readable on light and busy images.

### Prices, badges and status

Product and restaurant cards repeat data from the detail page, so they can **disagree** with it.

- **Sale price**: the new price is lower and highlighted, the **old price is higher** and struck through, and the "-25%" badge matches the math: (119 − 89) / 119 ≈ 25%.
- **Sold out / Closed**: the badge, the button and the detail page must all agree. "Sold out" with a working **Add to cart** is a classic bug.
- **Currency and format**: "€89.00" on the card but "89,00 €" on the detail page is a <Term id="localization" /> inconsistency.

### Loading: skeletons

While data loads, good apps show **skeleton cards**: grey shapes with the same size and layout as the real cards. The page doesn't jump when content arrives, and users see that something is happening.

## Whole-card tap vs action buttons

This is the most important card behavior to test. Users expect **tapping anywhere on the card** to open the item. But many cards also have their **own buttons**: a heart, Add to cart, a menu. Now the card has **nested tap targets**, and three things can go wrong:

1. **One tap, two actions.** You tap the heart, and the product page opens too, because the tap "bubbles up" from the heart to the card.
2. **Screen reader noise.** If the whole card is one link, a screen reader reads every word in it as one long link. If every part is its own link, users hear the same destination three times.
3. **Invalid markup (web).** A `<button>` inside an `<a>` is not allowed in HTML, and browsers and screen readers handle it unpredictably.

The fix most design systems recommend is **one link per card**: only the title is a real link, but its clickable area is stretched over the whole card. The secondary buttons sit **on top** of it as separate targets. Try all three versions:

## When to use it

Cards are a good choice when:

- Items have **different kinds of content** (photo, title, price, rating) that belong together.
- Users **browse and compare**, like scrolling restaurants or products.
- Each item leads to a **detail page**.
- The layout needs to **reflow**: a grid of cards can show 1 column on a phone and 4 on a desktop.

Also avoid cards when the content is a **table** (comparing many numbers side by side), when there's **only one item**, or when users need to **read long text**. A card is a preview, not the article itself.

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Action also opens the card** | Tapping the heart, Add to cart or ⋮ also navigates to the detail page | Medium–High |
| 2 | **Long text breaks the layout** | Names overlap the price, push the button out, or are cut mid-word without "…" | Medium |
| 3 | **Missing image not handled** | Broken-image icon, file name or alt text visible, card collapses | Medium |
| 4 | **Wrong or inconsistent price** | Old price lower than new, wrong % badge, card and detail page disagree | High |
| 5 | **Status ignored** | "Sold out" or "Closed" card still lets you order | High |
| 6 | **Uneven grid** | Cards in one row have different heights, buttons don't line up | Low |
| 7 | **Layout shift while loading** | Images pop in and push content down, skeleton has a different size | Low–Medium |
| 8 | **Wrong card opens** | Tapping card 3 opens product 4, often after filtering or scrolling | High |
| 9 | **Invisible in dark mode** | Card edge or text disappears; shadow doesn't show on black | Medium |
| 10 | **Screen reader noise** | Every fragment read separately, duplicate links, unlabeled "button" | High (for affected users) |

<Callout type="bug" title="Why #1 happens: event bubbling">
When you tap a button inside a clickable card, the tap is delivered to the button **and then to every parent**, including the card. Unless the developer stops it (or uses the one-link-per-card pattern), both handlers run. It often slips through because the favorite still gets saved, so a quick "does the heart work?" check passes.
</Callout>

## Accessibility

- **One link per card.** Make the title the link, and extend its clickable area over the card. Don't wrap the entire card, buttons and all, in one link.
- **Label icon buttons.** The heart needs a name like "Save Trail Rain Jacket to favorites" and a pressed/selected state. Just "button" or "heart" is useless in a list of 40 hearts.
- **Decorative images get empty <Term id="alt-text" />.** If the title already names the product, the photo should be skipped (`alt=""`), not read as "IMG_4021.jpg". If the image adds information (a color, a map), describe it.
- **Prices read as one phrase.** Struck-through text isn't announced as "old price" by most screen readers, so add hidden text like "was €119.00".
- **Group the card.** On iOS and Android, related text can be grouped so the screen reader reads "Trail Rain Jacket, 4.6 stars, €89.00" in one stop instead of six.
- **<Term id="touch-target">Touch targets</Term>:** buttons on cards need at least 44×44 pt (iOS) / 48×48 dp (Android), with enough space between them so a tap on the heart doesn't hit the card.
- **Contrast:** text on photos and badges needs 4.5:1. In <Term id="dark-mode" />, the card edge must still be visible (a lighter surface or a border, since shadows don't show on black).
- **Keyboard (web):** each card should be **one** Tab stop plus its buttons, in a logical <Term id="focus-order" />, with a visible focus ring around the whole card.

## Mobile vs web

<Tabs labels={['iOS', 'Android', 'Web']}>
<Tab>

- No native card component, so behavior varies by app.
  <DevNote>Cards are built with SwiftUI views or `UICollectionView` cells.</DevNote>
- Tapping often shows a **highlight or press animation**; long-press may open a **context menu** with a preview. Test both.
- Test with **Dynamic Type** at the largest sizes: does the card grow, or does text get cut?
- VoiceOver: check that card content is grouped and the image isn't read as a file name.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- Material 3 **Card** component: elevated, filled or outlined. Cards in a grid often reflow by <Term id="window-size-class" /> on tablets and foldables.
- Pressed cards show a **ripple**. A ripple on the whole card when you tap the heart is a hint that both got the tap.
- Test with **font scale** and **display size** changes, and with TalkBack.
- Rotate the device: do columns change correctly, and does the scroll position survive?

</Tab>
<Tab>

- Cards are custom HTML, usually an `article` with a link inside. Inspect the markup in <Term id="devtools" />: look for `<button>` inside `<a>` or several links to the same URL.
- Check **hover** states and that the cursor is a pointer over the whole card.
- Use **responsive mode** ([Responsive design testing](/web/responsive-design-testing)) to check 1, 2, 3 and 4 columns, and that cards never overflow the viewport.
- Watch for **layout shift** while images load (Lighthouse reports it as CLS), and check images are <Term id="lazy-loading">lazy-loaded</Term> below the fold.
- Middle-click / Cmd+click on a card should open it in a new tab, which only works if it's a real link.

</Tab>

<KeyTakeaways>

- A card groups **media, title, supporting text, metadata and actions** about one item, and usually opens a detail page.
- Test **every tap target separately**: the card itself, the heart, the button, the menu. One tap must do **one** thing.
- Use the **one link per card** pattern as your expected behavior for screen readers and keyboards.
- Feed cards **real, ugly data**: long names, missing images, sale prices, sold-out items, and compare them with the detail page.
- Check the **grid as a whole**: equal heights, fixed image ratios, skeletons while loading, dark mode and every screen width.

</KeyTakeaways>
