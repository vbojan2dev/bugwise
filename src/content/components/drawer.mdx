You're on a clothing shop's website on your phone. You tap ☰, pick **Sale**, and... the page behind changes, but the menu is still covering it. You close it by hand. Then you scroll the menu and the page behind it scrolls too. Each of these is small, but together they make the shop feel broken, and some shoppers simply leave.

Three short lines (☰) hide a whole navigation system. A drawer has to open and close in many ways, **move focus** correctly, **lock** the page behind, show **where you are**, and stay out of the way of the system's own gestures. Let's learn how a good one behaves.

## What is a drawer?

A **navigation drawer** is a panel of destinations that **slides in from the side** of the screen (the left in left-to-right languages, the right in Arabic or Hebrew). It's usually opened by the **menu button**, the three-line icon people call the **hamburger** (☰). On the web the same thing is called a *hamburger menu*, *side menu* or *off-canvas menu*.

It holds destinations that don't fit in, or don't deserve, a permanent spot on screen: all the categories of a shop, mail folders and labels, Settings, Help, Legal.

### Modal or standard?

| Type | Where | How it behaves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Modal drawer** | Phones, small windows | Slides **over** the page with a **scrim**. Must be closed before you can use the page again. Like a [modal](/components/modal). |
| **Standard (permanent) drawer** | Tablets, desktops | Sits **next to** the content, no scrim. Often collapsible to a narrow **rail** of icons. |

Many apps switch between the two at a <Term id="breakpoint" />: a modal drawer on a phone, a standard drawer on a tablet in landscape. Test **both sides of the breakpoint**, and what happens when you rotate or resize while the drawer is open.

### How it differs from similar components

| Component | Best for | Visible? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Drawer** | Many or secondary destinations | Hidden until opened (modal) |
| **[Bottom bar](/components/navigation-bar)** | 3–5 main areas, used often | Always on screen |
| **[Tabs](/components/tabs)** | Sections of one screen | Always on screen |
| **[Bottom sheet](/components/bottom-sheet)** | Actions or options for the current task | Opened on demand |

<Callout type="info" title="Platform habits">
**Android:** Material Design has a modal and a standard navigation drawer, and many apps still use them. In its 2025 *Expressive* update, Material 3 marks the navigation drawer as being deprecated and recommends the **expanded navigation rail** instead (aimed mainly at medium and larger windows, so many phone apps still use a modal drawer). **iOS:** Apple's guidelines have no hamburger menu. iPhone apps use a **tab bar**; iPad apps use a **sidebar**, which behaves like a standard drawer. A hamburger in an iPhone app is usually a cross-platform or web-style design. **Web:** the hamburger menu is everywhere on mobile sites.
</Callout>

## When to use it

- There are **many destinations** (more than five), like the categories of a shop or the folders of a mail app.
- The destinations are **secondary** or used **rarely**: Settings, Help, Legal, account switching.
- On **wide screens**, as a standard drawer that's always visible.
- On **mobile websites**, where the desktop's top navigation doesn't fit.

## Good and bad uses

### The hamburger debate

Hiding navigation saves space, but **people use what they can see**. A well-known Nielsen Norman Group study (2016) found that people used hidden navigation much less often than visible navigation, found things more slowly, and rated the tasks as harder. The effect was strongest on desktop, where hiding the menu wasn't even necessary.

There's another side, and you should know it too:

- Today almost everyone **recognizes** ☰ as "menu". The problem is **out of sight, out of mind**, not confusion.
- A drawer is a **reasonable home for secondary destinations**, as long as the main ones are visible.
- On a small phone, a long list of shop categories simply has to go **somewhere**.

The usual middle ground: keep the **3–5 most important destinations visible** (a bottom bar or tabs) and put the rest behind ☰.

## How a good drawer behaves

### Opening

- **The menu button** must have a **name** ("Menu" or "Open navigation menu").
  <DevNote>On the web it's a real `<button>` with `aria-expanded` that switches between `false` and `true`, and `aria-controls` pointing to the menu.</DevNote>
- **Edge swipe.** Some apps let you pull the drawer out from the edge of the screen. That edge is shared with the system:
  - **iOS:** the left edge swipe means **Back**. If a drawer also listens to it, one of the two loses. The safe rule: edge swipe opens the drawer **only on the first screen** of the app, where there's nothing to go back to.
  - **Android** (gesture navigation): a swipe from **either** side edge is **system Back**. Since Android 10, a drawer can still be pulled out by **pressing and holding** at the edge, then dragging. A quick swipe must stay Back.

  See [Gestures › Edge swipes belong to the system](/mobile/gestures#edge-swipes-belong-to-the-system) for the details.
- **Focus moves into the drawer**, usually to the current destination or the first link, so keyboard and <Term id="screen-reader" /> users know it opened.

### Closing

| Exit | Where | What must happen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Choose a destination** | Everywhere | Navigate **and close** the drawer |
| **Tap the scrim** | Modal drawers | Close. The tap must **not** reach the page behind |
| **Swipe the drawer away** | iOS, Android, mobile web | Close |
| **Close button (✕) / ☰ again** | Web, often apps | Close |
| **Esc** | Web, hardware keyboards | Close |
| **System Back** | Android | Close the drawer **first**, not the screen behind |
| **Route change** | Web (single-page apps) | Close, even when the page changed some other way (a link inside the page, browser Back) |

After closing, **focus returns to the menu button**. Otherwise keyboard users are thrown back to the top of the page, or into nothing.

### While it's open

A modal drawer is a dialog, so the rules from [Modal](/components/modal#focus-in-trapped-and-back) apply:

- **Focus is trapped** inside the drawer (<Term id="focus-trap" />). <kbd>Tab</kbd> must not wander through the links hidden behind the <Term id="scrim" />.
- **Scroll lock:** the page behind must not scroll when you scroll the drawer or swipe on the scrim. A long drawer scrolls on its own; when it reaches the end, the page behind stays still. See [Modal › Scroll lock](/components/modal#scroll-lock).
- The screen reader must not read the page behind.
  <DevNote>On the web: `aria-modal="true"` or `inert` on the rest of the page.</DevNote>

### Showing where you are

The **current destination** is highlighted (Material: a pill-shaped indicator) and, on the web, marked with `aria-current="page"`. Test it on **every** page, including pages reached **without** the drawer: from a link in the page, a [deep link](/features/deep-links), a search result or browser Back. A classic web bug: matching the URL with "starts with", so **Home** (`/`) matches every page.

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Nameless menu button** | Screen reader says "button"; `aria-expanded` stays `false` | High (for affected users) |
| 2 | **Stays open after navigating** | The page changes behind the still-open menu | Medium |
| 3 | **Page behind scrolls** | Scrolling the menu or the scrim moves the page | Medium |
| 4 | **Wrong item highlighted** | "Home" is marked on every page, or nothing is | Low–Medium |
| 5 | **Focus escapes** | Tab goes to links behind the scrim; Esc does nothing | High (for affected users) |
| 6 | **Focus lost on close** | After closing, focus jumps to the top of the page | Medium |
| 7 | **Back swipe opens the drawer** | On iOS, swiping back from a detail screen pulls the drawer instead | Medium–High |
| 8 | **Back closes the app** | Android system Back closes the app instead of the drawer | High |
| 9 | **Taps go through the scrim** | Tapping the dimmed area also presses a button behind it | Medium |
| 10 | **Broken at the breakpoint** | Rotating with the drawer open leaves a scrim with no drawer, or two menus | Medium |

<Callout type="bug" title="Why #2 happens">
On a classic website, every link loads a new page, so the menu is "closed" for free. **Single-page apps** (React, Vue, Angular…) only swap the content, so the menu stays exactly as it was unless the developer closes it on every route change. Always pick a destination from the menu, then check whether the menu is still open.
</Callout>

### Tools

- **Web:** in [DevTools](/web/devtools-for-testers), use the device toolbar for phone widths, check `aria-expanded` live in the **Elements** panel, and use the **Accessibility** pane to see each element's name and role. Type `document.activeElement` in the Console to see where focus really is.
- **Screen readers:** VoiceOver on Mac (<kbd>Cmd</kbd>+<kbd>F5</kbd>) and iPhone, TalkBack on Android, NVDA on Windows.
- **Android Emulator:** test system Back in **gesture** and **3-button** navigation (*Settings › System › Gestures › System navigation*), rotate with the toolbar, and switch the language to Arabic to check the right-to-left drawer.
- **iOS Simulator:** drag from the very left edge with the mouse to test the back swipe on every screen that has a drawer.

## Accessibility

- **Button:** a real button with a name ("Menu", "Open navigation menu") and an expanded state that reflects the drawer. Changing the label to "Close menu" while open is fine too.
  <DevNote>`aria-expanded` reflects the state and `aria-controls` points to the menu.</DevNote>
- **Focus:** moves into the drawer on open, stays inside while open, returns to the button on close.
  <DevNote>On the web, the rest of the page gets `inert` or the drawer is a `role="dialog"` with `aria-modal="true"`.</DevNote>
- **Landmark:** the list of links is a navigation landmark with a label, and the current link is announced as the current page.
  <DevNote>A `<nav>` with a label (e.g. `aria-label="Main"`); the current link has `aria-current="page"`.</DevNote>
- **Not gesture-only:** every drawer opened by a swipe also has a visible button, and every drawer closed by a swipe also has ✕, Esc and Back. <Term id="wcag" /> 2.5.7 (*Dragging Movements*) asks that dragging always has a simple tap alternative.
- **<Term id="touch-target">Touch targets</Term>:** ☰ and each item at least 44×44 pt / 48×48 dp. Destination rows in Material drawers are 56 dp tall.
- **Visible focus** on every item, and enough contrast for the current-page indicator (3:1) and labels (4.5:1).

<KeyTakeaways>

- A **modal drawer** slides over the page with a scrim; a **standard drawer** sits next to the content on wide screens.
- Hidden navigation gets **used less**. Keep the main destinations visible and put **secondary or many** destinations in the drawer.
- Closing must work **every way**: choosing a destination, scrim, swipe, ✕, Esc, Android Back, and every route change on the web.
- While open: **focus moves in and is trapped**, the page behind **doesn't scroll**, and on close focus **returns to ☰**.
- The **current page is highlighted** correctly on every page, and the ☰ button has a **name** and a live **aria-expanded**.
- Edge swipes compete with **iOS back swipe** and **Android system Back**. The system gesture must win on deeper screens.

</KeyTakeaways>
