*Open now · Free delivery · Vegan.* The row of little rounded buttons above a restaurant list. The recipients in the **To:** field of an email. The skills on a job profile. The "On my way!" reply under a message. All chips.

They look harmless, but they pack a lot of behavior into a tiny space: selecting, removing, scrolling sideways, counting results. That's where the bugs live.

## What is a chip?

A chip is a **small, compact element that represents a choice, a filter, a piece of entered information or a quick action**. It usually has rounded corners, a short label and sometimes an icon, a count or a remove button (×).

The word means different things to different people, so let's untangle it first:

- **Chip** (Material Design's name): an *interactive* element. You tap it to select, remove or do something.
- **Tag** or **badge**: a *label* that only describes something ("Vegan", "New", "−20%"). You can't tap it.
- **Pill**: a common nickname for the capsule shape. Designers use it for both.
- **Token**: an input chip inside a text field, like an email recipient.

<Callout type="tip" title="First question: control or label?">
Before you test anything, find out: **is it supposed to be tappable?** A tag that looks like a button (users tap it, nothing happens) is a bug. So is a chip that is tappable but looks like a static label.
</Callout>

### The four Material 3 chip types

Material Design 3 defines four chip types. iOS has no native chip, but iPhone apps build the same patterns by hand, and the web uses all of them.

| Type | What it does | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Filter** | Toggles a filter on or off (often multi-select) | Open now · Free delivery |
| **Input** | Shows information the user entered, removable with × | Email recipients, skills |
| **Assist** | Starts an action related to the content | Directions · Call |
| **Suggestion** | Offers a generated suggestion to tap | Smart replies: "On my way!" |

### Filter chips vs segmented control vs checkboxes

All three let users pick options. The difference is **how many** and **where**:

| Component | How many? | Typical place |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Filter chips** | Zero, one or many | Above a list, often in a horizontal scroll row |
| **<Term id="segmented-control">Segmented control</Term>** | Exactly one of 2–5 | Switching views: Day · Week · Month |
| **<Term id="checkbox">Checkboxes</Term>** | Zero, one or many | Forms and filter panels with longer labels |

Filter chips are basically compact checkboxes that apply **immediately**: tap "Vegan" and the list updates. See also [Segmented control](/components/segmented-control) and [Checkbox](/components/checkbox).

### Input chips done right

Input chips (tokens) turn text into removable pieces: email recipients, cuisines you like, skills on a CV, labels on a ticket. They have the most keyboard behavior of any chip, so they have the most bugs. Here's a correct one to compare against:

<TokenField client:visible />

The rules worth testing in every token field:

- **Adding:** Enter (and often comma) turns the text into a chip. Pasting a list ("a@x.com, b@y.com") creates several chips.
- **Duplicates:** "Pizza", "pizza" and "pizza " are the same thing. The app should ignore or merge them, ideally with a message.
- **Removing:** each × removes **exactly its own** chip.
- **Backspace** in the empty field removes (or first highlights) **the last chip only**. Never all of them.
- **Validation:** an invalid entry (a broken email address) is marked clearly, with an icon or text, not only red.

## When to use it

Chips are a good choice when:

- Users want to **narrow down a list quickly** with a few common filters, right above the results.
- Users enter **several short values** into one field: recipients, tags, skills, ingredients.
- The app can offer **smart suggestions or shortcuts** that save typing (search ideas, quick replies).
- You need to **show the active filters** compactly, each removable with ×, e.g. "Price: €20–50 ×".

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Selected state by color only** | In greyscale or with color blindness, you can't tell which filters are on | Medium–High |
| 2 | **Hidden chips in a scroll row** | The row ends cleanly, nothing hints at more chips; some filters are never found | Medium |
| 3 | **Last chip unreachable** | A button or gradient covers the end of the row, even when fully scrolled | High |
| 4 | **× removes the wrong chip** | Tap × on "Burgers", "Sushi" disappears | High |
| 5 | **Backspace clears everything** | One Backspace in the empty field deletes all recipients | High |
| 6 | **Duplicates** | "Pizza" and "pizza " both appear as chips | Low–Medium |
| 7 | **Stale counts** | "12 results" stays after a filter is removed | Medium |
| 8 | **Active filters out of view** | A selected chip is scrolled off-screen; users don't know why the list is short | Medium |
| 9 | **Long labels** | A German or user-entered label overflows the chip or pushes the × off-screen | Low–Medium |
| 10 | **Nothing announced** | Screen reader says only "Vegan" (no "selected") or "button" for the × | High (for affected users) |

<Callout type="bug" title="Why #4 happens">
Developers often remove an item by its **position** (index) in a list. That works until the list is shown in a **different order** than it is stored: sorted A–Z, newest first, or after a chip was moved. Then "remove the 1st chip on screen" deletes the 1st item in memory, which is a different one. Always test removing chips **after** the order has changed, and remove the first, middle and last chip.
</Callout>

## Stress-test it

Overflow is the chip row's biggest design question: **wrap** to several lines, or **scroll sideways**? Scrolling saves space but hides chips. Play with both, and turn on greyscale.

<Playground client:visible />

<Callout type="info" title="Make scrolling discoverable">
A horizontal chip row needs a **hint** that there is more: the next chip should peek in at the edge (cut in half), or a fade shows the content continues. A row that happens to end exactly between two chips looks complete, so test on **several screen widths**, because a row that peeks on one phone can end cleanly on another.
</Callout>

## Accessibility

Chips are small and dense, which makes them easy to get wrong for keyboard and screen reader users.

| Key (web) | What it should do |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Tab</kbd> | Moves to the next chip (or into the group) and to each × |
| <kbd>Space</kbd> / <kbd>Enter</kbd> | Toggles a filter chip or activates an action chip |
| <kbd>Backspace</kbd> (empty token field) | Highlights or removes the last token only |
| <kbd>Backspace</kbd> / <kbd>Delete</kbd> (on a token) | Removes that token |

- **Roles:** a filter chip is a **toggle button** or a checkbox. Screen readers then say "Vegan, toggle button, selected" (wording varies by reader). A single-select chip group should be a radio group. An assist chip is a plain button. A tag is plain text.
  <DevNote>A toggle button is `<button aria-pressed="true">`.</DevNote>
- **Remove buttons:** the × is its own button with a name that includes the chip: "Remove Italian". A bare "×" or "button" is useless when there are five of them.
  <DevNote>`aria-label="Remove Italian"`.</DevNote>
- **Not by color alone:** selected chips need a checkmark, fill or outline change (<Term id="wcag" /> 1.4.1). Chip borders and icons need at least 3:1 <Term id="contrast-ratio">contrast</Term>, labels 4.5:1.
- **Announce changes:** when a chip is added or removed, or the result count changes, tell <Term id="screen-reader">screen reader</Term> users (a polite live region: "Removed Italian. 2 cuisines.").
- **Focus after removing:** when a chip is removed, focus moves somewhere sensible (the previous chip or the text field), not back to the top of the page.
- **<Term id="touch-target">Touch targets</Term>:** chips are often 32 dp/pt tall, and the × even smaller. Their *touch area* should still be 44×44 pt (iOS) / 48×48 dp (Android); Material adds invisible padding for this.
- **Scroll rows:** keyboard focus moving to an off-screen chip must scroll it into view, uncovered.

## Mobile vs web

<Tabs labels={['iOS', 'Android', 'Web']}>
<Tab>

- iOS has **no chip component**. Every chip on iOS is custom (capsule buttons, tokens in a search field), so test VoiceOver labels and traits carefully.
- One native exception: **search tokens** (Mail, Photos), with Backspace behavior built in.
  <DevNote>Neither UIKit nor SwiftUI has a chip; search tokens live in `UISearchTextField`.</DevNote>
- Horizontal chip rows are usually a horizontal scroll view: check the swipe doesn't trigger the **back gesture** near the left edge.
- Test **Dynamic Type**: at the largest sizes, chips grow taller and wider and the row may need to wrap.

</Tab>
<Tab>

<DevNote>Material Components provide `Chip` (styles for assist, filter, input, suggestion) and `ChipGroup` (`singleSelection`, `selectionRequired`). Compose has `AssistChip`, `FilterChip`, `InputChip` and `SuggestionChip`.</DevNote>

- A chip group can be single-line (horizontal scroll) or wrap. Check which one is used on small screens and with large **font scale**.
- Selected filter chips should show the **checkmark**. Teams sometimes turn it off for looks, leaving color as the only cue.
  <DevNote>The setting is `checkedIconVisible`.</DevNote>
- Test with **TalkBack** and **color correction / greyscale** in the Accessibility settings.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- No native element, and libraries vary a lot in quality.
  <DevNote>Chips are `<button>`s (filters, actions), tags are `<span>`s, and token fields are custom widgets around an `<input>`.</DevNote>
- A horizontal row with a hidden scrollbar is hard to scroll with a **mouse**: users need Shift + wheel or a trackpad. Many sites add arrow buttons; test them at both ends.
- Test **paste** into token fields (comma- or newline-separated lists), **autocomplete** suggestions, and what is sent to the server (trimmed? deduplicated?).
- Check the **URL**: filter chips often add <Term id="query-parameter">query parameters</Term> (`?open=1&diet=vegan`). Reload and share the link: are the same chips selected?

<KeyTakeaways>

- **Chips** are interactive (filter, input, assist, suggestion); **tags** are just labels. Each must look like what it is.
- Filter chips are compact multi-select toggles that apply **immediately**. For "exactly one of a few", a segmented control is clearer.
- The selected state needs a **checkmark or shape change**, never color alone. Test in greyscale.
- Horizontal chip rows need a **scroll hint**, and the **last chip** must be reachable on the smallest screen.
- Token fields: each **× removes its own chip**, **Backspace removes only the last**, **duplicates** are ignored, and counts update on **every** change.

</KeyTakeaways>
