Choosing your country at checkout, a T-shirt size in a fashion app, a sort order in a hotel search: whenever an app needs one answer from a known list, there's a good chance it uses a <Term id="dropdown">dropdown</Term>. It saves space by hiding the options until you need them.

That hiding is also its weakness. Testers have to open it, scroll it, search it, use it with a keyboard, and check what happens when another field changes. Let's see how.

## What is a dropdown?

A dropdown (also called a **select** or **picker**) is a **closed field that shows the current value and opens a list of options when you tap it**. You pick one option (or several, in a multi-select), and the list closes again.

You'll usually see dropdowns used for:

- **Long, known lists**: country, state, language, currency.
- **Settings with many values**: sort order, items per page, time zone.
- **Product choices**: size, color, quantity.
- **Dependent choices**: car make → model, country → city.

### How it differs from similar components

| Component | Options visible | Selection | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Dropdown / select** | Hidden until opened | One (or many, in a multi-select) | 5–15 options when space is tight |
| **Combo box** (searchable dropdown) | Hidden; filtered as you type | One | Long lists: countries, cities, airports |
| **Segmented control** | All, in one row | Exactly one | 2–5 short options that switch a view |
| **Radio buttons** | All, stacked | Exactly one | 2–6 options, especially with longer labels |
| **Bottom sheet / picker** | Slides up from the bottom (mobile) | One or many | Long or rich lists on phones, with search or descriptions |
| **Menu** | Hidden until opened | None: you trigger an action | Commands like *Edit, Share, Delete*, not values |

<Callout type="dev" title="Platform names">
On the web it's a `<select>` element or a custom **listbox**/**combobox**. iOS uses a **pop-up button** (a menu with a checkmark) or a **picker** (the scrolling wheel). Material Design on Android calls it an **exposed dropdown menu**, and Android developers often say **Spinner**. A menu that runs *actions* is not a dropdown, even if it looks like one.
</Callout>

## Designs & variants

Native dropdowns are drawn by the browser or the operating system, so they're usually robust. Custom ones look nicer, but every behavior (keyboard, positioning, screen readers) has to be rebuilt by the team, and that's where bugs hide.

<VariantsGallery client:visible />

<Callout type="tip" title="Native vs custom: ask early">
Before testing, find out whether a dropdown is **native** or **custom**. A native `<select>` gets keyboard support, screen reader support and the phone's own picker for free. A custom one needs all of that tested by hand.
</Callout>

## When to use it

A dropdown is a good choice when:

- There are **more than about 5 options** and showing them all would take too much space.
- The options are **familiar** to users (they know their country, they don't need to compare).
- There's a **sensible default**, or the user picks once and rarely changes it.
- For **long lists (15+)**, add search. Then it becomes a combo box.

<Callout type="tip" title="Tester mindset">
A dropdown for two options, a pre-selected default nobody checks, or 200 options without search are all **usability** findings. Report them during design review, when they're cheap to change.
</Callout>

## Common bugs

These are the dropdown bugs testers report most often.

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Dependent field not reset** | You change the country, but the old city stays selected, so the order ships to "Zagreb, Serbia" | High |
| 2 | **Menu clipped or off-screen** | Near the bottom of the screen (or inside a card) the list is cut off and the last options can't be reached | High |
| 3 | **No keyboard support** | Tab skips the dropdown, or arrows, Enter and Esc do nothing | High (for affected users) |
| 4 | **Strict search** | "ceske" doesn't find "České Budějovice"; "zagreb" doesn't find "Zagreb" | Medium |
| 5 | **Unsorted list or duplicates** | Cities appear in random order, "Split" is listed twice | Low–Medium |
| 6 | **Doesn't close** | Clicking outside or scrolling the page leaves the menu floating on top of the form | Medium |
| 7 | **Selected value not shown** | After choosing, the field still says "Select…" (but the value was saved) | Medium |
| 8 | **Wrong default** | The first option is pre-selected, or the saved value isn't shown when you come back to edit | High |
| 9 | **Placeholder can be submitted** | "Select a country" is accepted as if it were a real value | Medium |
| 10 | **Long labels truncated** | "Tomorrow, 16:00–2…" or long translations are cut off in the field or menu | Medium |
| 11 | **Highlighted ≠ selected** | Moving the mouse over an option changes the value without clicking | Medium |
| 12 | **Screen reader silence** | VoiceOver or TalkBack says "button" or "clickable" with no label and no value | High (for affected users) |

<Callout type="bug" title="Why #1 is so sneaky">
Each dropdown works perfectly on its own, so the bug only appears when you change fields **in an unusual order**: pick country, pick city, then go back and change the country. Always test dependent fields **backwards**, too. The bad data reaches the warehouse, not the screen, which is why it often slips into <Term id="production" />.
</Callout>

## Accessibility

Custom dropdowns are one of the most common accessibility failures on the web. Check these:

- **Keyboard (web):** the dropdown is reachable with <kbd>Tab</kbd>; <kbd>Enter</kbd>, <kbd>Space</kbd> or <kbd>↓</kbd> open it; arrows move the highlight; <kbd>Enter</kbd> selects; <kbd>Esc</kbd> closes and returns focus to the field. Typing a letter jumps to matching options.
- **Screen readers** (<Term id="screen-reader">VoiceOver / TalkBack</Term>) should announce the **label**, the **current value**, that it's a **pop-up / combo box**, and whether it's **expanded**. Inside the list, each option should be announced with its position ("Zagreb, 3 of 6") and selected state.
  <DevNote>In code: `role="combobox"` + `aria-expanded` on the field, `role="listbox"` and `role="option"` + `aria-selected` in the menu.</DevNote>
- **Visible focus:** both the field and the highlighted option need a clear focus indicator, not only a subtle color.
- **Highlighted vs selected** must look different, so keyboard users know what Enter will choose.
- **Contrast** (<Term id="wcag" />): option text at least 4.5:1; the field border and chevron at least 3:1.
- **<Term id="touch-target">Touch targets</Term>:** options at least 44 pt tall on iOS and 48 dp on Android.
- **Combo boxes** should announce the number of results as you type ("5 results").

Try the gallery above with only your keyboard. Then try the bug hunt's City field. The difference is exactly what a keyboard or screen reader user experiences.

## Mobile vs web

<Tabs labels={['iOS', 'Android', 'Web']}>
<Tab>

- **Pop-up buttons** show a menu with a checkmark. Good for short lists.
  <DevNote>`UIButton` with a menu, or `Picker` with the `.menu` style in SwiftUI.</DevNote>
- **Wheel pickers** are used for dates, times and sizes. Test that the value saves when you stop scrolling, not only when you tap Done.
  <DevNote>`UIPickerView`, `UIDatePicker`, `.wheel` style.</DevNote>
- A web `<select>` in Safari opens the native iOS picker. Check long option labels there, because they get truncated.
- Long lists usually open a **new screen** with a search field instead of a menu.
- Test with **VoiceOver** (the rotor and swipe up/down adjust picker values) and the largest **Dynamic Type** size.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- Material 3 **exposed dropdown menu**; older apps use a spinner.
  <DevNote>`ExposedDropdownMenuBox` in Compose, `TextInputLayout` + `AutoCompleteTextView` in Views; the older one is a `Spinner`.</DevNote>
- The menu can open **above** the field when there's no room below. Test near the bottom of the screen and with the keyboard open.
- The system **Back** button should close an open menu without leaving the screen.
- Long lists often use a **bottom sheet** or a full-screen search instead.
- Test with **TalkBack**, **font scale** and **display size** settings.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- A native `<select>` is the safest choice: keyboard, screen reader and mobile pickers work out of the box.
- Custom dropdowns (React, Vue, UI libraries) must follow the ARIA **combobox** / **listbox** pattern. Check the roles in <Term id="devtools" />.
- Test **positioning**: near the bottom of the window, inside scrolling containers, inside modals and cards with hidden overflow.
- Test **zoom** at 200% and **small viewports**: does the menu still fit?
- <Term id="cross-browser-testing">Cross-browser</Term>: native selects look very different in Chrome, Safari and Firefox; check each one.

</Tab>
</Tabs>

<KeyTakeaways>

- A dropdown hides a **known list** of options behind a compact field. Use it for **5+ familiar options**; add **search** for long lists.
- For **2–5 options**, radio buttons or a segmented control are usually better. A **menu** runs actions and is a different component.
- Prefer a **clear placeholder** over a random pre-selected first option, and make required dropdowns block submit.
- The classic bugs: **dependent fields not reset, clipped menus, no keyboard support, strict search, unsorted or duplicate options, and menus that won't close**.
- Always test the **first and last** option, **changing the parent field**, **lowercase and accent-free search**, and **keyboard-only** use.

</KeyTakeaways>
