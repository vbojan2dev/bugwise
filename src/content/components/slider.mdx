Drag the volume in Control Center, set a price range in a hotel app, scrub through a podcast, choose how spicy your curry should be in a food delivery app: all sliders. They feel natural because you *move* to a value instead of typing it.

That's also why they're tricky. A slider turns finger movement into numbers, and every step of that translation (pixels, steps, rounding, limits) is a place for bugs. Here's how to test one properly.

## What is a slider?

A slider is a **control where you drag a thumb along a track to choose a value between a minimum and a maximum**. The position of the thumb *is* the value: far left is the minimum, far right the maximum (mirrored in right-to-left languages).

Sliders come in two pairs of flavors:

- **Continuous vs discrete.** A *continuous* slider allows any value in between (volume, brightness). A *discrete* slider snaps to **steps**: 1 km, €10, 0.5 stars. Discrete sliders often show tick marks (Material calls them *stops*) for each step.
- **Single vs range.** A *single* slider has one thumb and picks one value. A *range* slider has **two thumbs** and picks a low and a high value, like *€40 – €180* in a price filter.

You'll usually see sliders used for:

- **Settings you judge by feel**: volume, brightness, text size, photo filters.
- **Filters with a range**: price, distance, flight departure time.
- **Media playback**: the seek bar in video and audio players.
- **Small scales**: spice level, tip percentage, rating "4★ and up".

### How it differs from similar components

A slider is fast and visual, but **imprecise**. Knowing the alternatives helps you spot when the wrong one was used.

| Component | Precision | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Slider** | Rough, by feel | Relative values and ranges: volume, price filter |
| **[Stepper](/components/stepper)** (− / +) | Exact, small changes | Quantities: 1–10 tickets, guests, servings |
| **[Text field](/components/text-field)** (number) | Exact, any value | Age, amount to transfer, exact price |
| **[Segmented control](/components/segmented-control)** | One of 2–5 options | Named levels: *Mild · Medium · Hot* |
| **Toggle switch** | On / off | One setting that takes effect immediately |

<Callout type="dev" title="Platform names">
Apple and Google both call it a **slider**. Android's older view is called **SeekBar**, which is also what many people call the timeline in a video player. Material has a separate **RangeSlider**, and on the web you'll see `<input type="range">` or a custom "range slider". In bug reports, use the name your team uses.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Ask early: when does the value apply?">
A slider produces a **stream** of values while you drag. Before testing, find out whether the app should react **live** (volume, brightness: yes) or only **when you let go** (a price filter that asks the server: definitely). This one question uncovers a whole family of performance bugs.
</Callout>

## When to use it

A slider is a good choice when:

- The **exact value matters less than the rough position**: "louder", "cheaper", "a bit brighter".
- The range is **bounded** and **known**: 0–100 %, €0–€300, 1–10 km.
- Users benefit from **immediate visual feedback** while they move (brightness preview, photo filter).
- For a **range** (low and high value), a two-thumb slider is compact, ideally paired with text fields for exact input.

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **End value unreachable** | On a €0–€249 slider with €10 steps, the thumb stops at €240; the most expensive product never shows | High |
| 2 | **Range thumbs cross** | The minimum is dragged past the maximum: "€200 – €90" and zero results | Medium |
| 3 | **Floating-point noise** | The label says "0.30000000000000004" or "19.999999" instead of "0.3" or "20" | Low–Medium |
| 4 | **Request flood** | Every pixel of dragging sends an API call; the list flickers and the server gets hammered | Medium |
| 5 | **No keyboard support** | Tab can't reach the thumb, or arrow keys do nothing | High (for affected users) |
| 6 | **Screen reader says a bare number** | "40" instead of "€40", "0.35" instead of "35 %", or nothing but "slider" | Medium–High |
| 7 | **Value and thumb out of sync** | After Reset the label says €0 but the thumb is still in the middle | Medium |
| 8 | **Tiny thumb** | Hard to grab; you scroll the page or hit the other thumb instead | Medium |
| 9 | **Gesture conflicts** | Dragging the slider also swipes back, scrolls a carousel or closes a bottom sheet | Medium |
| 10 | **Not mirrored in RTL** | In Arabic or Hebrew, the minimum is on the left, or arrow keys move the wrong way | Medium |

<Callout type="bug" title="Why #1 and #3 happen">
**Steps and limits don't always fit.** If (max − min) isn't a multiple of the step, the last step falls short of the max. That's exactly how a native `<input type="range">` behaves: with min 0, max 249 and step 10, the highest value is 240. It's the slider version of an <Term id="off-by-one-error" />, and you find it with <Term id="boundary-value-analysis" />: always drag to **both ends**.

**Computers can't store 0.1 exactly.** In JavaScript, Swift and Kotlin alike, `0.1 + 0.2` gives `0.30000000000000004`. A slider that calculates `3 × 0.1` and shows the raw result displays noise, and worse, a filter "rating ≥ 4.800000000000001" silently hides a 4.8★ product. Always test decimal steps.
</Callout>

## Stress-test it

Push the slider to its limits: odd steps, decimal steps, a range of exact values, tiny touch areas and right-to-left layout. Drag both thumbs into each other and all the way to both ends.

<Callout type="info">
The **Events** counter shows why "when does the value apply?" matters. One drag across the track fires dozens of change events. That's fine for brightness. For a filter that calls the server, the app should wait until you let go, or use a <Term id="debounce" />.

<DevNote>On the web, a native range input fires `input` continuously and `change` when you release.</DevNote>

## Accessibility

Sliders are drag-only by nature, so everything else has to be added on purpose. Check these:

| Key (web) | What it should do |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>→</kbd> / <kbd>↑</kbd> | Increase by one step |
| <kbd>←</kbd> / <kbd>↓</kbd> | Decrease by one step |
| <kbd>Page Up</kbd> / <kbd>Page Down</kbd> | Bigger jump (e.g. 10 %) |
| <kbd>Home</kbd> / <kbd>End</kbd> | Jump to minimum / maximum |

- **Keyboard (web):** each thumb is reachable with <kbd>Tab</kbd>, has a visible focus ring and supports the keys above (the <Term id="aria" /> slider pattern). In right-to-left layouts, left and right follow the mirrored direction.
  <DevNote title="Roles and values">`role="slider"` with `aria-valuemin`, `aria-valuemax`, `aria-valuenow` and an accessible name (`aria-label` or a visible `<label>`). A native `<input type="range">` gets all of this for free.</DevNote>
- **Meaningful value text:** if the raw number isn't enough, announce words: "€40", "35 %", "1 minute 24 seconds of 3 minutes 45 seconds".
  <DevNote>On the web that's `aria-valuetext`; on iOS the `accessibilityValue`; on Android the state description.</DevNote>
- **Range sliders:** each thumb needs its **own** name ("Minimum price", "Maximum price"), otherwise users hear "slider, slider".
- **<Term id="screen-reader">Screen readers</Term>:** VoiceOver users swipe **up/down** to adjust a focused slider. TalkBack users adjust it with the volume keys or TalkBack's adjust gestures, depending on the version. Every step should be announced.
- **<Term id="touch-target">Touch targets</Term>:** the visible thumb may be small, but its touch area should be at least 44×44 pt (iOS) / 48×48 dp (Android). On the web, WCAG 2.5.8 (AA) asks for at least 24×24 CSS px; 44×44 px (2.5.5, AAA) is the comfortable target.
- **Contrast:** the active track and thumb need at least 3:1 contrast against the background (<Term id="wcag" />), and the value label 4.5:1.
- **An alternative to dragging:** WCAG 2.2 criterion 2.5.7 Dragging Movements (Level AA) asks for a way to operate dragging controls with a single tap or click. Tapping a point on the track, +/− buttons or a text field all count.

## Mobile vs web

<Tabs labels={['iOS', 'Android', 'Web']}>
<Tab>

- The native slider is **continuous** by default.
  <DevNote>Native component: `UISlider` (UIKit) or `Slider` (SwiftUI); SwiftUI takes an optional `step`.</DevNote>

  <DevNote>`UISlider.isContinuous` decides this.</DevNote>
- Test with **VoiceOver** (swipe up/down to adjust), **Dynamic Type** and the **swipe-back gesture**: dragging a slider near the left edge must not navigate back.

<Tab>

<KeyTakeaways>

- A slider picks a value (one thumb) or a range (two thumbs) by dragging. It's great for **rough, relative values**, poor for **exact values** like age or money.
- **Continuous** sliders allow any value; **discrete** sliders snap to steps. Steps that don't divide the range make the **max unreachable**.
- Decimal steps need **rounding**: `0.1 + 0.2` is not `0.3` in floating-point math.
- Range thumbs must **never cross**, and filters should apply **on release or debounced**, not on every pixel.
- Always test **both ends**, **keyboard** (arrows, Page Up/Down, Home, End), **screen reader value text with units**, **touch target size** and **RTL**.

</KeyTakeaways>
