Swipe an email to the left in iPhone Mail: red **Trash**, orange **Flag**, grey **More** slide out from behind the row. Swipe a chat in WhatsApp: **Archive**. Swipe a saved card in a banking app: **Remove**. These rows look like a plain list until your finger moves, and then they become a little toolbar.

That hidden toolbar is where the bugs live. Which row is open? What happens when you scroll? Does "Archive" really archive? Can someone who can't swipe still do the same thing? Let's learn the component properly.

## What is a list with swipe actions?

A list with swipe actions is a normal vertical list where each row hides **extra actions** that appear when you swipe the row sideways. Tapping the row still does the main thing (open the email, open the chat, open the product). Swiping is a **shortcut** for the secondary things you'd otherwise do from inside the item or from a menu.

You'll find it in:

- **Mail and messaging:** Archive, Delete, Mark as read, Pin, Mute.
- **Shopping:** Remove from cart or wishlist, Move to "Save for later".
- **Banking and finance:** Remove a saved payee or card, hide a transaction.
- **To-do and notes:** Complete, Delete, Flag.

### Anatomy

Every row is a normal list item first. Learn its parts, because many bugs are "text overlaps chevron" or "secondary line missing" rather than anything to do with swiping.

<Anatomy />

### Leading vs. trailing actions

The two sides have names that work in every language:

- **Trailing actions** sit at the **trailing edge** (the right in English) and appear when you swipe toward the leading edge (to the **left**). This is where **destructive** actions live: Delete, Archive, Remove.
- **Leading actions** sit at the **leading edge** (the left in English) and appear when you swipe to the **right**. They're usually **positive and reversible**: Mark as read, Pin, Add to cart.

In right-to-left languages like Arabic and Hebrew (<Term id="rtl">RTL</Term>), everything mirrors: trailing actions appear on the **left**. A row that still reveals Delete on the right in Arabic is a localization bug.

### Reveal buttons vs. full swipe

There are two ways a swipe can end:

| Style | What happens | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Reveal** | A short swipe snaps the row open and shows buttons. You tap one. | Low: two steps, you see what you'll get |
| **Full swipe** | Keep swiping past a point and the outermost action runs on release | Higher: one movement, easy to do by accident |
| **Swipe to dismiss** | No buttons; the row slides away and the action runs (Gmail style) | Highest: must come with Undo |

iOS uses **reveal + optional full swipe** (iPhone Mail: a full swipe left trashes or archives). Android apps usually use **swipe to dismiss** with a colored background and an icon (Gmail archives). A good full swipe gives feedback at the point of no return: the outermost button **stretches** across the row and the phone gives a small <Term id="haptic-feedback">haptic</Term> tick. If you let go before that point, nothing happens.

### How it differs from similar patterns

Swipe actions are never the only way to do something. These are the patterns they're usually paired with:

| Pattern | How it's triggered | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Swipe actions** | Swiping one row | Fast, frequent actions on one item |
| **Edit mode** | An **Edit** or **Select** button, then checkboxes | Acting on many items at once |
| **Long-press menu** | Touch and hold a row | Discoverable list of all actions |
| **Buttons in the detail view** | Open the item, use its toolbar | The always-available, visible path |

## When to use it

Swipe actions are a good choice when:

- People **process many items**, one after another: triaging an inbox, clearing notifications, checking off a shopping list.
- The actions are **frequent** and **quick** (one tap or one swipe), like Archive or Mark as read.
- The same actions are **also available elsewhere**: in the item's detail screen, a long-press menu or an edit mode.
- The result is **easy to undo**, or the action is protected by Undo or a confirmation.

<Compare>
<Do title="Two or three actions">

**Archive** and **Delete** on the trailing side, **Read** on the leading side. Each button is at least as tall as the row and has a short label.

</Do>
<Dont title="Five buttons crammed in">

Delete, Archive, Flag, Move, Share, Mute all on one side. Labels truncate, buttons get narrower than a fingertip, and the row has to be swiped across the whole screen. Put the extras behind a **More** button.

</Dont>
</Compare>

<Compare>
<Do title="Lists of separate items">

A list of emails, chats, products, alarms: each row is one thing you can act on.

</Do>
<Dont title="Rows that already move sideways">

A row with a horizontal carousel or a slider inside, or a list inside a screen that itself swipes between tabs. The gestures fight each other. See [Gestures](/mobile/gestures#when-gestures-collide).

</Dont>

## How good swipe rows behave

These are the rules to test against. Most of them aren't written in any requirement, but users expect them because the built-in apps behave this way.

### Only one row is open at a time

Open the actions on one row, then start swiping another: the first row **closes**. Two open rows look broken and, worse, can confuse the code about **which** item an action belongs to.

### An open row closes when you do something else

Scrolling the list, tapping the open row, tapping another row, pulling to refresh or leaving the screen should all **close** the open row. Tapping an open row should only close it, **not** also open the item.

### The action happens to the right item

The button you tap must act on **its own row**, even after you've scrolled, after another row was deleted, or after new items arrived at the top. In long lists, rows are **recycled** (the same row view is reused for a different item as you scroll). If the "open" state is stored on the recycled view or by **position** instead of by item, it jumps to the wrong item. That's a <Term id="state-persistence" /> bug in disguise.

<DevNote>Rows are recycled in `UITableView`, `RecyclerView`, `LazyColumn` and most web virtual lists.</DevNote>

### Labels tell the truth

"Archive" must archive (the item can be found in Archived), "Delete" must delete (maybe to a Trash folder), "Mute" must mute. Check the result **after** the action, not just that the row disappeared.

### Destructive actions: Undo or confirmation

| Use… | When | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Undo** in a <Term id="toast" /> or snackbar | The action is common and easy to reverse | Archive a chat, delete an email to Trash |
| **Confirmation** (an action sheet or dialog) | The action is permanent or expensive to reverse | Delete a saved card, cancel a booking |
| **Neither** | Only when the action is harmless | Mark as read, Pin |

Don't confirm everything: a dialog on every archive teaches people to tap **Delete** without reading. And Undo must really restore the item, **in its original position**, with its state (unread, pinned) intact.

### Haptics and feedback

While swiping, the row should show **what will happen**: the color, icon and label of the action. On iOS a full swipe gives a light haptic tick when it passes the point of no return. Haptics should follow the system setting and can only be checked on a <Term id="real-device" />.

### Large text, small screens and RTL

With the largest text size (<Term id="dynamic-type" /> on iOS, Font size on Android), the row grows taller, text wraps or truncates **inside its own area**, and the action buttons grow with it. Text must never run underneath or on top of the buttons. In RTL languages, the sides swap.

### Long lists stay smooth

A list of 2,000 transactions must scroll and swipe without stutter. Rows are usually created only when they come on screen (<Term id="lazy-loading" />, <Term id="infinite-scroll" />). Test swiping right after scrolling fast, and on an older, slower phone.

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Several rows open** | Two or more rows show their buttons at the same time | Low–Medium |
| 2 | **Action hits the wrong item** | Tap Delete on "Mom", and "Emma" disappears | Critical |
| 3 | **Row stays open** | Buttons still showing after scrolling, opening the item or coming back | Low–Medium |
| 4 | **Tap on an open row opens the item** | Instead of closing, the row opens the detail screen | Low |
| 5 | **Label lies** | "Archive" deletes; "Mark as read" also mutes | High |
| 6 | **State jumps to another row** | After a delete, or after scrolling a long list, a different row appears open | Medium–High |
| 7 | **No Undo / no confirmation** | A permanent delete happens with one swipe | High |
| 8 | **Layout breaks** | Text runs over the buttons at large text sizes; labels truncate in German | Medium |
| 9 | **Gesture-only** | No edit mode, menu or detail button; screen reader users can't reach the action | High (for affected users) |
| 10 | **Not mirrored in RTL** | In Arabic, Delete still appears on the right | Medium |

<Callout type="bug" title="Why #2 and #6 happen">
Developers often remember the open row by its **position** ("row 3 is open") instead of by **item** ("the chat with Mom is open"). As soon as rows move (a delete, an insert, a refresh, a recycled row), position 3 is a different item. To catch it: open a row, then make the list change **while it's open**, and check which item each action affects.
</Callout>

### Tools

- **Accessibility Inspector** (Xcode › Open Developer Tool) shows a row's **Custom Actions**. If the list is empty, VoiceOver users can't reach the swipe actions.
- **Android Studio Layout Inspector** and the **Accessibility Scanner** app show whether a row has accessibility actions; TalkBack's **Actions** menu is the real-world check.
- **Settings for stress:** largest text size, a long language (German), an RTL language (Arabic), and **Reduce Motion** (the row should still open and close, just without the slide).
- **Network log or <Term id="proxy-tool" />:** after Archive or Delete, check that **one** request goes to the server, for the **right** item ID. Then reopen the app to make sure the change was saved and not only hidden on screen.

## Accessibility

Swiping is a path-based gesture, so it's one of the hardest things to do for people with tremors, limited dexterity, or who can't see the screen. Check these:

- **Screen readers:** with <Term id="screen-reader">VoiceOver or TalkBack</Term> on, a swipe moves focus, it never reaches the row. Every swipe action must be exposed as an **accessibility action**. On iOS, standard swipe actions do this automatically ("Actions available", then swipe up or down to choose). TalkBack lists them in its Actions menu.
  <DevNote>Standard swipe actions are the ones in `List` and `UITableView`. Custom-built rows need `accessibilityCustomActions` (UIKit) or `.accessibilityAction` (SwiftUI). On Android, Compose uses `customActions` in semantics; Views use `ViewCompat.addAccessibilityAction`.</DevNote>
- **A visible alternative:** <Term id="wcag" /> 2.5.1 Pointer Gestures (Level A) requires a single-pointer way without a path, such as a button in the detail view, a long-press menu or an **Edit** mode. Voice Control and Switch Control users rely on these.
- **Clear labels:** icon-only action buttons need text labels for assistive tech ("Archive", not "button").
- **Announce the result:** after Archive or Delete, the screen reader should hear "Chat archived" and focus should move to a sensible place (the next row), not jump to the top.
- **<Term id="touch-target">Touch targets</Term>:** revealed buttons are at least 44×44 pt (iOS) / 48×48 dp (Android), and as tall as the row. On the web, WCAG 2.5.8 (AA) asks for at least 24×24 CSS px; 44×44 px (2.5.5, AAA) is the comfortable target.
- **Keyboard (web):** every action is reachable with <kbd>Tab</kbd> and <kbd>Enter</kbd>. Hover-only buttons must also show on focus. See [Accessibility basics](/web/accessibility-basics).

<KeyTakeaways>

- Swipe actions are **shortcuts** on list rows: **trailing** (swipe left) for destructive actions, **leading** (swipe right) for positive ones, mirrored in RTL.
- Know the three styles: **reveal buttons**, **full swipe** and **swipe to dismiss**, and the feedback each needs.
- Test **state**: only one row open, rows close on scroll and tap, actions hit the **right item**, nothing inherits an open state after a delete.
- Check that labels tell the truth and that destructive actions have **Undo or confirmation**.
- Every swipe action needs an alternative: **edit mode, long-press menu or detail button**, plus **accessibility actions** for VoiceOver and TalkBack.

</KeyTakeaways>
