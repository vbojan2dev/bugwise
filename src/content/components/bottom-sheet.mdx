You're about to order dinner and tap **Delivery time**. A panel slides up from the bottom with time slots. You pick 19:30, scroll to see later slots... and the whole panel slides away. Did it save 19:30 or not? You can't tell. That panel is a **bottom sheet**, and that moment of doubt is a bug.

Bottom sheets are everywhere on phones: sort and filter options, share menus, maps, music players, checkout choices. They feel natural because they sit where your thumb is. But they mix **two gestures in one place** (scroll the content, drag the sheet), they have **many exits**, and they often hold **unsaved choices**. Let's learn them properly.

## What is a bottom sheet?

A bottom sheet is a **surface that slides up from the bottom edge and shows extra content or options** related to the current screen. It can usually be **dragged** to different heights and **swiped down** to close.

There are two kinds, and testers must know which one they're looking at:

- **Modal bottom sheet**: sits on top of the page behind a dim **scrim**, blocks everything else and must be closed before you continue. It's a <Term id="modal" /> that happens to come from the bottom. Example: *Sort by*, *Share*, *Choose delivery time*.
- **Standard (persistent) bottom sheet**: lives **next to** the main content, without a scrim. You can still use the page behind it. Example: the list of places under a map, the mini player in a music app.

<Callout type="dev" title="Platform names">
Apple calls them **sheets**. They can stop at heights called **detents** (*medium* is about half the screen, *large* is almost full), and the little bar on top is the **grabber**. The short list of actions with a separate Cancel is an **action sheet** (now built as a *confirmation dialog*). Material Design says **bottom sheet** (*modal* or *standard*) and **drag handle**. On the web you'll hear **bottom sheet**, **drawer** or **bottom drawer**. In bug reports, use your team's name and say which kind it is.
</Callout>

### Modal or standard?

Ask one question: **can I still use the page behind it?**

- If **no**, it's modal: there must be a scrim, taps must not reach the page, focus must stay inside, and there must be a clear way out.
- If **yes**, it's standard: there's no scrim, the page behind must keep working (zoom the map, tap a pin), and it usually can't be dismissed, only collapsed to a "peek" height.

A sheet that dims the page but still lets taps through, or that blocks the page without dimming it, is mixing the two. That's a bug, or at least a design finding.

## When to use it

A bottom sheet is a good choice when:

- The task is **short and related to the current screen**: pick a sort order, choose a time slot, share a link.
- The user benefits from **seeing the screen behind** (a map, a product) while choosing.
- There are **a few options or one small form** that fit comfortably at the first height.
- The content should be **within thumb reach** on a phone.

## How a good bottom sheet behaves

### Heights and dragging

A sheet opens at a **defined height** (often medium, or just tall enough for its content) and can snap between a few heights, called **detents**. Dragging the grabber up expands it; dragging down collapses it to the next height, or closes it from the lowest one. When you let go between heights, it should **snap** to the nearest one. A fast flick should go one step in the flick's direction.

It should also **reopen at its default height** every time, and never show up cut off, empty, or taller than the screen.

### Scroll vs drag

This is where most sheet bugs live. A sheet with a long list has two things that move with the same finger movement. The rule real apps follow:

<Steps>

1. **Dragging up** on the content first expands the sheet to its largest height, then scrolls the list.
2. **Dragging down** on the content scrolls the list back up. Only when the list is **at the very top** does a downward drag start moving the sheet down.
3. **The grabber and header** always move the sheet, never the list.

</Steps>

If dragging the list moves the sheet even when it's scrolled down, users can't scroll, and they close the sheet when they only wanted to see earlier items.

### Every way to close a modal sheet

| Exit | Where | What must happen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cancel / X / Close** | Everywhere | Closes, changes nothing |
| **Swipe down** | iOS, Android, often mobile web | Same as Cancel |
| **Tap the scrim** | Everywhere | Same as Cancel. The tap must **not** reach the page behind |
| **System Back** | Android (button or gesture) | Closes the sheet first, not the screen behind |
| **Esc key** | Web; hardware keyboards on phones and tablets | Same as Cancel |
| **Screen reader escape** | VoiceOver two-finger Z, TalkBack Back | Same as Cancel |
| **Done / Apply / Confirm** | Everywhere | The **only** exit that saves the choice |

<Callout type="warning" title="The golden rule">
Every exit except the confirm button means **"cancel"**. A swipe must never **save** a choice the user didn't confirm, and must never **silently throw away** a half-filled form either. If there are unsaved changes, block the swipe and ask *"Discard changes?"*, which is what iOS apps do when they disable interactive dismissal. Some sheets apply each choice instantly (a sort order) and have no Confirm at all; that's fine, as long as it's consistent and clear.
</Callout>

### The keyboard

When a field inside the sheet gets focus, the keyboard takes up **about 40% of the screen**. The sheet must move up so the **focused field and the main button stay visible**. If the sheet is tall, its content should shrink and scroll rather than push the header off the top of the screen. Test in portrait **and** landscape, where very little space is left.

### Small screens, landscape and large text

Test on the **smallest supported phone**, in **landscape**, and at the **largest text size** (<Term id="dynamic-type" /> on iOS, Font size on Android). The header and the main button must stay reachable, the content must scroll, and the sheet must respect the <Term id="safe-area" /> so the home indicator doesn't cover the last button.

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Scroll fights drag** | Dragging the list moves the sheet; you can't scroll, or you close it by accident | High |
| 2 | **Keyboard covers the field** | The text field or Save button is hidden behind the keyboard | High |
| 3 | **Dismiss loses or saves silently** | A swipe throws away a half-filled form, or applies a choice you never confirmed | High |
| 4 | **Taps go through the scrim** | Tapping the dimmed area also presses a button on the page behind | Medium–High |
| 5 | **Gesture-only closing** | No Cancel or X; Esc, Back and screen reader escape don't work | High (for affected users) |
| 6 | **Wrong height** | Reopens half-collapsed, cut off, or taller than the screen on small phones | Medium |
| 7 | **Page behind scrolls** | Scrolling inside the sheet also scrolls the page under it | Medium |
| 8 | **Focus stays behind** | The screen reader keeps reading the page, not the sheet | High (for affected users) |
| 9 | **Stuck or jumpy animation** | The sheet stops between heights or bounces after a fast flick | Low |
| 10 | **Double sheets** | A double tap opens two sheets, or the sheet reopens after closing | Medium |

<Callout type="bug" title="Why #1 happens">
The sheet and the list inside it both listen for a vertical drag. The app has to decide, at the start of each gesture, **who gets it**: the list (if it can still scroll in that direction) or the sheet. This hand-off is called nested scrolling, and custom-built sheets often skip it. To catch it, **scroll the list down a bit first**, then drag down on the list and watch what moves.
</Callout>

### Tools

- **iOS Simulator:** *I/O › Keyboard › Toggle Software Keyboard* (<kbd>Cmd</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd>) shows the on-screen keyboard. With *Connect Hardware Keyboard* on, the software keyboard may never appear, so you'd miss bug #2. Turn on VoiceOver in *Settings › Accessibility* or use the Accessibility Inspector.
- **Android Emulator:** test **Back** with the button and with gesture navigation, rotate with the toolbar, and raise *Font size* and *Display size* in Settings. Turn on TalkBack from Settings › Accessibility.
- **Web:** use the device toolbar in [DevTools](/web/devtools-for-testers) for small viewports, but test the keyboard on a **real phone**: desktop emulation doesn't show the virtual keyboard. Check `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal` and focus in the Accessibility pane.

## Accessibility

- **Announce it.** A modal sheet is a dialog: the <Term id="screen-reader" /> should say its title and that it's a dialog when it opens. See [Modal](/components/modal) for the full set of dialog rules.
  <DevNote>On the web, use `role="dialog"` with `aria-modal="true"` and `aria-labelledby` (or a native `<dialog>`).</DevNote>
- **Focus moves in, stays in, comes back.** Focus goes to the sheet when it opens, a <Term id="focus-trap" /> keeps <kbd>Tab</kbd> inside a modal sheet, and focus returns to the trigger when it closes.
- **Never gesture-only.** Every <Term id="gesture" /> needs a non-gesture alternative (see [Gestures](/mobile/gestures)): a visible **Cancel** or **Close** button, **Esc**, Android **Back** and the **VoiceOver escape** gesture. <Term id="wcag">WCAG</Term> 2.2 asks that anything done by **dragging** can also be done with a simple tap or click (success criterion 2.5.7, Dragging Movements), so resizing should also work by tapping or activating the grabber.
- **Grabber with a name.** If the grabber is focusable, it needs a label and action, e.g. "Sheet grabber, double-tap to expand". iOS and Material's drag handles provide this; custom ones often don't.
- **<Term id="touch-target">Touch targets</Term>:** Cancel, X and every option at least 44×44 pt (iOS) / 48×48 dp (Android). A thin grabber alone is hard to hit, so the whole header should be draggable.
- **Large text and contrast:** content scrolls rather than clips at the biggest text size, text has 4.5:1 contrast and the sheet's edge stands out from the page (3:1, or a clear scrim).

## Mobile vs web

<Tabs labels={['iOS', 'Android', 'Web']}>
<Tab>

- **Sheets**: rounded card with an optional **grabber**, swipe down to close.
  <DevNote>`UISheetPresentationController` / SwiftUI `.sheet` with `.presentationDetents([.medium, .large])`.</DevNote>
- Apps can block swipe-to-dismiss when there are unsaved changes and ask first. Test that it's on where it should be.
  <DevNote>`.interactiveDismissDisabled()` / `isModalInPresentation`.</DevNote>
- A sheet can leave the page behind usable at smaller heights (like Maps). Then it behaves like a standard sheet: no dimming, page still works.
- **Action sheets** (confirmation dialogs) are not draggable and always have Cancel. On iPad, sheets and action sheets look different (centered or as a popover), so test both.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- Material 3 **modal bottom sheets** have a scrim, rounded 28 dp top corners and a **drag handle**. They can open partially expanded, then expand fully.
- **Standard bottom sheets** stay on screen next to the content, often with a peek height.
  <DevNote>Compose: `ModalBottomSheet` and `BottomSheetScaffold`.</DevNote>
- **System Back** (button or gesture) must close a modal sheet first. With predictive back, the sheet should animate along with the Back swipe and only close when you release.
- Test **rotation** and **process death**: the sheet may vanish, duplicate or lose its selection. Test **TalkBack**, Font size and Display size.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- On desktop the sheet is often a side drawer or centered dialog. Test **both breakpoints**.
  <DevNote>There's no native "bottom sheet" element. It's a `<dialog>` or a custom `div` styled as a sheet.</DevNote>
- Check **Esc**, the **close button**, **click outside**, the **focus trap** and **scroll lock** (the page behind must not scroll, especially on iOS Safari).
- On a real phone, check the **virtual keyboard**: mobile browsers don't always resize the page, so a sheet stuck to the bottom can end up behind it.
- Decide what **browser Back** does while the sheet is open. Either way, it must not lose the user's input.

<KeyTakeaways>

- A **modal** sheet blocks the page with a scrim; a **standard** sheet sits beside the content and keeps the page usable. Know which one you're testing.
- Sheets snap between **detents**. They should always **reopen at their default height**, fully visible.
- **Scroll before drag:** the content scrolls first, and the sheet moves only from the top of the list.
- Every exit except Confirm means **cancel**. Swipes must never silently save a choice or silently throw away a form.
- Always test **the keyboard**, **small screens and landscape**, **taps on the scrim**, and **closing without gestures** (Cancel, Esc, Back, screen reader).

</KeyTakeaways>
