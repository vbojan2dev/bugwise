Standard or Express delivery? Card or PayPal? Window or aisle? Whenever an app asks you to pick **exactly one** answer from a short list, you're probably looking at radio buttons. The name comes from old car radios, where pushing in one station button popped the others out.

That "only one" promise is the whole point, and it's exactly what breaks: two payment methods selected at once, a total that ignores your choice, an order placed with nothing selected. Let's learn how to catch it.

## What is a radio button?

A radio button is a **round option inside a group**, where **exactly one option can be selected**. Selecting one automatically unselects the others: the options are <Term id="mutually-exclusive" />. A single radio button on its own makes no sense. It always lives in a **group** with a question ("Delivery", "Payment method").

You'll see radio groups used for:

- **Delivery and shipping options**: Standard / Express / Next day, each with a date and a price.
- **Payment methods**: Card / PayPal / Pay on delivery.
- **Plans and sizes**: Monthly / Yearly, Small / Medium / Large.
- **Survey and preference questions**: Seat preference, "How did you hear about us?".

### Two rules that define a radio group

1. **Exactly one can be selected.** Selecting a second option unselects the first.
2. **Once something is selected, you can't go back to "nothing".** Tapping a selected radio again does nothing. If "no answer" is valid, the group needs an option for it, like *None* or *Prefer not to say*.

Both rules are worth a test case of their own.

### Radio vs dropdown vs segmented control vs checkbox

| Component | Choices | Visible? | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Radio buttons** | Exactly one of ~2–6 | All at once | Forms, options that need a description or price |
| **[Dropdown](/components/dropdown)** | One of many | Hidden until opened | Long lists: country, size chart, time slot |
| **[Segmented control](/components/segmented-control)** | One of 2–5 short labels | All at once | Switching views or filters **immediately** |
| **[Checkbox](/components/checkbox)** | Zero, one or many | All at once | Picking several items, or one yes/no |

<Callout type="dev" title="Platform names">
The web calls it a **radio button** (`<input type="radio">`). Material Design calls it a **radio button** too. Apple's iOS guidelines don't have one at all: iPhone apps use **checkmark lists**, pickers or menus for single choice. Some teams say **option button**. In bug reports, use the name your team uses.
</Callout>

When options carry details (delivery dates, prices, plan features), the label gets long. That's fine, and much better than hiding details in a dropdown, but then **the whole card must be tappable**, not just the 20 px circle. Testers should always tap the description and the price too.

### Keyboard: one tab stop, arrows inside

Radio groups work differently from every other form control on the keyboard. Try it: click into "Passenger name", then use <kbd>Tab</kbd>, the arrow keys and <kbd>Space</kbd>, and watch the log.

<KeyboardPlayground client:visible />

This is what native radio buttons do for free. Custom-built radio groups (made of `div`s or styled buttons) often get it wrong: every option becomes its own tab stop, or the arrow keys do nothing. Both are bugs. See <Term id="keyboard-navigation" />.

## When to use it

Radio buttons are the right choice when:

- The user must pick **exactly one** option.
- There are **few options** (roughly 2–6), so they can all be shown at once.
- Seeing the options **side by side** helps the decision: prices, delivery dates, plan details.
- The choice is part of a **form** and applies when the user presses Continue, Pay or Save.

### Should one option be preselected?

Many design guidelines recommend selecting a sensible default, because it saves a tap and the user can't accidentally skip the question. But a default is a decision made *for* the user, so it has to be a safe one.

| Preselect when… | Don't preselect when… |
| --- | --- |
| There's a **safe, common** choice: Standard (free) delivery | The default **costs money**: insurance, a paid upgrade |
| The setting already **has a value**: the user's saved card | The question is **personal or sensitive**: title, gender, date of birth |
| A wrong default is easy to notice and **cheap to change** | You need to know the user **really chose** (consent, legal answers) |

If there's no default, the form **must** block submission until the user chooses, with a clear error. Whatever the rule, it should be written in the spec, so you can test against it.

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Broken grouping** | Two options in the same group can be selected at once (often a mismatched `name` on the web) | High |
| 2 | **Wrong keyboard behavior** | Arrow keys don't move the selection, or every option is a separate tab stop | High (for affected users) |
| 3 | **Dependent values not updated** | Switching Express → Standard doesn't change the total, delivery date or fee | High |
| 4 | **Nothing selected, still submits** | No default, and the form goes through with no choice (or sends `null`) | High |
| 5 | **Tiny target** | Only the circle reacts; tapping the label, description or price does nothing | Medium |
| 6 | **Selection lost** | After a validation error, Back, rotation or reload the choice jumps back to the default | Medium |
| 7 | **Bad default** | A paid or sensitive option is preselected, or the default doesn't match the spec | Medium–High |
| 8 | **Disabled option without reason** | An option is greyed out, but nothing says why ("Not available for your address") | Low–Medium |
| 9 | **Disabled option still selectable** | A greyed-out option can be picked by keyboard or by tapping its label | Medium |
| 10 | **Screen reader silence** | No group name, no "radio button", no "selected", no "2 of 3" | High (for affected users) |

<Callout type="bug" title="Why bug #3 is so common">
The radio button shows one value, but other parts of the screen are **calculated** from it: shipping fee, total, delivery date, the label on the Pay button. Developers often update the selection and forget one of the calculated parts, or calculate it from the **previous** value. After every switch, re-read every number on the screen, and check the final amount on the confirmation screen and in the order email.
</Callout>

## Accessibility

- **Name the group.** Otherwise a <Term id="screen-reader" /> user hears "Express, radio button" without knowing the question.
  <DevNote>On the web, put radios in a `<fieldset>` with a `<legend>`, or use `role="radiogroup"` with `aria-labelledby`.</DevNote>
- **Group by name.** Native radios belong together only if they share the same `name`. A typo or a "new" option with a different name breaks the group.
- **Connect labels.** The label makes the text clickable and gives the radio its spoken name. On mobile the whole row should be the <Term id="touch-target" />: at least 44×44 pt on iOS and 48×48 dp on Android. On the web, WCAG 2.5.8 (AA) asks for at least 24×24 CSS px; 44×44 px (2.5.5, AAA) is the comfortable target.
  <DevNote>`<label for>` or a wrapping `<label>`.</DevNote>
- **Keyboard (web):** <kbd>Tab</kbd> enters the group once, arrow keys move focus and selection, <kbd>Space</kbd> selects the focused option. See <Term id="aria" /> and <Term id="focus-order" />.
  <DevNote>Custom radios need `role="radio"`, `aria-checked` and a roving `tabindex` to behave the same.</DevNote>
- **Announce state and position.** A good screen reader output sounds like "Express, radio button, not selected, 2 of 3".
- **Errors.** If a required group is left empty, show the error text next to the group, and move focus or announce it.
  <DevNote>Link the error with `aria-describedby`.</DevNote>
- **Not color alone.** The inner dot shows the selection. The ring needs at least **3:1** contrast against the background (<Term id="wcag" />).

## Mobile vs web

<Tabs labels={['iOS', 'Android', 'Web']}>
<Tab>

- **No native radio button.** Single choice is shown as a **list with a checkmark** on the selected row (Settings › Display & Brightness › Auto-Lock), a picker (inline, menu or wheel style), or a menu.
  <DevNote>The picker is a SwiftUI `Picker`.</DevNote>
- VoiceOver should announce the selected row as "Selected". Silence is a bug.
- Some apps draw custom radio circles. Check the whole row is tappable and that VoiceOver reads the state.
- Test with the largest **Dynamic Type** sizes: option descriptions with prices must wrap, not truncate.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- Material 3 radio button: a **20 dp** circle inside a **48 dp** touch target. The whole row should be tappable, and TalkBack should understand the group.
  <DevNote>`RadioButton`. In Compose, rows should use `Modifier.selectable(role = Role.RadioButton)` and the group `Modifier.selectableGroup()`.</DevNote>
- TalkBack should announce the label, "radio button" and whether it's selected. Test with **font scale**, **display size** and dark theme.
- Check the selection after **rotation** and after the app was killed in the background.
- Android dialogs often use radio lists ("Choose ringtone") with OK / Cancel. Cancel must discard the change.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- Native `<input type="radio">` gives grouping, keyboard and screen reader support. Inspect the `name` attributes with <Term id="devtools" />: all options of one question must share it.
- Custom radio cards are very common. Check they behave like native radios with the keyboard.
- The browser doesn't stop a form from submitting with nothing selected unless `required` is set, and the server must validate too.
- Test **browser Back** and **reload**: some browsers restore form state, others don't. Check what the spec expects.

</Tab>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Radio buttons are for **exactly one** choice from a **small, visible** set. Many options → dropdown. Several choices → checkboxes. Instant view switch → segmented control.
- Once selected, a group can't go back to "nothing". A **lone radio** is always wrong.
- Preselect only a **safe, free, common** default. No default → the form must **require** a choice, on the server too.
- Test **grouping** (only one selected), **everything calculated from the choice** (totals, dates), **the whole row as a target**, and **state after errors and Back**.
- On the web: one **tab stop** per group, **arrow keys** inside, a **legend** for the question, and a shared **name** for all options.

</KeyTakeaways>
