"I agree to the Terms." "Remember me." "Leave at the door." "Select all." You tick boxes like these dozens of times a week, usually without thinking. That's exactly why checkbox bugs are so sneaky: the box you can't tap, the "Select all" that lies about how many emails you're deleting, the marketing consent that was ticked *for* you.

A checkbox is also where UI testing meets **legal** testing. Let's learn both sides.

## What is a checkbox?

A checkbox is a small square the user **ticks to say "yes, include this"**. Each checkbox is **independent**: ticking one doesn't untick the others. And, unlike a <Term id="toggle-switch">toggle switch</Term>, ticking a checkbox usually **does nothing on its own**. The choice is applied when the user presses a button like **Continue**, **Save** or **Pay**.

You'll see checkboxes used for:

- **A single yes/no in a form**: *I agree to the Terms*, *Remember me*, *Billing address is the same as delivery*.
- **Picking several items from a list**: cuisines in a food app filter, newsletter topics, pizza toppings.
- **Bulk actions**: ticking emails, photos or files, then pressing **Archive** or **Delete**.
- **Consent**: *Send me offers by email*, *Share my data with partners*.

### The states

A checkbox has more states than "ticked or not". Each one needs testing:

| State | Looks like | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Unchecked** | Empty box | Not selected |
| **Checked** | Filled box with a tick | Selected |
| **Mixed** (indeterminate) | Filled box with a dash | Parent of a list where **some** children are checked |
| **Disabled** | Faded, keeps its tick or not | Can't be changed right now |
| **Error** | Red box + error text | A required box was left unchecked |
| **Focused** | Ring or highlight around it | Keyboard focus is here (web, external keyboards) |

<Callout type="info" title="Mixed is a display state, not a value">
You can't really "tick" mixed. It only appears on a **parent** checkbox ("Select all") when some of the items below are ticked. Screen readers announce it as "mixed" or "partially checked".

<DevNote>On the web it's set with JavaScript (`input.indeterminate = true`).</DevNote>
</Callout>

### Checkbox vs toggle switch vs radio button

The fastest way to spot a wrong component is to ask two questions: **can the user pick more than one?** and **does it apply immediately?**

| Component | How many? | When it applies | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Checkbox** | Zero, one or many | **On Submit / Save** | Toppings, "I agree to the Terms" |
| **Toggle switch** | One setting, on/off | **Immediately** | Dark theme, Wi-Fi |
| **Radio buttons** | **Exactly one** of a few | Usually on Submit | Standard / Express delivery |
| **Segmented control** | Exactly one of 2–5 | Immediately | Day / Week / Month |

See also the [radio button](/components/radio-button), [toggle switch](/components/toggle-switch) and [segmented control](/components/segmented-control) pages.

The idea is the same everywhere, but the platforms disagree on what it looks like. iOS doesn't even have one.

### "Select all" and the mixed state

A parent checkbox that controls a list is one of the most bug-prone patterns in any app. The rule is simple, and it must hold after **every** click:

<SelectAllPlayground client:visible />

What happens when you click a parent that shows a dash? Most apps **select all**, some **clear all**. Both are fine if the behavior is consistent and matches the spec. What's never fine: the parent saying "all" when it isn't, or a count that disagrees with the ticks.

## When to use it

A checkbox is the right choice when:

- The user can pick **any number** of options, including none: *Wi-Fi, Parking, Pool*.
- There's **one optional yes/no** in a form: *Remember me*, *Send me a copy*.
- The user must **actively agree** to something before continuing: *I accept the Terms*.
- The choice is applied **later**, when the user presses a button.

Also avoid checkboxes when the user must pick **exactly one** option (use radio buttons) and for a **single setting that applies immediately** (use a switch).

<Callout type="warning" title="Consent checkboxes are a legal topic">
Under the EU's GDPR, consent has to be an active choice: the regulation's Recital 32 says that silence, pre-ticked boxes or inactivity should not count as consent, and the EU Court of Justice confirmed this for pre-ticked boxes (the *Planet49* case, 2019). Similar rules exist in other countries, and they often require separate consent for separate purposes (for example, not bundling marketing into "I accept the Terms"). You're a tester, not a lawyer: if a consent box is pre-ticked or bundled, **report it** and let product and legal decide. See also <Term id="consent-banner">consent banners</Term>.
</Callout>

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Label not clickable** | Tapping the text does nothing, only the tiny box reacts | Medium |
| 2 | **"Select all" out of sync** | Parent stays ticked after you untick one item, never shows mixed, or the count is wrong | High (if it drives a bulk delete) |
| 3 | **Pre-ticked consent** | Marketing or data-sharing boxes are ticked by default | High (legal risk) |
| 4 | **Not keyboard operable** | A custom checkbox can be focused but <kbd>Space</kbd> doesn't toggle it, or it can't be focused at all | High (for affected users) |
| 5 | **Required box not enforced** | The form submits without "I agree", or only the browser checks it and the server accepts the request anyway | High |
| 6 | **Error not explained** | A required box turns red with no text, or the error isn't announced to screen readers | Medium |
| 7 | **State lost** | Ticks disappear after going Back, rotating, or a validation error elsewhere on the form | Medium |
| 8 | **Disabled but clickable** | A greyed-out box can still be toggled by tapping or with the keyboard | Medium |
| 9 | **Long labels break** | Text wraps under the box, gets truncated, or overlaps at large font sizes | Low–Medium |
| 10 | **Silent screen reader** | VoiceOver / TalkBack doesn't say "checkbox", "checked" or the label | High (for affected users) |

<Callout type="bug" title="Why bug #5 matters">
Hiding the Continue button until the box is ticked, or using the browser's `required` attribute, is only a UI check. Anyone can send the request directly with <Term id="devtools">DevTools</Term> or a proxy. If the server doesn't check that the Terms were accepted, you can end up with accounts that never agreed to anything. When you can, ask the developer how the server validates it, or try the request without the field. This is classic <Term id="input-validation" /> on both sides.
</Callout>

## Accessibility

- **Use the real thing if you can.** A native `<input type="checkbox">` gets keyboard support, form submission and screen reader announcements for free. Inspect custom checkboxes with <Term id="devtools" />. See <Term id="aria" />.
  <DevNote>Custom checkboxes need `role="checkbox"`, `aria-checked` (`true`, `false` or `mixed`), `tabindex="0"` and a <kbd>Space</kbd> key handler.</DevNote>
- **Connect the label.** The label makes the text clickable *and* gives the checkbox its spoken name. On mobile, make the whole row the <Term id="touch-target">touch target</Term>: 44×44 pt on iOS, 48×48 dp on Android.
  <DevNote>`<label for>` (or wrapping the input in a `<label>`).</DevNote>
- **Group related boxes.** A set like "Email me about" belongs in a group, so <Term id="screen-reader">screen readers</Term> read the question before the options.
  <DevNote>A `<fieldset>` with a `<legend>`.</DevNote>
- **Announce errors.** A required box needs a visible error text, and the error should be announced. A red outline alone fails users who can't see it.
  <DevNote>Link the error with `aria-describedby`; announce it, for example, with `role="alert"`.</DevNote>
- **Not color alone.** The tick or dash shape must show the state. The box border needs at least **3:1** contrast against the background (<Term id="wcag" />).
- **Keyboard (web):** <kbd>Tab</kbd> moves between checkboxes (each one is its own tab stop, unlike radio buttons), and <kbd>Space</kbd> toggles. <kbd>Enter</kbd> usually submits the form instead.

<KeyTakeaways>

- Checkboxes are **independent** and usually apply **on Submit**. Exactly one choice → radio buttons. Instant on/off → toggle switch.
- States to test: **unchecked, checked, mixed, disabled, error, focused**, in light and dark mode.
- A **"Select all"** parent must always match its children: none = unchecked, some = mixed, all = checked, and counts must agree.
- The **label is part of the target**. Tapping the text must toggle the box.
- **Consent boxes must not be pre-ticked**, and required boxes must be enforced on the **server** with a clear, announced error.
- Custom checkboxes need **Space** to work, plus the right role and state for screen readers.

</KeyTakeaways>
