You archive an email and a dark bar slides up from the bottom: **"Conversation archived · Undo"**. A few seconds later it's gone. You didn't have to tap anything, and you could keep working while it was there. That little bar is a <Term id="toast" />, called a **snackbar** in Material Design.

Toasts are small, but testers love them. They disappear on a timer, they appear on top of other things, they promise that something worked, and the **Undo** button has to really undo. Each of those is a place for bugs.

## What is a toast?

A toast is a **brief, non-blocking message** that appears on top of the screen, confirms what just happened, and **disappears automatically**. Unlike a <Term id="modal" />, it doesn't stop the user. They can ignore it and carry on.

You'll usually see one used to:

- **Confirm an action**: *Link copied*, *Added to bag*, *Settings saved*.
- **Offer a quick way back**: *Conversation archived · Undo*, *Item removed · Undo*.
- **Report a small, temporary problem with a fix**: *Couldn't send. Retry*.

### How it differs from similar components

| Component | Blocks the user? | Disappears by itself? | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Toast / snackbar** | No | Yes, after a few seconds | Feedback on an action, optional Undo |
| **Banner (in-page)** | No | No, until fixed or dismissed | Ongoing status: "You're offline", "Card expires soon" |
| **Inline message** | No | No | Form errors, next to the field that has the problem |
| **Dialog / alert** | Yes | No | Decisions and errors the user must deal with |
| **<Term id="push-notification">Push notification</Term>** | No | Goes to the notification center | Messages from outside the app, even when it's closed |

<Callout type="dev" title="Platform names">
On **Android**, a *Toast* is a specific system component (plain text, no buttons) and a *Snackbar* is the Material component with an optional action. **iOS** has no native toast at all: apps use custom banners or HUDs. On the **web**, people say *toast*, *notification*, *flash message* or *alert*. In a bug report, describe what you saw ("the snackbar at the bottom").
</Callout>

### How long should it stay?

There's no single number, but these are good rules to test against:

| Situation | Typical duration |
| --- | --- |
| Android system Toast | 2 s (`LENGTH_SHORT`) or 3.5 s (`LENGTH_LONG`), fixed by the system |
| Short confirmation, no action | About 4–6 s |
| With an action (Undo, Retry) | Longer, around 6–10 s, or until dismissed. Jetpack Compose's Material 3 snackbar stays until dismissed by default when it has an action |
| Material snackbar presets | Material's guidance is roughly 4–10 s. In Compose, `SnackbarDuration.Short` is 4 s and `Long` 10 s; the older View-based `Snackbar` uses much shorter presets (about 1.5 s and 2.75 s), so check which one the app uses |
| Error that needs the user | Don't auto-hide at all, or don't use a toast |

The longer and more important the message, the longer it should stay. On the web, the timer should also **pause while the pointer or keyboard focus is on the toast**, so it doesn't vanish under the cursor.

### The Undo pattern

Undo is what makes toasts powerful: instead of asking *"Are you sure?"* in a dialog, the app **does the action immediately and offers to reverse it**. That's faster for everyone, but it's only safe if Undo really works. Testers check:

<Steps>

1. **Undo restores exactly the right item**, in the same place, with the same state (read/unread, starred, labels).
2. **Undo works after several actions in a row**: archive two emails, then undo the last one.
3. **The server agrees.** After Undo, a refresh or another device still shows the item.
4. **Undo is reachable in time** with large text, a screen reader or a slow hand.
5. **Nothing happens twice.** Tapping Undo twice doesn't restore two copies.

</Steps>

## When to use it

A toast is a good choice when:

- The message is **short** (one line, two at most) and **confirms something the user just did**.
- The user **doesn't need to do anything**. If they miss it, nothing bad happens.
- There is **at most one** simple action, like Undo or Retry.
- The action was **reversible** or already succeeded.

<Callout type="warning" title="Critical information never goes in a toast">
Anything the user **must** see or act on (failed payments, lost data, security warnings, a booking that didn't go through) doesn't belong in something that disappears on a timer. If you see it in a toast, report it as a usability or accessibility bug.
</Callout>

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Undo doesn't undo** | Item doesn't come back, the wrong item comes back, or it reappears after refresh | High |
| 2 | **Too fast** | Disappears before you can read it or reach Undo | Medium |
| 3 | **Success after failure** | "Saved" even though the request failed (500 or offline in the network log) | High |
| 4 | **Endless stacking** | Many toasts pile up and cover the screen | Medium |
| 5 | **Covers important UI** | On top of the bottom navigation, the FAB, "Checkout" or the keyboard | Medium |
| 6 | **Truncated text** | Message or action cut off in other languages or with large text | Medium |
| 7 | **Timer never pauses (web)** | The toast disappears while your mouse is on its button | Low–Medium |
| 8 | **Screen reader silence** | VoiceOver / TalkBack doesn't announce it | High (for affected users) |
| 9 | **Shows on the wrong screen** | You navigate away and the toast follows, or appears after you return | Low |
| 10 | **Can't be dismissed** | No close button on a long or persistent toast; it blocks content | Low–Medium |

<Callout type="bug" title="Why #3 happens: optimistic UI">
Many apps update the screen **before** the server answers, to feel fast. That's fine, as long as they **roll back and show an error** if the request fails. When they don't, the user sees "Archived" while the server still has the email. To catch it, turn on a slow or failing network and watch the network log, then refresh.
</Callout>

## Accessibility

- **Announce it.** Screen readers don't notice text that just appears (<Term id="wcag" /> 4.1.3 Status Messages, Level AA). Native Android Snackbars and Toasts are announced by TalkBack; custom iOS banners need an announcement.
  <DevNote>On the web, put toasts inside a container with `role="status"` (or `aria-live="polite"`) that **already exists in the page** before the message is added. Use `role="alert"` only for urgent errors. On iOS: `UIAccessibility.post(notification: .announcement, ...)`.</DevNote>
- **Don't steal focus.** A toast should be announced, not focused: moving focus would interrupt what the user was doing.
- **Give enough time.** WCAG 2.2.1 (Timing Adjustable) asks that users can turn off, adjust or extend time limits. For toasts that means a long enough duration, pausing on hover and focus, and never putting the only copy of important information in one. Android also has a system setting ("Time to take action") that apps should respect.
- **Make the action reachable.** Keyboard users must be able to reach Undo before it disappears. Some apps offer an alternative, like a keyboard shortcut or an Undo item in a menu.
- **Contrast.** Message text needs 4.5:1 against the toast background, and the action button color too. Check in light and <Term id="dark-mode" />.
- **Don't rely on color alone** for success vs error: add an icon and clear words.
- **Large text.** At the biggest <Term id="dynamic-type" /> or font scale, text must wrap, not clip, and the action must stay visible.

## Mobile vs web

<Tabs labels={['iOS', 'Android', 'Web']}>
<Tab>

- **No native toast.** Apps build their own: top banners that look like notifications, centered HUDs, or Material-style bars from a library. Quality varies a lot.
- Check placement against the **notch / Dynamic Island**, the home indicator and the tab bar.
- Custom components often forget **VoiceOver announcements** and **Dynamic Type**.
- Swipe-to-dismiss (usually up for top banners) should work and must not trigger the system notification center.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- **Toast**: system pill, text only, 2 s or 3.5 s, no actions. Since Android 11, apps in the background can't show custom-view toasts, and Android 12+ limits toast text to two lines and shows the app icon.
- **Snackbar**: Material component with one optional action. One at a time, above the bottom navigation, pushes the FAB up.
- Test with **TalkBack**, maximum **font size**, gesture navigation vs 3-button navigation, and with the **keyboard open**.
- Rotate while a snackbar is visible: it should not duplicate or reappear.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- Usually from a library (or hand-made). Common positions: top right, top center, bottom center. Check the position on **small screens** too.
- Check the live region in <Term id="devtools" /> (Elements panel: `role="status"` / `aria-live`), and test with a screen reader.
- Check **pause on hover/focus**, the **close button**, and **stacking limits**.
- Check that a toast doesn't survive a page navigation it shouldn't, and that "Saved" matches the real <Term id="api" /> response in the Network tab.

</Tab>
</Tabs>

<KeyTakeaways>

- A toast is a **short, non-blocking message that disappears on its own**. Use it for confirmations and easy Undo, never for errors the user must act on or critical information.
- Android has a **Toast** (text only) and the Material **Snackbar** (one action). **iOS has no native toast**. On the web, toasts usually stack in a corner.
- Timing matters: **4 s or more**, **longer with an action**, and on the web **pause on hover and focus**.
- Test **Undo** hard: the right item, several in a row, and after a refresh.
- Classic bugs: **success shown after a failure**, **endless stacking**, **covering navigation**, **truncated text** and **screen reader silence** (`role="status"`).

</KeyTakeaways>
