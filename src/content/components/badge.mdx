A red **3** on the Chats tab. A dot on the bell. **Sale −20%** on a pair of running shoes. A **12** on your banking app's icon that won't go away, no matter how many times you open the app.

Badges are tiny, but they make a promise: *"there's something here for you"*. When the promise is wrong (a "0" that should be gone, a count that never updates, a number nobody can hear) users stop trusting it, or they open the app twenty times a day for nothing. Here's how to test them.

## What is a badge?

A badge is a **small marker attached to another element** (an icon, a tab, an avatar, a card) that adds a bit of status. It doesn't do anything on its own; tapping the badge is tapping the thing it sits on.

There are three kinds, and it's worth naming them correctly in bug reports:

| Kind | Looks like | Means | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Count badge** | Small pill with a number | *How many* new things | Chats **3**, cart **2** |
| **Dot badge** | Small dot, no number | *Something* new, or a presence state | Dot on the bell, green dot on an avatar |
| **Status label** (pill, tag) | Word in a colored pill | A *property* of the item | **New**, **Sale −20%**, **Sold out**, **In transit** |

Count and dot badges are about **attention** and change all the time. Status labels describe the item and change with its data. Both are <Term id="ui-state" /> that has to match the real data behind it.

### Where badges sit

- **On icons in tab bars and navigation bars**: the top-right corner of the icon, overlapping it slightly, never covering it. See [Navigation Bar](/components/navigation-bar#badges-are-counts-not-decorations) and [Tabs](/components/tabs#badges-stay-current).
- **Inside a tab or list row**: a counter next to the label (*Inbox 24*, *Unread 3*). Here it's part of the text, not floating on top.
- **On avatars**: a presence dot (online, away) in the bottom-right corner, with a ring in the background color so it stands out from the photo.
- **On cards and product tiles**: a status label in a corner or next to the price, like the "Sold out" label on the [Card](/components/card) page.
- **On the app icon**: drawn by the operating system, not by the app. More on that below.

### The rules for counts

<Steps>

1. **Zero hides the badge.** "0" means "nothing new", which is exactly what *no badge* says. A red "0" is a bug.
2. **Big numbers are capped.** Exact counts above a limit don't help anyone and break the layout. iOS apps usually show **99+**; Material 3 allows at most four characters, so **999+**.
3. **The number is the truth.** It equals the number of unread (or new) things, not "how many arrived since you last looked", unless the spec says otherwise.
4. **It updates when the truth changes.** Reading a message, marking all as read, reading it on another device, deleting it: the badge follows, without a restart.
5. **It has words for screen readers.** The visible "3" becomes "Chats, 3 unread" in the accessible name.

</Steps>

### When does the count update?

Ask this before you test, because there are two valid designs:

- **Realtime**: the server pushes changes (a <Term id="push-notification" />, a websocket) and the badge updates while you look at the screen. Typical for chat.
- **On open or refresh**: the app fetches counts when it starts, comes back from the background or the user pulls to refresh. Typical for shops and news.

Either way, the badge must be right **after every way of reading**: opening the item, "Mark all as read", swiping it away, reading it on the web, and coming back from the background ([Background and foreground](/mobile/background-and-foreground)). And when the user reads something, the badge should drop **immediately**, without waiting for the next sync.

### App icon badges (iOS and Android)

The number or dot on the **app icon** is drawn by the system, so it follows the system's rules:

- **iOS** shows a red number on the icon. The app can only set it if the user allowed **notifications with badges**; the user can switch badges off per app in *Settings › Notifications*. The app (or its server, via the push payload) is responsible for setting it back to zero. A stuck icon badge is one of the most common badge bugs.
- **Android** (8.0 and newer) shows **notification dots**: a dot appears while the app has active notifications, and long-pressing the icon shows them. Some launchers (Samsung, for example) can show a number instead. The dot disappears when the notifications are dismissed, so test dismissing them, not only opening the app. On Android 13+, notifications need a runtime permission, so no permission means no dot. See [Push Notifications](/features/push-notifications).
- **Web**: installed web apps can set an icon badge where the browser and OS support it. Websites often put the count in the page title instead: *(3) Inbox*.
  <DevNote>Icon badges use the Badging API (`navigator.setAppBadge()`).</DevNote>

## When to use it

- To show that **new or unread things** are waiting somewhere else: messages, notifications, order updates.
- To show **how many items** are in a container the user cares about: cart, downloads queue.
- To show a short **status** that helps users scan a list: *Sale*, *New*, *Sold out*, *Delivered*.
- To show **presence**: online, away, busy.

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Zero shown** | A red "0" instead of no badge | Low–Medium |
| 2 | **No cap** | "1000" or "12345" overflows the tab, overlaps the next icon or is cut off | Medium |
| 3 | **Stale count** | Badge doesn't drop after reading, only after restarting the app | Medium |
| 4 | **Wrong count** | Counts messages instead of chats (or the other way round), counts read items, counts twice | Medium |
| 5 | **Stuck app icon badge** | The icon shows 12 even though everything is read | Medium |
| 6 | **Count not spoken** | Screen reader says "Chats, tab" but not "3 unread" | High (for affected users) |
| 7 | **Color-only status** | Sale and New differ only by red vs green | Medium |
| 8 | **Badge hides the anchor** | With large text or on a small screen the badge covers the icon | Medium |
| 9 | **Low contrast** | Light text on a bright red or a pastel pill; invisible in <Term id="dark-mode" /> | Low–Medium |
| 10 | **Inconsistent counts** | Tab says 3, the list shows 2 unread, the app icon says 5 | Medium |
| 11 | **Negative or nonsense values** | "-1" after deleting, "NaN", "undefined" | Medium |
| 12 | **Localization** | "99+" or "Sold out" not translated, or a long word like "Ausverkauft" truncated | Low–Medium |

<Callout type="bug" title="Count bugs hide in the edges">
Most count bugs only appear at **0**, at the **cap** (99 → 100) and when the count **changes while you watch**. That's [boundary value analysis](/fundamentals/boundary-value-analysis) applied to a red dot: always test 0, 1, the cap, cap + 1 and a very large number, and test every way the count can go **down**.
</Callout>

## How to test it

### Checklist

<Checklist
  title="Badge test checklist"
  storageKey="badge"
  items={[
    'The count equals the real number of unread or new items, as defined in the spec (chats vs messages)',

## Accessibility

- **The count belongs in the accessible name.** A visual badge is usually hidden from assistive tech, so the element itself must say it: "Chats, 3 unread", "Cart, 2 items". This is WCAG 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value (Level A). Test with a <Term id="screen-reader" />.
  <DevNote>The badge is hidden with `aria-hidden="true"`. On the web, use `aria-label` on the button or visually hidden text; on iOS the `accessibilityValue` or label; on Android the `contentDescription` or state description.</DevNote>
- **Say what's capped.** If the badge shows "99+", the name can say "more than 99 unread". Never just "99 plus".
- **Changing counts.** Users don't need every change announced, but if a count change matters (an item added to the cart), a polite announcement helps (WCAG 4.1.3 Status Messages, Level AA). See <Term id="aria" />.
- **Not color alone.** Status labels need text; presence dots need a text equivalent ("Ana, online"). WCAG 1.4.1 Use of Color (Level A).
- **Contrast.** The number in the badge needs 4.5:1 against the badge color, and the badge 3:1 against what's behind it (WCAG 1.4.3 / 1.4.11). White on a bright red often lands around 3.5:1, so measure it with a <Term id="contrast-ratio" /> checker, in light and in dark mode.
- **Text size.** At the largest <Term id="dynamic-type" /> or font scale, the badge must stay readable and must not hide its icon or the neighbor.
- **<Term id="touch-target">Touch targets</Term>.** The badge isn't a separate target; the element it sits on is, and it needs at least 44×44 pt on iOS and 48×48 dp on Android. On the web, WCAG 2.5.8 (AA) asks for at least 24×24 CSS px; 44×44 px (2.5.5, AAA) is the comfortable target.

## Mobile vs web

<Tabs labels={['iOS', 'Android', 'Web']}>
<Tab>

- Tab bar badges: red pill with white text by default.
  <DevNote>`badgeValue` on `UITabBarItem` (a string; `"0"` shows a zero) or `.badge()` in SwiftUI.</DevNote>
- App icon badges need notification permission **with badges**. Test: allow, deny, and turning Badges off later in *Settings › Notifications*.
- Push payloads can set the icon badge. Check it's correct after a push arrives while the app is closed, and that it clears when the app is opened and the items are read.
- Test with **VoiceOver** and the largest **Dynamic Type** size (long-press a tab bar item to see the large content viewer).

</Tab>
<Tab>

- Material 3 badges: a **6 dp** dot or a **16 dp** badge with up to four characters ("999+").
  <DevNote>`Badge` / `BadgedBox` (Compose) or `BadgeDrawable` (Views).</DevNote>
- App icons show **notification dots** on most launchers; the dot follows the app's active notifications. Test dismissing notifications, and the Notification dot setting in system settings.
- Android 13+ asks for notification permission; without it there are no notifications and no dots ([App permissions](/features/app-permissions)).
- Test with **TalkBack**, a large **font size** and **display size**, and on a narrow phone in landscape.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- Badges are usually custom `<span>`s. Check the button's accessible name in DevTools' **Accessibility** pane: does it include the count?
- The page title count *(3) Inbox* and installed web apps' icon badges (where supported) must match the in-page badge.
  <DevNote>Icon badges are set with `navigator.setAppBadge()`.</DevNote>
- Test at **200% zoom** and narrow viewports: header badges often overlap the logo or the next icon ([Responsive design testing](/web/responsive-design-testing)).
- Check realtime updates in two browser tabs: read in one, watch the other.

</Tab>

<KeyTakeaways>

- Three kinds: **count badges**, **dot badges** and **status labels**. Name them correctly in bug reports.
- **Zero hides** the badge, **big numbers are capped** (99+, 999+), and the badge stays inside its space without hiding the icon.
- The count is **data**: it must match the list, update immediately when items are read, and agree with the app icon badge.
- App icon badges belong to the **OS**: on iOS they need notification permission with badges; on Android they're usually dots tied to active notifications.
- **Accessibility**: the count goes into the accessible name ("Chats, 3 unread"), status uses **words, not only color**, and contrast is checked in light and dark mode.

</KeyTakeaways>
