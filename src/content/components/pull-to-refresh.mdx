You're waiting for a refund. You open your banking app, pull the transactions list down, the spinner turns... and turns... and turns. Is the bank down? Is your internet down? Did the refund arrive? Nothing tells you. You close the app, open it again, and there it is: the refund was in the response all along, but the spinner never stopped.

Pull to refresh is one of the smallest components in an app, and one of the most used. It's also full of **timing** bugs: double requests, endless spinners, duplicate items and lists that jump. Let's learn it properly.

## What is pull to refresh?

Pull to refresh lets people **reload a list by dragging it down when it's already scrolled to the top**, then letting go. A spinner shows while new data loads, and fresh items appear at the top.

You'll find it in:

- **Feeds:** social posts, news, friends' workouts.
- **Inboxes:** mail, messages, notifications.
- **Time-sorted lists:** bank transactions, orders and deliveries, flight status.

It's a **shortcut**. Good apps also refresh automatically (when you open the screen, when the app returns from the background) and offer a visible way to refresh. Pulling is what impatient people do when they think something new should be there.

### The four states

Every pull goes through the same states. Most bugs happen at the borders between them.

<States />

### Threshold and resistance

Two numbers decide how a pull feels:

- **Threshold:** how far you must pull before letting go triggers a refresh. Too low and it fires by accident while you scroll up; too high and people give up. Most apps need roughly 60–120 points of pull. The indicator usually "fills up" as you approach it, and many apps give a <Term id="haptic-feedback">haptic</Term> tick when you pass it.
- **Resistance:** the list moves **less** than your finger (often around half), so it feels like stretching a rubber band. Without resistance the list can be dragged halfway down the screen. With too much, the threshold feels unreachable.

One more rule matters: **the pull only counts if the gesture started at the top**. If you start scrolled down and flick back up to the top in one movement, that's scrolling, not a request to refresh.

### How it differs from similar patterns

| Pattern | What it does | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pull to refresh** | Loads **newer** items at the **top** on request | Feeds, inboxes, time-sorted lists |
| **Refresh button** | The same, without a gesture | Toolbars, desktop web, the accessible alternative |
| **Auto refresh / live updates** | New items arrive by themselves | Chats, live scores, order tracking |
| **"New posts" pill** | Tells you new items are waiting above | Long feeds where you've scrolled down |
| **<Term id="infinite-scroll" /> / Load more** | Loads **older** items at the **bottom** | Long lists, search results |

## When to use it

Pull to refresh is a good choice when:

- The list is sorted by **time, newest first**, so "new" has an obvious place: the top.
- Content changes **often** and people check back for updates: inbox, feed, order status.
- The data might be **stale** even after auto refresh (on a weak connection, after a long time in the background).

## Good and bad uses

<Compare>
<Do title="Feeds, inboxes, transactions">

A list of orders sorted by date. Pulling down checks for new deliveries and updates the statuses.

</Do>
<Dont title="Forms and editable screens">

A checkout form or a profile editor. A refresh would throw away what the user typed, and there's nothing "new" to load. Scrolling up at the top of a long form shouldn't risk that.

</Dont>
</Compare>

<Compare>
<Do title="Newest at the top">

A news list or a notification list, where new things appear above old ones.

</Do>
<Dont title="Newest at the bottom">

A chat conversation. New messages arrive at the **bottom** in real time, and pulling down at the top usually means "load **older** messages". Pull to refresh there would confuse both users and code.

</Dont>
</Compare>

<Compare>
<Do title="Simple vertical lists">

A list that scrolls vertically and has nothing else that reacts to a downward drag.

</Do>
<Dont title="Maps, canvases and sheets">

A map pans when you drag down. A [bottom sheet](/components/bottom-sheet) closes when you drag down. A drawing canvas draws. Adding pull to refresh to these makes gestures fight.

</Dont>
</Compare>

For every pull to refresh, ask: **"What exactly is reloaded, and what happens to what's already on screen?"** Then test the unhappy paths: slow, failing, offline, and pulling twice.

## How good pull to refresh behaves

### One request at a time

While a refresh is running, pulling again should **not** start a second request. The app either ignores the new pull or keeps showing the spinner. The same goes for a refresh while the screen is still doing its **first load**, or while it's loading the next page at the bottom. Two overlapping requests are the classic source of duplicates and of a <Term id="race-condition" /> where an older response overwrites a newer one.

### New items, no duplicates, no jumps

- When the user pulled from the top, the new items appear **at the top**, and the list shows them.
- Items that were already there are **not duplicated**. The app should merge by item ID, not just stick the response on top.
- Items that changed (an order went from "On the way" to "Delivered") update **in place**.
- The list doesn't **jump** to a random position. If an app auto-refreshes while you're reading further down, it should keep your place and show a "3 new posts" pill instead of moving the content under your thumb.

### Errors stop the spinner

When the request fails, the spinner **must stop**. The old content stays on screen, and the user gets a clear, polite <Term id="error-message" />: a [toast](/components/toast) or an inline banner like "Couldn't load new orders. Retry". An endless spinner is the worst outcome: the user can't tell whether to wait, retry or give up. Also check what happens on a <Term id="timeout" />: a request that never answers should end in an error after a reasonable time.

### Offline is not an error you wait for

When the device is offline (<Term id="airplane-mode" />, no signal, a <Term id="captive-portal" />), good apps answer **immediately**: "You're offline. Showing saved orders." They keep the cached list and don't pretend to load. See [Network conditions](/mobile/network-conditions) for how to simulate this.

### "Last updated" tells the truth

Many apps show **"Updated 9:41"** or **"Updated just now"** under the title or in the indicator. It must change after **every successful** refresh, and must **not** change after a failed one. A stale timestamp makes users distrust everything else on the screen.

### It plays nicely with other gestures

Pulling only starts at the top. Horizontal swipes on rows ([swipe actions](/components/swipe-list)), carousels inside the list, and the system's own gestures must still work. On the web, the app's pull to refresh must not fight the **browser's** pull to refresh.

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Endless spinner** | On an error or offline, the spinner never stops; no message | High |
| 2 | **Double request** | Pulling again during a refresh sends another request | Medium |
| 3 | **Duplicate items** | The same posts or transactions appear twice after refreshing | High |
| 4 | **List jumps** | After a refresh you're in the middle of the list, not at the new items | Medium |
| 5 | **Fires by accident** | Normal scrolling up to the top triggers a refresh (threshold too low or wrong start rule) | Medium |
| 6 | **Never fires** | The threshold is unreachable, or the pull doesn't work on short lists that don't scroll | Medium |
| 7 | **Stale "Updated" time** | Still says 8:15 after a successful refresh, or updates after a failed one | Low–Medium |
| 8 | **No alternative** | No refresh button or menu; keyboard, switch and voice users can't refresh | High (for affected users) |
| 9 | **Lost input** | A refresh wipes a half-typed comment or a filter the user set | Medium–High |
| 10 | **Browser reloads the page** | On a mobile website, pulling reloads the whole page instead of the list | Medium |

<Callout type="bug" title="Why #2 and #3 go together">
Each request asks the server "what's new since my last update?". If two requests start before either finishes, **both** ask with the same starting point and get the **same** new items. If the app simply adds every response to the top, you see everything twice. To catch it: make the network **slow** (or use a <Term id="proxy-breakpoint" /> to hold the response), pull, then pull again while the spinner is turning.
</Callout>

### Tools

- **Slow it down:** <Term id="network-throttling" /> (Network Link Conditioner on iOS, the emulator's network settings on Android, DevTools on the web) gives you time to pull twice.
- **Hold or break responses:** a <Term id="proxy-tool" /> like Charles or Proxyman can pause a response with a breakpoint, return a `500`, or add a long delay to check <Term id="timeout" /> handling. See [Proxy tools](/mobile/proxy-tools).
- **Go offline:** airplane mode on a real device, or **Offline** in Chrome DevTools' Network panel.
- **Watch the requests:** count them. One pull, one request. Check the query (`since=`, `page=`, cursor) to see whether two requests asked for the same thing.
- **Precise pulls on Android:** `adb shell input swipe 540 600 540 1100 400` drags down 500 px in 400 ms; change the distance to find the threshold. See [ADB basics](/mobile/adb-basics).

## Accessibility

Pull to refresh is a **dragging** gesture, so some people can't do it at all: people with motor impairments, people using Switch Control, Voice Control or a keyboard. Check these:

- **A visible alternative:** a Refresh button in the toolbar, a menu item, or automatic refresh when the screen opens. <Term id="wcag" /> 2.5.1 Pointer Gestures (Level A) and 2.5.7 Dragging Movements (Level AA) ask for a way to do gesture and drag actions with a simple tap or click instead.
- **Screen readers:** <Term id="screen-reader">VoiceOver</Term> users scroll with **three fingers**. With a standard iOS refresh control, a three-finger swipe down at the top of the list often triggers the refresh, but not reliably, and custom implementations often don't support it at all. TalkBack users need a Refresh button or action. Test the real thing, not the documentation.
- **Announce the state:** "Refreshing", then the result: "3 new stories" or "Couldn't refresh". The spinner needs an accessible label, and the result should be announced without moving focus.
- **Don't move focus:** when new items arrive at the top, focus should stay on the item the user was on.
- **Reduced motion:** with Reduce Motion on, the refresh should still work and be visible, just with less animation.
- **Keyboard (web):** the Refresh button is reachable with <kbd>Tab</kbd>, has a visible focus ring and a clear name ("Refresh orders", not just an icon).

<Tabs labels={['iOS', 'Android', 'Web']}>
<Tab>

- The spinner stays until the work finishes, so a request that never ends means a spinner that never ends.
  <DevNote>Built in: `UIRefreshControl` (UIKit) and `.refreshable` (SwiftUI). With `.refreshable`, the spinner stays until the async work finishes.</DevNote>
- The list moves down and the spinner sits in the gap; a haptic tick can mark the threshold.
- Test with VoiceOver's three-finger scroll, Voice Control and Reduce Motion.

</Tab>
<Tab>

<DevNote>Built in: `SwipeRefreshLayout` (Views) and `PullToRefreshBox` (Compose Material 3).</DevNote>

- A small circular indicator slides down **over** the list; the list itself usually stays put. Android 12+ also shows a "stretch" overscroll effect elsewhere.
- Check that the indicator doesn't clash with the top app bar or tabs, and that TalkBack users have a Refresh action.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- Mobile browsers have their **own** pull to refresh that reloads the whole page (Chrome on Android, Safari on iOS 15 and later).
- A web app with its own pull to refresh has to stop the browser's version. Support differs between browsers and versions, so test on a real iPhone and Android phone.
  <DevNote>It uses the CSS `overscroll-behavior` property (for example `overscroll-behavior-y: contain`).</DevNote>
- Desktop has no pull at all: the page needs a visible Refresh button (and browser reload must not lose the user's work).

<KeyTakeaways>

- Pull to refresh reloads **time-sorted lists** (feeds, inboxes, transactions); avoid it on forms, maps, sheets and bottom-anchored chats.
- It moves through **pulling → armed → refreshing → done (or error)**; the pull must start at the top and pass a deliberate **threshold**.
- Test the timing: **one request at a time**, **no duplicates**, **no jumps**, and a spinner that **always stops**, including on errors and offline.
- "Last updated" must tell the truth.
- Always provide a **visible Refresh** and announce the result for screen reader users.

</KeyTakeaways>
