You tap **Delete account** in a food delivery app and the screen dims. A small box asks *"Delete account?"* with **Cancel** and **Delete**. You can't scroll, you can't tap the menu, you have to decide. That box is a <Term id="modal">modal</Term>.

Modals look like the simplest thing in an app: a box with a title and two buttons. But they control **what the user is allowed to do**, they guard **destructive actions**, and they hold **unsaved data**. When they misbehave, people delete things by accident, lose what they typed, or get stuck. Let's learn them properly.

## What is a modal?

A modal is a **window that appears on top of the current screen and blocks interaction with everything behind it** until the user responds or dismisses it. The word comes from "mode": while it's open, the app is in a special mode where only the modal works.

You'll usually see one used to:

- **Confirm a destructive or important action**: *Delete 3 photos?*, *Cancel your subscription?*, *Discard changes?*
- **Ask for a quick decision**: *Allow notifications?*, *Choose a delivery time*.
- **Do a short, focused task without leaving the page**: edit a delivery address, add a card, write a review.
- **Tell the user something they must acknowledge**: *Your session expired. Please log in again.*

### How it differs from similar components

The key questions are: **does it block the screen?** and **does the user have to respond?** Using a blocking component for something that didn't need it is a valid usability finding.

| Component | Blocks the screen? | Needs a response? | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Alert dialog** | Yes | Yes | Short, urgent decision: "Delete account?" |
| **Dialog** | Yes | Usually | A small task or choice: pick a date, rename a file |
| **Full-screen dialog** | Yes (covers it) | Save or close | A longer task on a phone: new event, new address |
| **Modal bottom sheet / action sheet** | Yes | Pick an option or dismiss | A list of actions or options: Share, Sort by |
| **Popover / menu** | Partly (closes on outside tap) | No | Extra options next to a button |
| **<Term id="toast">Toast / snackbar</Term>** | No | No (optional action) | Brief feedback: "Message archived · Undo" |
| **Banner / inline message** | No | No (optional action) | Persistent status or warning on the page |

<Callout type="dev" title="Platform names">
Apple calls the small centered one an **alert** and the bottom list an **action sheet**; larger ones are **sheets**. Google's Material Design calls them **dialogs** (basic and full-screen) and **bottom sheets**. On the web you'll hear **modal**, **dialog**, **popup** and **lightbox**. In bug reports, use the name your team uses, and say which kind it is.
</Callout>

## When to use it

A modal is a good choice when:

- The user is about to do something **destructive or hard to undo** and must confirm it.
- The app **can't continue without an answer** (permission, expired session, required choice).
- The task is **short and focused**, and leaving the current page would lose the user's context.
- The information is **critical** and must not be missed.

## How a good modal behaves

Most modal bugs aren't about how it looks. They're about **behavior**: which button does what, how you get out, and where focus and scrolling go. These are the rules to test against.

### Destructive confirmations

- **The title is a specific question** with the action in it: *Delete "Lisbon 2025"?*, not *Are you sure?*
- **The confirm button repeats the verb**: *Delete*, *Remove*, *Cancel order*. Not *OK* or *Yes*.
- **Destructive actions look destructive**: red text (iOS, Material) or a red button (web).
- **Button order follows the platform.** iOS two-button alerts put Cancel on the left and the destructive action on the right. Material puts the dismissive action left and the confirming action right. On the web, follow the design system and be **consistent across the whole product**.
- **The safe option is the default.** Pressing <kbd>Enter</kbd> or having initial focus should never trigger *Delete* in a destructive dialog. Focus the least destructive button.
- **Confirming happens once.** The button shows progress and ignores extra taps (a <Term id="double-submit">double submit</Term> can delete twice or charge twice).

### Every way to close a modal

| Exit | iOS | Android | Web | What must happen |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Cancel / Close button** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | Closes, changes nothing |
| **X icon** | Sheets, full-screen | Full-screen dialogs | Very common | Same as Cancel |
| **Tap outside (scrim)** | Sheets yes, alerts **no** | Yes by default | Usually | Same as Cancel, or ignored. **Never** confirm |
| **Esc key** | With a hardware keyboard | With a hardware keyboard | ✓ | Same as Cancel |
| **System Back** | — | ✓ (button or gesture) | Browser Back: team decision | Closes the dialog first, not the screen behind |
| **Swipe down** | Sheets | Bottom sheets | Mobile web sheets | Same as Cancel |

<Callout type="warning" title="The golden rule">
Every exit except the confirm button means **"cancel"**. If the modal holds unsaved input (a form, a review, an edited photo), a stray tap outside or a swipe must **not** silently throw it away: ignore it or ask *"Discard changes?"* first.
</Callout>

### Focus: in, trapped, and back

Keyboard and <Term id="screen-reader" /> users can't "see" the dim layer. They rely on focus:

<Steps>

1. **Focus moves in** when the modal opens, to the first field or the safest button, so the screen reader starts reading the dialog.
2. **Focus is trapped inside.** <kbd>Tab</kbd> and <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Tab</kbd> cycle through the modal's controls only. This is a <Term id="focus-trap" />. If focus escapes, users end up on invisible buttons behind the scrim.
3. **Focus returns** to the element that opened the modal when it closes. Otherwise keyboard users are thrown back to the top of the page.

</Steps>

### Scroll lock

While a modal is open, the page behind it should **not scroll**. Test by scrolling with a mouse wheel, a trackpad and a finger **over the dialog and over the scrim**. A classic bug on iOS Safari: the page behind scrolls when you swipe on the dim area. If the modal content is long, the **modal itself** should scroll, with its buttons still reachable.

### Nested modals

A modal opening another modal (*Edit address* → *Delete this address?*) is a warning sign in design. If it exists, test it hard: Esc and Back should close **only the top one**, focus should return to the dialog underneath (not the page), and the scrims shouldn't stack into a black screen.

### Small screens and the open keyboard

On a phone, a centered dialog with a form often breaks when the keyboard appears: the **Save button ends up behind the keyboard**, or the dialog is taller than the screen and can't scroll. Test on the smallest supported phone, in landscape, with the largest text size, and with the keyboard open. Good apps switch to a **full-screen dialog** or a sheet on small screens.

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Outside tap confirms or loses data** | Tapping the dim area deletes, submits, or throws away typed text | High |
| 2 | **Can't close it** | Esc, Android Back or swipe-down do nothing; the user is stuck | Medium–High |
| 3 | **Background still works** | Taps go through the scrim, or the page behind scrolls | Medium |
| 4 | **Focus escapes** | Tab reaches links behind the modal; screen reader reads the page, not the dialog | High (for affected users) |
| 5 | **Focus not returned** | After closing, focus jumps to the top of the page | Medium |
| 6 | **Double confirm** | Fast double tap fires two requests: two orders, two deletions, an error after success | High |
| 7 | **Doesn't fit the screen** | Long text or large fonts push the buttons off-screen with no scrolling | High |
| 8 | **Hidden by the keyboard** | Save or Continue is behind the on-screen keyboard | Medium |
| 9 | **Reopens or stacks** | The modal comes back after closing, or opens twice on a double tap | Medium |
| 10 | **Wrong or vague buttons** | "OK / Cancel" for a delete, destructive button not red, safe option not default | Medium |

<Callout type="bug" title="Why #6 happens">
The confirm button stays enabled while the request is in flight. The first request succeeds, the second finds nothing to delete and fails, and the app shows the **last** response: an error. The user tries again, panics, or contacts support. Expected: disable the button (or ignore repeat taps) until the first request finishes. Servers should also be <Term id="idempotency">idempotent</Term> where possible.
</Callout>

## Accessibility

- **Role and name.** The dialog is announced as a dialog, with its title. Native iOS and Android dialogs get this for free; custom ones often don't.
  <DevNote>On the web, use the native `<dialog>` element with `showModal()`, or `role="dialog"` (`role="alertdialog"` for urgent confirmations) plus `aria-modal="true"` and `aria-labelledby` pointing to the title.</DevNote>
- **Focus management.** Move focus in on open, trap it inside (<Term id="focus-trap" />), and return it to the trigger on close. Make the page behind inert so screen readers can't wander there.
- **Esc closes** on the web, and the **Back gesture** closes on Android. A visible Close or Cancel button must always exist: not everyone can tap outside or press Esc.
- **Contrast.** Text needs 4.5:1, button shapes need 3:1 against their background, and the dialog must stand out from the page (an edge with 3:1 contrast, or a clear scrim). Red destructive text must still reach 4.5:1.
- **<Term id="touch-target">Touch targets</Term>** of at least 44×44 pt (iOS) / 48×48 dp (Android), including the X icon, which is often too small.
- **Large text.** Content must scroll rather than clip at the biggest <Term id="dynamic-type" /> and Android font sizes.
- **Don't rely on color alone** to mark the destructive action: use the verb in the label too.

## Mobile vs web

<Tabs labels={['iOS', 'Android', 'Web']}>
<Tab>

- **Alerts**: centered, title + message + up to two side-by-side buttons (more stack vertically). Tapping outside does **not** close them.
- **Action sheets**: slide up from the bottom with a separate Cancel; on iPad they appear as a popover next to the button.
- **Sheets**: cards that slide up, can have detents (medium / large) and close with a **swipe down**. Test swipe-down with unsaved data: apps can block it and ask first.
  <DevNote>Alerts: `UIAlertController` / SwiftUI `.alert`. Action sheets: `.confirmationDialog`. Sheets: `.sheet`.</DevNote>
- Test with **VoiceOver**, **Larger Text**, iPhone SE size, landscape, and a hardware keyboard on iPad (Esc / ⌘ + .).

</Tab>
<Tab>

- Material 3 **basic dialogs** (rounded 28 dp, text buttons on the right) and **full-screen dialogs** (X, title, Save) for longer tasks on phones.
- **Back** (button or edge-swipe gesture) must close the dialog first. By default, tapping outside closes a dialog too, but developers can turn that off.
- **Modal bottom sheets** have a drag handle and close with a swipe down.
- Watch for **rotation**: a dialog can disappear, duplicate or lose its input when the activity is recreated.
- Test with **TalkBack**, maximum **font size** and **display size**, and small devices.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- Custom `div` modals often get focus and scroll wrong.
  <DevNote>Prefer the native `<dialog>` with `showModal()`: it gives Esc handling, top-layer rendering and an inert background.</DevNote>
- Check **Esc**, **click outside**, the **X**, and focus with <kbd>Tab</kbd>. Inspect roles and `aria-modal` in <Term id="devtools" />.
- Test **scroll lock** with wheel, trackpad and touch, especially on iOS Safari.
- Test **responsive** behavior: at 360 px wide and with the mobile keyboard open, can you still reach every button?
- Decide what **browser Back** does with an open modal. Either way, it shouldn't lose the user's data.

<KeyTakeaways>

- A modal **blocks the screen until the user responds**. Use it for decisions and destructive actions, not for news or success messages.
- Destructive dialogs need a **specific title**, a **verb on the confirm button**, **destructive styling** and the **safe option as default**.
- Test **every exit**: buttons, X, tap outside, Esc, System Back, swipe down. All of them mean *cancel*, and none may silently lose typed data.
- While open: **background blocked and not scrolling**, **focus trapped**, and **focus returned** on close.
- Classic bugs: **double confirm**, **dialogs that don't fit** small screens, large text or the keyboard, and **dialogs that reopen**.

</KeyTakeaways>
