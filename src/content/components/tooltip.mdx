You're sending rent from your banking app on your phone. Next to **IBAN** there's a little ⓘ. You tap it. Nothing. You tap it again, harder. Still nothing. On the laptop the same ⓘ shows a helpful bubble, but only when the mouse is over it. The developer tested with a mouse, and never noticed that **phones don't have one**.

Tooltips look like the most harmless component on the page: a few words in a dark bubble. But they depend on **how** the user points at things (mouse, finger, keyboard, screen reader), they float over other content, and they love to hide behind screen edges. That makes them a great place to find bugs.

## What is a tooltip?

A tooltip is a **small, temporary bubble of plain text that describes the element it's attached to**. It appears when the user hovers over the element with a mouse, moves keyboard focus to it, or (on touch screens) long-presses it. It disappears when they move away.

You'll usually see tooltips used to:

- **Name icon-only buttons**: *Archive*, *Share*, *Add to wishlist*.
- **Show keyboard shortcuts**: *Bold (Ctrl+B)*.
- **Add a short hint** next to a form label: *"Shown on the recipient's statement"*.
- **Show the full text** of something that is cut off, like a long file name.

### Tooltip, toggletip, toast or inline help?

Several components show "a bit of extra text". Picking the wrong one is one of the most common tooltip problems.

| Pattern | Opens when | Content | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Tooltip** | Hover, focus, long-press | A few words of plain text | Naming icons, shortcuts |
| **Toggletip / popover** | Click or tap | Text, links, buttons | Explanations, "Learn more" |
| **[Toast](/components/toast)** | The app shows it after an action | A status message, maybe "Undo" | "Transfer sent" |
| **Inline help** | Always visible | Text under a field | Rules users need to succeed |
| **Coachmark** | Once, during onboarding | Title, text, close button | Pointing at a new feature |

<Callout type="info" title="The names are messy">
Designers say *tooltip* for almost any bubble. Material Design has **plain tooltips** (short text) and **rich tooltips** (title, text and an optional action; the ones with an action usually open on a tap and stay open). Accessibility experts call a click-to-open info bubble a **toggletip**. Apple's TipKit has **popover tips**. Don't argue about the name; test what it **does**: how it opens, what it contains, and how it closes.
</Callout>

### Hover, focus, long-press: touch screens have no hover

A tooltip has to work for **every way** people point at things. This is where most tooltip bugs come from.

| Input | How the tooltip should open | How it should close |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mouse / trackpad** | Hover, after a short delay | Pointer leaves (trigger and tooltip), or <kbd>Esc</kbd> |
| **Keyboard** | <kbd>Tab</kbd> focus, immediately | Focus moves on, or <kbd>Esc</kbd> |
| **Touch (Android)** | Long-press the icon button | Shortly after the finger lifts |
| **Touch (iPhone, web)** | Usually not at all, so don't hide anything important in it | n/a |
| **Screen reader** | Never "opens"; the text is read as the name or description | n/a |

The key lesson: **on a phone, a hover-only tooltip doesn't exist**. A tap is not a hover. If the information matters on mobile, it must be visible, or opened with a tap as a toggletip.

### Delay and dismissal: WCAG 1.4.13

Mouse tooltips usually wait a few hundred milliseconds before appearing, so they don't flash while the pointer crosses the page. Keyboard focus should show them **right away**.

Once a tooltip is visible, <Term id="wcag" /> success criterion **1.4.13 Content on Hover or Focus** (level AA) asks for three things:

<Steps>

1. **Dismissible.** The user can hide it without moving the pointer or focus, usually with <kbd>Esc</kbd>. A tooltip that covers the next field must not force the user to move away.
2. **Hoverable.** The user can move the pointer **onto** the tooltip without it disappearing. People who use screen magnifiers need this to read it.
3. **Persistent.** It stays until the user moves away, dismisses it, or it's no longer relevant. No timer that hides it after two seconds while the pointer is still there.

</Steps>

### Positioning and screen edges

A tooltip normally appears **above** (or below) its trigger, centered. Near an edge there is no room, so a good tooltip:

- **Shifts** sideways to stay fully on screen, while its arrow keeps pointing at the trigger.
- **Flips** to the other side when there's no room above (for example, under a toolbar at the top of the page).
- Is **not clipped** by a parent with hidden overflow, a scrolling list, the notch or the <Term id="safe-area" />.

Test the **first and last** icon in every toolbar, at the narrowest <Term id="viewport" /> and with long translations.

### Plain text only

A tooltip disappears when the pointer or focus leaves the trigger, so anything **interactive** inside it is a trap: a mouse user has to "chase" the link, a keyboard user can't Tab into it, and a touch user never sees it. If the bubble needs a **link or a button**, it should be a **toggletip or popover** that opens on click and stays open.

### Icon buttons still need a name

A tooltip that says "Delete" **looks** like the button's name, but a <Term id="screen-reader" /> only uses it if the code connects them. An icon button needs an **accessible name** of its own. Otherwise it's announced as just **"button"**. The tooltip is the visual version of that name, not a replacement for it.

<DevNote>`aria-label="Delete"` on the web, `accessibilityLabel` on iOS, `contentDescription` on Android.</DevNote>

### Onboarding coachmarks

Coachmarks (or "tips") point at a feature the first time a user sees a screen: *"Tap here to start a new note"*. They're related to tooltips, but appear without hovering. Test that they:

- Have a clear **close** button (with a name) and close with Back / <kbd>Esc</kbd>.
- **Don't come back** after being dismissed, after a restart or an update (unless that's the plan).
- **Point at the right thing**, on every screen size and in landscape.
- Don't **block** the very button they're pointing at, and don't stack three on top of each other.

## When to use it

Tooltips are a good choice when:

- You need to **name an icon-only button** for mouse and keyboard users (and the button already has an accessible name).
- The hint is **nice to know**, not need to know: the user can succeed without ever seeing it.
- The text is **short**: a few words, one short sentence at most.
- The product is used with a **mouse or keyboard** (desktop web, tablets with a pointer), or on Android, where long-press shows it.

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Hover only** | Nothing on Tab, nothing on tap | Medium–High |
| 2 | **Clipped at the edge** | Half the text is cut off near the screen edge | Medium |
| 3 | **Not hoverable** | It vanishes when the pointer moves towards it | Medium (fails WCAG) |
| 4 | **Can't dismiss** | <kbd>Esc</kbd> does nothing; it covers the next field | Medium (fails WCAG) |
| 5 | **Interactive content** | A "Learn more" link that can't be clicked or reached | Medium |
| 6 | **Icon button with no name** | Screen reader says just "button" | High (for affected users) |
| 7 | **Sticky tooltip** | Stays on screen after scrolling, closing a dialog or tapping elsewhere | Low–Medium |
| 8 | **Flicker** | Tooltips flash on and off while the mouse crosses a toolbar | Low |
| 9 | **Wrong or stale text** | "Mark as read" on a message that's already read | Medium |
| 10 | **Coachmark returns** | A dismissed onboarding tip shows up again after every restart | Low–Medium |

<Callout type="bug" title="Why #1 is so common">
Many web tooltips are built with only `mouseenter` / `mouseleave` events, or with the HTML **`title` attribute**. The `title` tooltip only appears on mouse hover after a delay: not on keyboard focus, not on touch, and screen readers don't reliably read it. If a tooltip works on your laptop, **pick up your phone** and press <kbd>Tab</kbd> before you mark the test as passed.
</Callout>

### Tools

- **[DevTools](/web/devtools-for-testers) › Elements:** right-click › *Force state* › `:hover` or `:focus` keeps CSS tooltips open while you inspect them. For JavaScript tooltips, use *Emulate a focused page* (Rendering panel) so they don't close when you click into DevTools.
- **Accessibility pane:** shows each button's computed **Name** and **Description**. An empty name on an icon button is bug #6.
- **Device toolbar:** test at 360 px wide and with touch emulation. Remember that emulation is not a real finger: confirm touch behavior on a <Term id="real-device" />.
- **Screen readers:** VoiceOver (Mac and iPhone), TalkBack (Android), NVDA (Windows, free).

## Accessibility

- **Name first.** Every icon-only button needs an accessible name.
  <DevNote>On the web that's `aria-label` (or a tooltip linked with `aria-labelledby`). On iOS it's `accessibilityLabel`; on Android, `contentDescription`. For Android toolbar menu items, the item's title usually serves as both the spoken name and the long-press tooltip.</DevNote>
- **Link extra text as a description.** A screen reader reads a tooltip that adds information after the name.
  <DevNote>The tooltip gets `role="tooltip"` and is referenced with `aria-describedby` from the trigger.</DevNote>
- **Never rely on `title`.** The native `title` tooltip doesn't show on keyboard focus or touch, can't be styled, and screen readers handle it inconsistently.
- **<Term id="keyboard-navigation">Keyboard</Term>.** The trigger must be focusable; focus shows the tooltip, <kbd>Esc</kbd> hides it, and focus never moves **into** a tooltip. If users need to Tab into it, it's a popover.
- **WCAG 1.4.13.** Dismissible, hoverable, persistent (see above). This is a level AA criterion, so most accessibility audits check it.
- **Contrast and size.** Tooltip text needs 4.5:1 contrast against its bubble, and must still fit at 200% zoom and the largest text size.

## Mobile vs web

<Tabs labels={['iOS', 'Android', 'Web']}>
<Tab>

- iPhone apps rarely use tooltips: there's no hover, and Apple's guidelines prefer **clear labels** and **standard icons**. Icon buttons still need a label for VoiceOver.
  <DevNote>That's `accessibilityLabel`.</DevNote>
- On the **Mac** (including Mac Catalyst apps), apps can show a hover tooltip; on **iPad with a trackpad or mouse**, check what the app's iPadOS version actually shows.
  <DevNote>SwiftUI's `.help("…")` shows the hover tooltip.</DevNote>
- For feature hints, apps use **TipKit** (iOS 17+): popover tips and inline tips. Test that they close, stay closed, and don't cover what they point at.
- Larger hints usually live in a **popover** or a [bottom sheet](/components/bottom-sheet).

</Tab>
<Tab>

- Android has built-in tooltips. They appear on **long-press** and on **mouse hover**.
  <DevNote>`android:tooltipText`, `TooltipCompat`; `TooltipBox` with `PlainTooltip` / `RichTooltip` in Compose.</DevNote>
- Action bar / toolbar icons show their title as a tooltip on long-press. Check that the long-press doesn't also **trigger the action**.
- Material 3 **rich tooltips** can include a title and an action; persistent ones stay open until dismissed. Test them like popovers.
- Test with **TalkBack**: icon buttons must have a description.
  <DevNote>That's `contentDescription`.</DevNote>

</Tab>
<Tab>

- Test hover, focus **and** touch. Use real phones; mobile browsers have no hover, and taps can "stick" a hover state.
- Check **edges**, scrolling containers and zoom: the tooltip must stay visible and move with its trigger.
- Inspect the markup: `role="tooltip"`, `aria-describedby` or `aria-labelledby`, no interactive content, no reliance on `title`.
  <DevNote>Newer HTML features like the **Popover API** (`popover` attribute) help build toggletips and popovers that open on click and close with <kbd>Esc</kbd>.</DevNote>

</Tab>

<KeyTakeaways>

- A tooltip is a **short, plain-text** hint that appears on **hover, keyboard focus or long-press**. Links and buttons belong in a toggletip or popover.
- **Touch screens have no hover.** Never hide need-to-know information in a hover-only tooltip.
- WCAG 1.4.13: tooltips must be **dismissible** (<kbd>Esc</kbd>), **hoverable** and **persistent**.
- Test the **edges**: tooltips must shift or flip, never get clipped.
- Every icon button needs its **own accessible name**. The tooltip is just the visible version of it.

</KeyTakeaways>
