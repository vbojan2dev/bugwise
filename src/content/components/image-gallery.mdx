You're flat hunting. You scroll down to the photos of a promising listing, filter to **Outside** and tap the balcony. The app shows you the living room instead. You close it, and you're back at the very top of the page. You scroll down again, open the bedroom, zoom in on the wardrobe, tap **Next**, and the bathroom appears already zoomed into a corner. Meanwhile your data plan has quietly paid for 40 MB of photos you only saw as tiny squares.

An image gallery looks simple: a grid of small pictures, and a big picture when you tap one. But it combines **image loading**, **gestures**, a **modal** viewer, **focus management** and **state** (which photo, which filter, how far you scrolled). Every one of those can break, and users notice right away.

## What is it?

An image gallery has two parts:

- A **thumbnail grid**: small, usually cropped previews of many photos (and sometimes videos).
- A **viewer** (often called a **lightbox**): a full-screen layer that shows one photo at a time, **whole and sharp**. You swipe between photos, zoom in, and close it to get back to the grid.

You meet galleries in property and hotel listings, product pages, social profiles, chat apps ("Media, links and docs"), review sections and of course the phone's own Photos app.

### How it differs from a carousel

A [carousel](/components/carousel) shows slides **in the page**, one at a time, often with autoplay. A gallery shows **many thumbnails at once** and opens them in a separate **viewer**. Many product pages combine both: a small swipeable carousel on the page, and a tap on it opens the full-screen viewer described here. Uploading photos is a different story too: see [Image upload](/features/image-upload) for cameras, permissions, HEIC and EXIF rotation.

| | Carousel | Image gallery |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shows | One slide in the page | Many thumbnails, then one photo full screen |
| Moves by itself | Sometimes (autoplay) | Never |
| Typical content | Banners, product photos | Listing photos, albums, user media |
| Main risks | Autoplay, gestures, dots | Loading, zoom, closing, focus |

## When to use it

- Users want to **browse many photos** of the same thing and pick which ones to look at: a flat, a hotel room, a used car, a restaurant's dishes.
- Photos need to be seen **large and in detail** (zoom into a floor plan, a scratch on a used phone, the fabric of a jacket).
- Users collect media themselves: albums, chat media, a profile's posts.

## How good galleries behave

### Small thumbnails, sharp viewer

A camera photo is often **4000 × 3000 px and 3–5 MB**. A thumbnail on a phone is about 120 CSS px wide. Loading the original for every thumbnail is one of the most common gallery bugs: 12 photos are **40 MB**, slow on a train and expensive on a data plan.

Good galleries load **two versions**:

- **Thumbnails** in the grid: small files (roughly 15–60 KB), sized for the grid cell and the screen's pixel density.
- **A large version** in the viewer, loaded **when the photo is opened**, plus the next one in advance (preloading) so swiping feels instant. Not the 4000 px original either: around the screen width is enough.

On the web this is done with `srcset` and `sizes`: the page offers several widths of the same photo, says how wide it will be shown, and the browser picks the smallest file that is still sharp. Thumbnails further down use <Term id="lazy-loading" /> (`loading="lazy"`) so they load only when you scroll near them. Apps do the same with image libraries and server-side resizing. Try it:

<DevNote>Kingfisher or SDWebImage on iOS, Coil or Glide on Android.</DevNote>

<SrcsetLab client:visible />

Things to check: count the image requests and **total MB** in the network log when the gallery opens; check that the **viewer** image is sharp on a high-density phone (not the thumbnail stretched up); and that images also have `width`/`height` (or a fixed aspect ratio box), so the page doesn't jump while they load.

### Crop in the grid, whole photo in the viewer

Grid cells are usually all the same shape, so photos are **cropped to fill** them. That's fine for a preview, but it means portraits lose their top and bottom, and a **panorama** loses most of its width. In the variants above, compare the balcony panorama in the square grid (the boat on the right is gone) with the justified rows.

<DevNote>CSS `object-fit: cover`.</DevNote>

The **viewer must show the whole photo**, never cropped: black or blurred bars around it are expected. Also check that photos are **not stretched** (wrong aspect ratio), that **portrait photos aren't sideways** (EXIF orientation, see [Image upload](/features/image-upload)), and that the crop never cuts off what the photo is *about*.

<DevNote>CSS `object-fit: contain`.</DevNote>

### Swiping between photos

- **Swipe** left and right on touch, **drag** or **arrow buttons** with a mouse, **arrow keys** on a keyboard.
- The **ends are clear**: on the first photo, Previous is disabled; on the last, Next is disabled, and a swipe past the end **bounces back** (rubber band). It never shows an empty slide. Some viewers loop from the last photo to the first: fine, if it's seamless and the counter says so.
- Only **horizontal** swipes change the photo. A swipe that is mostly vertical belongs to "swipe down to close" (or does nothing), and a small wobble is not a swipe (the <Term id="touch-slop" />).
- A **video** in the gallery doesn't start playing while you swipe past it, and stops when you swipe away.

### Zoom and pan

- **Pinch** with two fingers, and **double-tap** (double-click on the web) to zoom into that spot; double-tap again to zoom back out. On the web, a viewer should also offer **zoom buttons** and keys.
- **Limits**: zoom stops at a sensible maximum (often 3–5×) and can't go smaller than "fit to screen". While zoomed, a one-finger drag **pans** the photo, but only **up to its edges**: you should never pan into empty black space.
- **Zoom resets** when you move to another photo. If photo 4 opens already zoomed into a corner because you zoomed photo 3, that's a bug.
- While zoomed, a drag pans the photo instead of swiping to the next one. Many apps switch to the next photo only when you're already at the edge of the zoomed photo, so check that at the edges too.

### Closing and coming back

Users close a viewer in many ways, and every one must work and land in the **same place**:

- The **X** button (always visible, at least 44×44 pt on iOS and 48×48 dp on Android. On the web, WCAG 2.5.8 (AA) asks for at least 24×24 CSS px; 44×44 px (2.5.5, AAA) is the comfortable target).
- **Esc** on a keyboard.
- **Swipe down** (common on iOS and Android): the photo follows your finger, the background fades, and letting go far enough closes it; a short drag snaps back.
- The system **Back** (Android Back button or gesture, browser Back on the web) closes the **viewer**, not the whole screen.

After closing, the grid is **exactly where you left it**: same scroll position, same filter, and **focus on the thumbnail of the photo you were last looking at** (photo 7 if you swiped from 3 to 7). On iOS and Android, the viewer often shrinks back into that thumbnail, which only works if the app knows which one it is.

### Counter, captions and alt text

- The **counter** ("3 of 12") is 1-based, matches the photo shown, and its total matches the photos the user can actually swipe through. If the grid was **filtered** to 3 outside photos, the viewer shows those 3 ("1 of 3"), and tapping a thumbnail always opens **that** photo.
- A **caption** ("Balcony with a view over the river") helps everyone; long captions wrap or expand, they don't run under the buttons.
- Every photo needs <Term id="alt-text" /> that describes what matters for the task: "Floor plan: living room 24 m², bedroom 14 m²..." is useful; "IMG_4032.jpg" or "photo" is not. The thumbnail button's name is the alt text, plus the position if that helps ("Photo 3 of 12: ...").

### Video items

Galleries often mix in videos (a video tour, a short clip in a chat). Check that the thumbnail shows it's a video (play icon, duration), that it **does not autoplay with sound** in the viewer, that swiping away **stops** it, that it has controls (play, pause, sound) and <Term id="captions" /> when there's speech, and that the network doesn't download the whole video until the user presses Play.

### Sharing and saving

If the viewer offers **Share** or **Save**: it must share **the photo currently shown** (not the first one, not the thumbnail-size version), open the **system share sheet** on mobile, and saving to the photo library must ask for permission the right way (on iOS, the "add photos only" permission; see [App permissions](/features/app-permissions)). Shared links should open the **same photo** in the listing (a <Term id="deep-link" /> like `?photo=7`).

### Slow networks: placeholders and blur-up

On a slow connection the grid must not look broken. Good options:

- **Placeholders** with the right size (a grey box or the photo's dominant color), so nothing jumps.
- **Blur-up**: show a tiny, blurred version first (a few hundred bytes, sometimes embedded in the page), then fade in the sharp image.
- In the viewer: show the **thumbnail** (already loaded) scaled up and blurred, plus a loading indicator, until the large version arrives. Never a blank black screen with no feedback.

Test with <Term id="network-throttling" />: DevTools presets "Slow 4G" and "3G", the Network Link Conditioner on iOS, or a proxy (see [Network conditions](/mobile/network-conditions)).

### Tools

- **Real devices:** pinch, double-tap, swipe-down and edge gestures only feel right with real fingers. See [Gestures](/mobile/gestures).
- **DevTools › Network** (filter *Img*, *Disable cache*) to count requests and MB; the *Size* column and the image preview show each file's real pixel size. **Lighthouse** flags "Properly size images" and "Defer offscreen images". See [Performance basics](/web/performance-basics).
- **Proxy tools** (Charles, Proxyman) to see what a mobile app downloads, and to slow it down. See [Proxy tools](/mobile/proxy-tools).
- **DevTools › Rendering:** emulate `prefers-reduced-motion`. **Elements / Accessibility pane:** check the dialog role, labels and where focus goes.
- **Screen readers:** VoiceOver (iOS/macOS), TalkBack (Android), NVDA (Windows).

## Accessibility

- **Thumbnails are buttons or links** with a name from the <Term id="alt-text" /> (and "video, 0:42" for videos). A grid of unlabeled images is a list of "image, image, image" for screen reader users.
- **The viewer is a modal dialog** with a label ("Photos of Bright 2-room flat"). Focus moves **into** it on open, stays inside it (a <Term id="focus-trap" />), the page behind is hidden from assistive technology, and focus **returns to the right thumbnail** on close (WCAG 2.4.3 Focus Order, Level A).
  <DevNote>`role="dialog"` with `aria-modal="true"`, or a native `<dialog>` opened with `showModal()`; the page behind gets `inert`.</DevNote>
- **Keyboard** (WCAG 2.1.1 Keyboard, Level A): Tab reaches Close, Previous, Next, Share and zoom; Left/Right change the photo; Esc closes. Focus is always visible.
- **Gesture alternatives:** swiping and pinching are path-based and multi-point gestures, so every action needs a simple tap or click alternative: Previous/Next buttons, an X button, zoom buttons or double-tap (WCAG 2.5.1 Pointer Gestures, Level A). Panning a zoomed photo by dragging needs a non-drag alternative too, for example zooming at a tapped point or zoom buttons (WCAG 2.5.7 Dragging Movements, Level AA).
- **Announce changes:** when the photo changes, screen readers hear its name and position ("Balcony view, photo 8 of 12"), using a polite live region or by moving focus.
- **Don't block browser zoom.** Blocking it (to make the custom pinch work) stops low-vision users from zooming the rest of the page (WCAG 1.4.4 Resize Text, Level AA).
  <DevNote>Zoom is blocked by a viewport with `user-scalable=no` or `maximum-scale=1`.</DevNote>
- **Motion:** respect <Term id="reduced-motion" />. Videos with sound must not start by themselves (WCAG 1.4.2 Audio Control, Level A).
- **Contrast:** white buttons and captions over photos need a dark background or <Term id="scrim" />: 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text and UI parts (WCAG 1.4.3 / 1.4.11).

## Mobile vs web

<Tabs labels={['iOS', 'Android', 'Web']}>
<Tab>

- Apps build their own viewer, or show files with **Quick Look**, which brings zoom and the share button.
  <DevNote>A paging `UIScrollView` / `UICollectionView` (zoom via `minimumZoomScale` and `maximumZoomScale`), SwiftUI, or `QLPreviewController`.</DevNote>
- Users expect **swipe down to close** and the photo **shrinking back into its thumbnail**. Test that it lands on the right thumbnail after swiping to another photo.
- Test the **left-edge swipe**: in a viewer it may trigger the navigation Back gesture instead of the previous photo.
- Saving needs the "add photos" permission text.
  <DevNote>`NSPhotoLibraryAddUsageDescription`.</DevNote>
- Test **VoiceOver**, **Reduce Motion** and **rotation** while zoomed.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- No standard viewer in Material 3: apps use a pager with a zoomable image, often from a library.
  <DevNote>`ViewPager2`, Compose `HorizontalPager`; Coil or Glide for loading and caching thumbnails.</DevNote>
- **System Back** (button or gesture) must close the viewer first. With **predictive back** (Android 14 and later), the viewer may shrink while you swipe; cancelling the gesture must leave it open.
- Test **rotation** and other <Term id="configuration-change">configuration changes</Term>: same photo, same filter after rotating? Also test **process death** (does the app come back on the same photo?) and **TalkBack**.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- Libraries (PhotoSwipe, GLightbox, Fancybox) or a native `<dialog>`. Check `srcset`/`sizes`, `loading="lazy"` and `width`/`height` on thumbnails in DevTools.
- **Browser Back** should close the viewer (the page pushes a history entry), and a **URL per photo** (`#photo-7` or `?photo=7`) lets users share and reload the same photo.
- Test with **mouse only** (drag, double-click, arrows), **keyboard only** and **touch** (phone or DevTools device mode); trackpad pinch on a laptop may zoom the whole page instead.
- Check the **page scroll is locked** while the viewer is open (<Term id="scroll-lock" />), and that it's restored exactly on close.

<KeyTakeaways>

- A gallery is two parts: a **thumbnail grid** (small, cropped previews) and a **viewer** (whole, sharp photo in a modal dialog).
- **Load smart:** small sized thumbnails, lazy-loaded; the large version only when opened, with the next one preloaded. Check the **MB** in the network log.
- **The right photo, every time:** test tapping thumbnails **after filtering or sorting**, the **counter** (1-based, right total) and the **ends** (no empty slide).
- **Zoom** has limits, **panning** stops at the edges, and zoom **resets** on the next photo.
- **Closing** (X, Esc, swipe down, Back) returns to the **same scroll position** and the **thumbnail of the last viewed photo**.
- Accessibility: thumbnails named by **alt text**, viewer as a **dialog** with a **focus trap**, arrow keys, and **button alternatives** to swipe, pinch and drag (WCAG 2.5.1, 2.5.7).

</KeyTakeaways>
