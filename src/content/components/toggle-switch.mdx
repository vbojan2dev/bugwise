Open Settings on any phone and you'll see a column of little pills: **Wi-Fi**, **Bluetooth**, **Airplane Mode**. Flip one and something happens right away. That's a <Term id="toggle-switch">toggle switch</Term>.

A switch has only two states, so what could go wrong? Quite a lot: the switch says "on" while the server says "off", the setting is forgotten after a restart, or the label is so confusing that nobody knows what "on" means. Let's learn to catch all of that.

## What is a toggle switch?

A toggle switch is a control with **exactly two states, on and off**, that **takes effect immediately**. There's no Save button: flipping the switch *is* the action. It mimics a physical light switch.

You'll see it used for:

- **Device and app settings**: *Dark theme*, *Wi-Fi*, *Face ID for payments*.
- **Notifications and privacy**: *Order updates*, *Share precise location*, *Personalized ads*.
- **Turning a feature on or off in place**: *Show prices with VAT*, *Auto-play videos*.

### Toggle vs checkbox vs segmented control

These three are mixed up all the time. The key question is: **does the change happen immediately, or after the user presses a button?**

| Component | States | When it takes effect | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Toggle switch** | On / off | **Immediately** | Settings: Dark theme, Notifications |
| **[Checkbox](/components/checkbox)** | Checked / unchecked (or mixed) | **After Submit / Save** | Forms: "I agree to the terms", picking several items |
| **[Segmented control](/components/segmented-control)** | One of 2–5 named options | Immediately | Two options that aren't on/off: km / miles, List / Map |
| **[Radio buttons](/components/radio-button)** | One of several | Usually after Submit | Forms: choose a delivery option |
| **Button** | No lasting state | When tapped | Actions: Save, Pay, Delete |

<Callout type="dev" title="Platform names">
Apple and Google both call it a **switch** (`UISwitch`, SwiftUI `Toggle`, Material `Switch`). Designers and web libraries often say **toggle**. On the web it's usually a checkbox or button styled to look like a switch, with `role="switch"`.
</Callout>

In the iOS Settings app, tapping a row's text does nothing; only the switch reacts. On Android, Material list rows with a switch are usually tappable across the whole row. On the web, clicking the `<label>` should toggle the switch. Know which behavior your app is supposed to follow, and test it.

### When does the setting really save?

Most switches change something on a server: your notification preferences, your privacy settings. That's where the nastiest bugs live. The UI can say one thing while the server stores another.

<SavePlayground client:visible />

There are two common patterns:

- **Optimistic UI**: the switch flips instantly and the request is sent in the background. If the request fails, the switch must **roll back** and tell the user. This feels fast and is the usual choice.
- **Pessimistic UI**: the switch shows progress and only flips after the server confirms. It's slower, but makes sense for important settings like *Two-factor authentication* or *Allow card payments abroad*.

The bug is the third option nobody plans for: **optimistic without rollback**. The switch shows *on*, the server has *off*, and the user finds out only when they miss an important notification.

## When to use it

A toggle switch is the right choice when:

- The setting has **two clear states** that can be described as on and off.
- The change applies **immediately**, without a Save button.
- The label names a **thing that is on or off**: *Dark theme*, *Order updates*.
- The user might switch it back and forth, and both states are safe.

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Silent failure** | The switch flips, the request fails (500 or offline), and the switch stays flipped with no message | High |
| 2 | **Not persisted** | The setting is back to its old value after restarting the app, logging out and in, or on another device | High |
| 3 | **Out of sync** | The switch shows "on" but the feature behaves as "off" (e.g. notifications still arrive) | High |
| 4 | **Race condition on fast toggling** | Quick on/off/on taps end in the wrong final state, because responses arrive out of order | Medium |
| 5 | **Dependent switches not disabled** | Child options stay active while the parent switch ("Push notifications") is off | Medium |
| 6 | **Label does nothing** | Tapping the label or row doesn't toggle, where the platform says it should (Android rows, web labels) | Low–Medium |
| 7 | **Confusing label** | Negative or double-negative wording, so users don't know what "on" means | Medium |
| 8 | **Wrong default** | Marketing or tracking switches default to "on" without consent (a legal risk under GDPR) | High |
| 9 | **State shown only by color** | Users with color blindness can't tell on from off, especially in dark mode | Medium |
| 10 | **Screen reader silence** | VoiceOver / TalkBack doesn't say "switch", "on" or "off", just the label | High (for affected users) |

<Callout type="bug" title="Why switches lie">
Most bugs above come from one thing: there are **two copies** of the setting, one on the screen and one on the server. Every time they can disagree, a tester should ask: what does the user see, what is stored, and what happens after a restart? Testing <Term id="state-persistence" /> means checking that the screen and the server agree after every possible interruption.
</Callout>

<Callout type="example" title="How would you report bug #1?">

**Title:** Settings: switch stays flipped when saving fails (500), no error shown

**Steps:** 1. Simulate a server error for `PATCH /api/settings` (proxy or test environment). 2. Open Settings. 3. Turn off **Share precise location**. 4. Restart the app.

**Expected:** After the failed request, the switch returns to **on** and a message explains that the change wasn't saved.
**Actual:** The switch stays **off** with no message. After restart it shows **on** again. The user believes location sharing is off when it isn't.

**Severity:** High (privacy setting silently not applied) · **Reproducible:** 10/10 while the API returns 500

## Accessibility

- **Role and state:** <Term id="screen-reader">Screen readers</Term> must announce the label, the role ("switch" or "toggle button") and the state ("on"/"off").
  <DevNote>On the web use `role="switch"` with `aria-checked`, or a native `<input type="checkbox" role="switch">`.</DevNote>
- **A real label:** the switch must be connected to its visible label, not just placed next to it. Otherwise VoiceOver says only "switch, on". For what?
  <DevNote>`<label for>` or `aria-labelledby`.</DevNote>
- **Not color alone:** the handle position already helps; Material's check/cross icons and iOS's optional on/off labels (Settings › Accessibility › Display & Text Size › On/Off Labels) help more. Check contrast of the track and handle: at least **3:1** against the background.
- **<Term id="touch-target">Touch target</Term>:** at least 44×44 pt on iOS and 48×48 dp on Android. On the web, WCAG 2.5.8 (AA) asks for at least 24×24 CSS px; 44×44 px (2.5.5, AAA) is the comfortable target. A whole tappable row is even better.
- **Keyboard (web):** <kbd>Tab</kbd> reaches the switch, <kbd>Space</kbd> toggles it, and the focus ring is visible.
- **Announce failures:** if a save fails and the switch rolls back, announce the error message too, or screen reader users will never know.

<Callout type="tip">
Try the web switches in the gallery with your keyboard: press <kbd>Tab</kbd> to reach one, then <kbd>Space</kbd>. Click the label text too.
</Callout>

## Mobile vs web

<Tabs labels={['iOS', 'Android', 'Web']}>
<Tab>

- Green track when on, grey when off. The color can be customized, so check contrast.
  <DevNote>Native: `UISwitch` (UIKit) or `Toggle` (SwiftUI).</DevNote>
- In grouped Settings-style lists, the switch sits on the **right**; tapping the row text usually does nothing.
- Optional **On/Off Labels** accessibility setting shows I/O marks on the switch. Test with it on.
- VoiceOver reads "Label, switch button, on". Double-tap toggles.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- Material 3 switch: the handle grows when on, and can show **check / cross icons**. The whole list row is usually tappable.
- Test with **TalkBack**, **font scale** and **display size**, plus dark theme (the colors change completely).
- Watch for **process death**: Android may kill the app in the background. Coming back, are the switches still correct?
- Older apps may still use Material 2 switches: a thin track with a larger round handle. Mixed styles on one screen are a visual bug.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- No cross-browser HTML switch element, so quality varies. Inspect it in <Term id="devtools">DevTools</Term>.
  <DevNote>It's built from a checkbox or a button with `role="switch"`. (Safari has started supporting a `switch` attribute on checkboxes, but other browsers don't yet.)</DevNote>
- Check that clicking the **label** toggles it, that <kbd>Space</kbd> works, and that the focus ring is visible.
- Use DevTools to throttle or block the settings request and check rollback and error messages.
- Inside forms, ask: does this switch save immediately, or only when the form is submitted? If the answer is "on submit", it should probably be a checkbox.

</Tab>
</Tabs>

<KeyTakeaways>

- A toggle switch has **two states** and **takes effect immediately**. In forms that need Submit, use checkboxes.
- If both options have names (km / miles), use a segmented control, not a switch.
- Labels must be **positive**: on means the named thing is on.
- The screen and the server are two copies of the setting. Test **failed saves (rollback + message)**, **restarts**, **second devices** and **fast toggling**.
- Check **dependent switches**, **label/row tapping**, **color-independent state**, and **screen reader** announcements.

</KeyTakeaways>
