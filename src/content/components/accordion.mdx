You click the "Track your return" link in an email. The shop opens the order page, scrolls down a bit... and shows a closed section called **Returns**. You click the title. Nothing. You click it again. Nothing. Finally you spot the tiny arrow on the right, click it, and the section opens, while the arrow keeps pointing down.

That's four bugs in ten seconds, all in one of the "simplest" components on the web. Accordions hide content, and **hidden content is where bugs hide too**: in the state, the scroll position, links, search and screen readers. Let's learn to find them.

## What is an accordion?

An accordion is a **vertical stack of sections, each with a header that opens or closes its content**. Closed, you only see the headers, so a long page becomes a short, scannable list. Open, a section pushes the content below it down, like the bellows of an accordion.

One single open/close section is often called a **disclosure** (or "collapsible", "expandable section"). An accordion is a group of them.

You'll usually see accordions in:

- **FAQ and help pages**: *How do I return an item? Can I change my address?*
- **Order and booking details**: *Delivery · Items · Payment · Returns*.
- **Product pages on mobile**: *Description · Size & fit · Shipping*.
- **Settings and filters**: *Price, Brand, Size* in a shop's filter panel; advanced options in a form.

### How it differs from similar components

<Callout type="dev" title="Names differ by platform">
Designers say *accordion*, the HTML standard has **`<details>` and `<summary>`**, the WAI-ARIA guide calls one section a **disclosure**, SwiftUI calls it a **`DisclosureGroup`**, and Android developers talk about **expandable list items**. It's the same idea: a header that shows or hides content.
</Callout>

### Single or multiple open?

- **Multiple open** (the default on most platforms): each section opens and closes on its own. Best when users may want to **compare** sections or keep several open, like filters or order details.
- **Single open** (an "exclusive" accordion): opening one closes the others. It keeps the page short, but users can't see two answers at once, and closing a long section above can **move** the section you just opened (more on that below).

Neither is wrong. The bug is when the product says one thing and the app does another, or when the two modes are mixed on similar pages.

### Default open state

Decide, document and test which sections start open:

- **All closed** is common for FAQs: the questions are the content.
- **First section open** (or the most important one) helps users see that the sections open at all, and answers the most common question without a tap. On an order page, *Delivery* is often open.
- **Remember the user's choice** only if the product asks for it. Then test that it really comes back after a reload, Back navigation and an app restart (<Term id="state-persistence" />).

## When to use it

Accordions work well when:

- The page has **many sections** and users only need **one or two** of them (FAQs, help, order details).
- Each section has a **clear, predictable title**, so users can tell what's inside without opening it.
- The content is **secondary** or reference material, and the screen is small.
- Users don't need to **compare** content across sections at the same time.

## How good accordions behave

Most accordion bugs are not about colors. They are about **state, focus and scrolling**. These are the rules to test against.

### The indicator tells the truth

The chevron points **down when closed and up when open** (on iOS: **right when closed, down when open**). Whatever opens or closes a section (a tap, the keyboard, "Expand all", a link, the default state on load) the chevron, the panel and the screen reader state must **agree**. A chevron that's driven by its own variable instead of the real open state drifts out of sync quickly.

### The whole header is the button

Users tap the **title**, not the arrow. The entire header row must react, with a <Term id="touch-target" /> of at least 44×44 pt on iOS and 48×48 dp on Android. A tiny chevron that's the only clickable part fails users with shaky hands, and feels broken to everyone else.

### Opening doesn't lose your place

Opening and closing changes the height of the page, so things move. Test with **long content**:

- In **single-open** mode, opening a section **below** a long open section closes the long one. Everything moves up, and the header you just tapped can **jump out of view**. Good implementations keep the tapped header where it was.
- Code that "scrolls the new content into view" must scroll to the **header**, not to the end of the panel. Otherwise long panels push their own header off-screen.
- Closing a long section while you're scrolled inside it should leave you **on its header**, not somewhere random further down the page.
- On the web, check that the page height update doesn't make the browser's **scroll anchoring** jump in odd ways, and on phones check rotation (<Term id="reflow" />).

### Animation respects "Reduce motion"

A short height animation (about 200–300 ms) helps users see where the content came from. But users who turn on **Reduce motion** (iOS), **Remove animations** (Android) or `prefers-reduced-motion` (web) should get an **instant** change or a simple fade. Also check that fast double taps during the animation don't leave the section half-open or out of sync with the chevron.

### Deep links open the section

FAQ and help pages are full of links like `shop.example.com/help#returns`, and apps get <Term id="deep-link">deep links</Term> like `shop://orders/4821/returns`. The link must **open** the target section and scroll its **header** into view, both when the page loads and when the user clicks an in-page link. Scrolling to a closed header, or opening the section without scrolling to it, are both bugs.

### Find in page and hidden content

Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>F</kbd> (<kbd>Cmd</kbd>+<kbd>F</kbd> on a Mac) and search for a word that's inside a closed section. On most custom accordions, the browser finds **nothing**, because closed content is hidden. Users then think the answer doesn't exist.

<DevNote>Closed content is hidden with `display: none`.</DevNote>

Two web features help:

- **Native `<details>`**: Chrome, Edge and recent Firefox versions search inside closed `<details>` elements and **open** the one with the match. Safari added this later, so check it there too.
- **`hidden="until-found"`**: a custom accordion can hide its panel this way. The browser can still search it and reveals the section (the page should then update its chevron). Chromium browsers and recent Firefox support it; Safari support is newer and partial at the time of writing, so test in your target browsers.
  <DevNote>The browser fires a `beforematch` event; the page should also update `aria-expanded`.</DevNote>

<Callout type="info" title="SEO and discoverability">
Search engines can only index text that is **in the page**. Content that is in the HTML but collapsed is generally indexed (Google has said it treats hidden-for-design content in accordions and tabs normally on mobile). Content that's **fetched from the server only when a section opens** may never be seen by a crawler, and it isn't found by <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>F</kbd> either. If an FAQ is meant to bring visitors from Google, ask how the answers are loaded.
</Callout>

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Chevron out of sync** | Arrow points down while the section is open, or doesn't turn after "Expand all" | Low–Medium |
| 2 | **Only the arrow is clickable** | Clicking the title does nothing | Medium |
| 3 | **Scroll jump** | After opening a section, its header is off-screen | Medium |
| 4 | **Wrong screen reader state** | VoiceOver says "collapsed" for an open section | High (for affected users) |
| 5 | **Deep link lands on a closed section** | `#returns` scrolls there but doesn't open it | Medium |
| 6 | **"Expand all" out of sync** | The button says "Expand all" while everything is open | Low |
| 7 | **Hidden content not searchable** | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>F</kbd> finds nothing that's inside a closed section | Low–Medium |
| 8 | **Content cut off** | Long text, large fonts or a late image are clipped at a fixed height | Medium–High |
| 9 | **Focus lost or trapped** | After closing, focus jumps to the top; or links in a closed panel are still Tab stops | Medium |
| 10 | **Double tap breaks it** | Fast taps during the animation leave a half-open section | Low |

<Callout type="bug" title="Why #8 happens">
To animate the height, developers sometimes give the open panel a **fixed max-height** (like 300 px). It works with the sample text, but a long answer, a translated one, a bigger font size or an image that loads late gets **cut off** with no scrollbar. Always test the **longest** section, at the **largest text size** (<Term id="dynamic-type" /> on iOS, Font size on Android, 200% zoom on the web).
</Callout>

### Tools

- **Elements panel in [DevTools](/web/devtools-for-testers):** click a header and watch `aria-expanded` change live in the DOM. If it never changes, you've found bug #4 without a screen reader.
- **Accessibility pane / Lighthouse:** shows the computed role, name and state of each header.
- **Reduced motion on the web:** DevTools › <kbd>Cmd</kbd>/<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>P</kbd> › "Emulate CSS prefers-reduced-motion".
- **Deep links:** paste URLs with `#section` into a new tab; on Android use `adb shell am start -a android.intent.action.VIEW -d "shop://orders/4821/returns"`, on the iOS Simulator `xcrun simctl openurl booted "shop://orders/4821/returns"`.

## Accessibility

- **Button inside a heading (web).** The heading lets <Term id="screen-reader" /> users jump between sections; the button makes it focusable and clickable.
  <DevNote>The ARIA accordion pattern puts a real `<button>` inside a heading (`<h3><button aria-expanded="false" aria-controls="panel-id">Returns</button></h3>`); <Term id="aria" /> attributes carry the state.</DevNote>
- **`aria-expanded` must follow the state.** `true` when open, `false` when closed. Put it on the **button**, not on the panel or a wrapper `div`.
  <DevNote>`aria-controls` points to the panel's id. Panels can be a `role="region"` labeled by their header, but with many panels that creates noise, so it's optional.</DevNote>
- **Native `<details>` / `<summary>`** gets all of this for free: focusable, <kbd>Enter</kbd>/<kbd>Space</kbd> to toggle, state announced. Check that custom styling didn't remove the focus ring.
- **<Term id="keyboard-navigation">Keyboard</Term>.** <kbd>Tab</kbd> moves between headers and into open panels, <kbd>Enter</kbd> or <kbd>Space</kbd> toggles. Links inside **closed** panels must not be reachable with <kbd>Tab</kbd>. After closing, focus stays on the header (<Term id="focus-order" />).
- **Mobile.** On iOS, VoiceOver should announce the expanded or collapsed state; on Android, TalkBack announces "expanded" / "collapsed" and offers expand / collapse actions when the app sets them. Custom rows that only change an icon announce nothing.
- **Don't rely on the arrow alone.** The open section should also be visible from its content, and the chevron needs 3:1 contrast against its background.

<KeyTakeaways>

- An accordion is a stack of **headers that show or hide content** in place. Use it for lots of optional detail, never to hide prices, fees or errors.
- The **whole header** is the button, and the **chevron, panel and `aria-expanded`** must always agree.
- Test **long content**: scroll jumps in single-open mode, panels scrolled to the end, text cut off at a fixed height.
- **Deep links** must open the section they point to, and closed content should still be **findable** (`<details>`, `hidden="until-found"`).
- Always test with the **keyboard**, a **screen reader**, **Reduce motion** and the **largest text size**.

</KeyTakeaways>
