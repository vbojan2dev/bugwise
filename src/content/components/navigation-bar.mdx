You open a pizza in a food delivery app. The button in the top-left corner says **‹ Restaurants**, so you tap it, expecting the list of restaurants... and land on the pizzeria's menu instead. Nothing crashed. But you no longer trust that button, and you start using the app more carefully. That small lie in the corner is a bug, and a very common one.

Navigation bars are on almost every screen, so users touch them hundreds of times a day. They tell people **where they are**, **how to go back** and **where else they can go**. When they're wrong, users get lost. Let's learn how they should behave.

## What is a navigation bar?

"Navigation bar" is used for two different things, and you need to know both:

- **Top bars** sit at the top of a screen. They show the screen's **title**, a **back button** (on deeper screens) and a few **actions** like Search or Share. iOS calls this the *navigation bar*; Android (Material Design) calls it the *top app bar*; on the web it's the *header*.
- **Bottom bars** sit at the bottom and switch between the app's **main areas**: Home, Search, Orders, Account. iOS calls this the *tab bar*; Material calls it the *navigation bar* (older name: *bottom navigation*).

Yes, "navigation bar" means a top bar on iOS and a bottom bar on Android. When a ticket says "the nav bar is broken", ask which one.

### Top bars

- **iOS navigation bar** is 44 pt tall. The **large title** (big, bold, left-aligned) sits under the bar on main screens and **collapses into a small centered title** as you scroll. The **back button** shows a chevron plus the **previous screen's title**. If that title doesn't fit, iOS shortens it to "Back" or just the chevron.
- **Material top app bar** classically comes in four types: **small** (64 dp), **center-aligned** (for a main screen or a brand name), **medium** (112 dp) and **large** (152 dp). Medium and large start tall and **shrink to small** as you scroll. The 2025 Material 3 *Expressive* update renames it *app bar*, makes center alignment an option of the small bar, replaces medium and large with **medium flexible** and **large flexible** versions, and adds a **search app bar**, so newer apps may use those names. When content scrolls under it, the bar gets a tinted background. Actions that don't fit go into the **⋮ overflow menu**.

### Bottom bars

- **iOS tab bar**: icon plus label, the selected tab tinted in the app color, red badges. On iPhone, up to **five** tabs; more go into a "More" tab. Since iOS 26, the Liquid Glass tab bar floats over the content and can **shrink while you scroll**.
- **Material navigation bar**: **3 to 5 destinations**, icons with labels, and a **pill-shaped indicator** behind the selected icon. Fewer than three should be tabs or buttons; more than five should be a [drawer](/components/drawer) or a navigation rail.

## When to use it

- **Top bar:** on nearly every screen. It answers "where am I?" (title) and "how do I get out?" (back or close).
- **Bottom bar:** when the app has **3–5 top-level areas** of similar importance that people switch between **often**, like Home, Search, Orders and Account in a shopping app. It must be visible on those top-level screens.

Also not a job for bars:

- **Bottom bar for actions.** "Add to cart" or "Delete" belong in buttons, not next to destinations.
- **Bottom bar inside flows.** Hide it during checkout, sign-up or a full-screen photo editor, where jumping away would lose the user's work.
- **Two stacked bottom bars**, or a bottom bar on top of a cookie banner. The content gets squeezed into a strip.

## How good bars behave

### The back button tells the truth

On iOS the back button **names the screen you'll go back to**. That's a promise, and it breaks easily when screens are reused or pushed from different places (a dish opened from *Search* vs from *Home*). Check the label on **every level** of a flow, not just the first. On every platform, check that **all the ways back** lead to the same place:

- iOS: the back button and the **edge swipe** from the left.
- Android: the app's ← arrow and the **system Back** (button or gesture from either edge).
- Web: the site's back link and the **browser Back** button.

The differences between iOS and Android back behavior are explained in [iOS vs Android](/mobile/ios-vs-android#navigation-the-biggest-source-of-bugs). The short version: **always test system Back** on Android, never only the arrow on screen.

### Titles fit, or truncate politely

Restaurant names, product names, people's names and translations can be long. A title must **<Term id="truncation">truncate</Term> with "…"** (or wrap, for large titles), and must never run under the back button or the actions. Test with a really long real name, in **German**, and at the **largest text size** (<Term id="dynamic-type" /> on iOS, Font size on Android).

### Re-tapping the selected destination

Tapping the destination you're already on is a **shortcut**, not a dead button:

<Steps>

1. **On a deeper screen?** Go back to that destination's **first screen** (pop to root).
2. **Already on the first screen?** **Scroll to the top**.
3. **Already at the top?** Some apps focus the search field (Search tab) or refresh. Either way, nothing should break or reload twice.

</Steps>

This is a long-standing iOS convention, and Material apps do the same. If it does nothing, users with a long list have to scroll all the way back by hand.

### Badges are counts, not decorations

A badge on *Orders* or *Chats* must equal the number of **unread** things, update when the user reads them (in any way: open the item, mark all as read, read it on another device) and disappear at zero. Re-opening something you've already read must not change the count. See [Tabs › Badges stay current](/components/tabs#badges-stay-current).

### Safe areas and the home indicator

Modern phones have a notch or Dynamic Island at the top and a **home indicator** (iPhone) or **gesture bar** (Android) at the bottom. The space around them is the <Term id="safe-area" />. Bars must extend their **background** into it, but keep their **buttons and labels out of it**:

- Labels under the home indicator are hard to read, and a tap there may start the system's "go home" gesture instead.
- In **landscape**, the notch moves to the side: check the left and right edges too.
- On Android 15+, apps targeting the new SDK are drawn **edge-to-edge** by default. Apps that weren't ready suddenly have bars under the status bar or the gesture bar. Test every new OS version.

### The content is inset for the bars

A bar that floats over the content (blurred iOS bars, Liquid Glass, transparent Android bars) needs the content to have **extra space** at the end. Otherwise the **last row of the list is hidden behind the bar** forever. Scroll to the very end of every long list.

### Behavior on scroll

Some apps **hide the bars when you scroll down** and **bring them back when you scroll up**, to give the content more room. That's a design choice, and it has rules: the bars must come back on a small upward scroll, at the end of the list, and when the user taps the status bar or rotates. They must never stay hidden on a screen with no scrolling, and must not hide while a screen reader user is navigating.

### The keyboard

When the keyboard opens (in Search, for example), the bottom bar is usually **covered by the keyboard**, which is fine. A bug is when the bar is **pushed up above the keyboard**, stealing space, or stays stuck halfway after the keyboard closes. Open and close the keyboard several times, in portrait and landscape.

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Back label lies** | "‹ Restaurants" goes back to the restaurant menu | Medium |
| 2 | **Title overlaps actions** | A long name runs under the ♡ and Share icons | Medium |
| 3 | **Re-tap does nothing** | Tapping the selected tab doesn't pop to root or scroll to top | Low–Medium |
| 4 | **Under the home indicator** | Tab labels sit under the home bar; taps trigger "go home" | Medium–High |
| 5 | **Last row hidden** | The final list item is stuck behind the bottom bar | Medium–High |
| 6 | **Wrong badge count** | *Orders* **0** while an update is still unread, or a badge that never clears | Medium |
| 7 | **System Back ≠ arrow** | The ← arrow goes back one screen, system Back closes the app | High |
| 8 | **Bars stay hidden** | After scrolling, the bottom bar never comes back | High |
| 9 | **Pushed up by the keyboard** | The bottom bar floats above the keyboard, covering the field | Medium |
| 10 | **Unnamed icon buttons** | The screen reader says "button" for Search, Share and ⋮ | High (for affected users) |

<Callout type="bug" title="Why #1 happens">
The back label should come from the **previous** screen in the stack. When developers take the title from somewhere else (the tab's first screen, a cached value, the screen that was open *last time*), it's right on the first level and wrong one level deeper. That's why you should always go **at least three screens deep** and read the back label on each one.
</Callout>

## Web headers

On websites, the top bar is the **header**, usually with a logo, a search icon, a cart and a [hamburger menu](/components/drawer). Many sites make it **sticky**, so it stays visible while you scroll. On a desktop that costs nothing. On a **small phone in landscape**, with a promo strip and a cookie bar, it can leave a tiny slit for the content.

<DevNote>`position: sticky` or `fixed`.</DevNote>

<WebHeaderLab client:visible />

What to check on web headers:

- **Space for content.** Try the smallest phone, **landscape**, and **200% text zoom**. If sticky bars take more than about a third of the screen, report it. Hiding the header on scroll down (and showing it on scroll up) is a common fix. WCAG's *Reflow* criterion (1.4.10) expects content to work at 320 CSS px wide without scrolling in two directions.
- **Focus not hidden.** Press <kbd>Tab</kbd> through the page. When a focused link scrolls behind the sticky header or the cookie bar, keyboard users can't see where they are. This fails <Term id="wcag" /> 2.2's *Focus Not Obscured* (2.4.11).
  <DevNote>Developers fix it with `scroll-padding-top` / `scroll-padding-bottom`.</DevNote>
- **Skip link.** The first <kbd>Tab</kbd> on the page should reveal a **"Skip to main content"** link, so keyboard users don't tab through the whole header on every page (WCAG 2.4.1, *Bypass Blocks*).
- **Anchors.** Links to `#reviews` should not land with the heading hidden under the sticky header.
- **Bottom bars on mobile web** must stay above the iPhone home indicator, and must not jump around when the browser's own toolbar shows and hides.
  <DevNote>They need `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)`.</DevNote>

### Tools

- **iOS Simulator:** test an iPhone with Face ID **and** an iPhone SE, rotate with <kbd>Cmd</kbd>+<kbd>←</kbd>/<kbd>→</kbd>, and raise the text size with the **Accessibility Inspector** (*Xcode › Open Developer Tool*) or *Settings › Accessibility › Display & Text Size › Larger Text*.
- **Android Emulator:** switch between **gesture** and **3-button navigation** (*Settings › System › Gestures › System navigation*) and test Back in both. Raise *Font size* and *Display size*.
- **Layout bounds (Android):** *Developer options › Show layout bounds* draws every view's edges, so overlaps and missing insets become obvious.
- **Web:** use the device toolbar in [DevTools](/web/devtools-for-testers) for small viewports and landscape, zoom the page to 200%, and press <kbd>Tab</kbd> from the address bar. On a real iPhone, check the bottom bar against the home indicator and Safari's toolbar.

## Accessibility

- **Name every icon button.** Search, Share, ♡ and ⋮ need accessible names ("Search", "Share", "Add to favorites", "More options"). A <Term id="screen-reader" /> that only says "button" is a bug.
- **Announce where you are.** The screen title should be read when a new screen opens. Bottom bar destinations should be announced with their **selected** state (iOS: "Home, selected, tab, 1 of 4").
  <DevNote>On the web, use `<nav aria-label="Main">` with `aria-current="page"` on the current link.</DevNote>
- **Badges need words.** "Orders, 2 new", not "Orders, 2" or just a red dot.
- **<Term id="touch-target">Touch targets</Term>:** at least 44×44 pt (iOS) / 48×48 dp (Android) for the back button, actions and each destination, even when the icon itself is smaller.
- **Large text.** At the largest text size, iOS shows a big **label popup** when you long-press a tab bar item. Titles must still fit or truncate; labels must not overlap.
- **Keyboard (web).** A skip link, a logical focus order (header, then main content), visible focus, and focus never hidden behind sticky bars.
- **Don't hide bars from assistive tech users.** Bars that hide on scroll must come back when focus moves into them.

<KeyTakeaways>

- "Navigation bar" means a **top bar** on iOS and a **bottom bar** on Android. Top bars: title, back, actions. Bottom bars: 3–5 main destinations.
- The **back button must tell the truth**: right label, and every way back (arrow, edge swipe, system Back, browser Back) lands on the same screen.
- Long titles, translations and large text must **truncate or wrap**, never overlap the buttons.
- Respect **safe areas** and **inset the content**: labels above the home indicator, last row above the bar.
- **Re-tapping** the selected destination pops to root, then scrolls to top. **Badges** count unread items exactly.
- On the web, sticky headers must leave **room for content** on small phones, never **hide focus**, and come with a **skip link**.

</KeyTakeaways>
