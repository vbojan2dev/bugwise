Two bunches of bananas. Three guests at the table. One adult, two children on the flight. Every time an app asks "how many?", there's a good chance it answers with a **stepper**: a minus, a number, a plus.

It's the simplest control on the screen, and one of the most expensive to get wrong: the number in a cart stepper becomes **money**. Let's see where it breaks.

## What is a stepper?

A stepper is a control that **changes a number by a fixed step** (usually 1) with a **decrease (−)** and an **increase (+)** button. The value stays between a **minimum** and a **maximum**.

You'll find steppers in:

- **Shopping carts**: quantity of each product.
- **Bookings**: guests, rooms, adults/children, nights.
- **Recipes and fitness**: servings, sets, reps.
- **Settings**: number of copies to print, reminder minutes.

### What to know about the value

- **Min and max.** At the limit, the matching button must be **disabled** (and look it). Common limits: 1 for a cart line (0 means "remove"), the **stock** reported by the server, "max 8 guests", "max 9 travellers in total".
- **Step size.** Usually 1, but not always: 0.5 kg of cheese, packs of 6, 15-minute slots. Check the value can't land between steps.
- **Typing.** Some steppers let you tap the number and type "12". Then everything from [text fields](/components/text-field) applies: validation, the keyboard type, what happens to "05", "1e2", "-3" or an empty field.
- **Long-press.** Many steppers **repeat** while you hold the button and speed up after a while. It must stop at the limit and stop when you let go.
  <DevNote>iOS `UIStepper` repeats by default.</DevNote>

### Stepper vs slider vs text field

| Component | Best for | Weak at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Stepper** | Small exact numbers, small changes (1–10) | Big jumps: 1 → 50 needs 49 taps |
| **[Slider](/components/slider)** | Rough values in a range (volume, price range) | Exact values |
| **[Text field](/components/text-field)** (number) | Any exact number, big ranges | Quick +1 changes, limits are invisible |

A good rule: if users usually change the value by **one or two**, use a stepper. If the value can be **large**, let users type (an editable stepper or a plain field).

### The web's number input and its quirks

`<input type="number">` looks like the perfect stepper: arrows, min, max, step. But it has surprises every web tester should know:

<NumberInputQuirks client:visible />

- **"e" is allowed**, because `1e2` (= 100) is a valid number in scientific notation. "e" on its own makes the value **empty**, not an error the app can see.
- **The mouse wheel can change the value** in Chromium browsers when the field is focused. Users scroll the page and silently order 7 instead of 2.
- **min, max and step are only hints.** The browser flags invalid values (`validity`), but nothing stops the app from sending them unless the form is validated.
- **Leading zeros** stay in the text ("05"), and code that treats the value as a string can do `"05" + 1 = "051"`.

<DevNote>That's why many shops use `type="text" inputmode="numeric"` with their own validation instead.</DevNote>

## When to use it

A stepper is a good choice when:

- The number is **small** and **exact**: 1–10 items, guests, tickets.
- Users usually change it by **one or two** from a sensible default.
- The **limits** are known and meaningful (stock, max guests), and can be shown.
- The change is easy to see and to undo: the number and the total update right away.

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **+ enabled at the stock limit** | Tap + at the limit, the server refuses, the number jumps back (or checkout fails) | Medium–High |
| 2 | **Typed value not validated** | "05", "1e2", "-3" or an empty field break the total or reach the server | High |
| 3 | **Shown and saved quantity differ** | After quick taps the field says 6, the order is for 3 | Critical (money) |
| 4 | **− at 1 removes without warning** | One tap too many and the item is gone, no undo | Medium |
| 5 | **Long-press doesn't stop** | Value keeps running after release, or runs past the max | Medium |
| 6 | **Disabled state lies** | − looks disabled at 1 but still works (or the other way round) | Low–Medium |
| 7 | **Silent for screen readers** | "Button, button", and the new value is never announced | High (for affected users) |
| 8 | **Mouse wheel changes the number** | Scrolling the page over a focused field changes the quantity | Medium |
| 9 | **Tiny buttons** | − and + are 24 px apart; users hit the wrong one | Medium |

<Callout type="bug" title="Why #3 happens: rapid taps vs the network">
Each tap sends a request to update the cart. Requests travel over a real network, so **they don't always arrive or come back in order**. If the app trusts whatever reply arrives last, or the server saves whatever request arrives last, an older value wins. Good apps **debounce** (wait until you stop tapping, then send once), send the **absolute** value ("set to 6", not "add 1"), and **ignore stale replies**, ideally with a version number the server checks too. The cart math itself is covered in [Shopping cart & checkout](/features/shopping-cart-and-checkout).
</Callout>

## Stress-test it

Tap + as fast as you can, or hold it, and then wait. Switch between the three sync strategies and compare what the cart shows with what the server saved.

<SyncLab client:visible />

<Callout type="tip" title="How to do this on a real app">
Turn on <Term id="network-throttling" /> (or a proxy with random delays, like Charles or Proxyman), then tap + quickly 5–10 times. Wait until all requests finish, **reload the cart**, and compare the quantity and the total with what you saw before. A <Term id="race-condition" /> often only shows up on slow or unstable networks.
</Callout>

## Accessibility

A stepper is two tiny icon buttons and a number. For screen reader users, all three need words.

| Key (web, editable value) | What it should do |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>↑</kbd> / <kbd>↓</kbd> | Increase / decrease by one step |
| <kbd>Page Up</kbd> / <kbd>Page Down</kbd> | Bigger step (optional) |
| <kbd>Home</kbd> / <kbd>End</kbd> | Jump to min / max (if they exist) |
| Typing digits | Sets the value directly |

- **The <Term id="aria" /> spinbutton pattern:** the value has a name ("Quantity of Oat milk") and, when the number needs a unit, a text value: "2 guests", "30 minutes".
  <DevNote>The value is an element with `role="spinbutton"` (a native `<input type="number">` has it already), with `aria-valuenow`, `aria-valuemin` and `aria-valuemax`. Add `aria-valuetext` for the unit.</DevNote>
- **Named buttons:** "Increase quantity" / "Decrease quantity", ideally with the product name. Icon-only buttons with no name are read as "button".
- **Announce the new value** after each press. With a spinbutton that has focus, screen readers do this automatically; when the focus stays on the + button, use a polite live region.
- **Disabled at the limits:** keyboard focus must not be lost when + becomes disabled under your finger.
  <DevNote>Use `aria-disabled` (or keep focus somewhere sensible).</DevNote>
- **<Term id="touch-target">Touch targets</Term>:** each button needs at least 44×44 pt (iOS) / 48×48 dp (Android), with enough space between − and +. On the web, WCAG 2.5.8 (AA) asks for at least 24×24 CSS px; 44×44 px (2.5.5, AAA) is the comfortable target.
- **On iOS**, VoiceOver reads the native stepper's two halves as buttons ("Increment", "Decrement" by default). Make sure the label with the value next to it is announced too, or give the stepper a meaningful accessibility value.
  <DevNote>That's `UIStepper`.</DevNote>

## Mobile vs web

<Tabs labels={['iOS', 'Android', 'Web']}>
<Tab>

- Ask about the limits, the step, long-press repeat (on by default) and wrapping (going past max back to min, off by default).
  <DevNote>Native: `UIStepper` (UIKit) or `Stepper` (SwiftUI). Properties: `minimumValue`, `maximumValue`, `stepValue`, `autorepeat` and `wraps`.</DevNote>
- The native stepper shows **no number itself**. Check the label next to it updates on every change, including during long-press.
- Cart steppers in shopping apps are almost always **custom**. Test them like a web component.
- Test with **VoiceOver**, **Dynamic Type** (the number must not be cut off: "10" vs "1") and **external keyboards** on iPad.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- No Material stepper: apps build their own. Ask how limits, long-press and disabled states were built.
  <DevNote>Apps combine `IconButton`s with a `Text` (Compose) or views.</DevNote>
- `NumberPicker` is a different control: a scrolling wheel. It's still used for timers and old-style forms.
- Test **TalkBack** names (an icon button without a description is read as "Button" or "Unlabelled"), **font scale**, and **configuration changes**: rotate the phone and check the quantity survives.
  <DevNote>An `ImageButton` without `contentDescription`.</DevNote>
- Watch rapid taps on slow networks: the same race conditions apply.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- `<input type="number">`: free arrows and keyboard support, but **"e" is allowed**, **the wheel can change it** in Chromium browsers, invalid input reads as **empty**, and min/max/step are not enforced on their own.
- Mobile browsers show the **numeric keyboard** only with the right `type` or `inputmode`. Check it on iOS Safari and Chrome on Android.
- Custom steppers: check **keyboard** support, **focus** after + becomes disabled, and the **requests** in <Term id="devtools" /> (one per tap? debounced? what does the server answer?).
- Open the cart in **two tabs**, change the quantity in one, reload the other: which value wins?

</Tab>

<KeyTakeaways>

- A stepper changes a **small exact number** by a fixed step between a **min** and a **max**. For large values, let users type.
- At the limits, the matching button is **disabled with a reason**. Server limits like **stock** belong in the UI before the request.
- Typed values need **validation**: "05", "1e2", "-3" and empty. The web's `<input type="number">` allows "e" and can change on **mouse wheel**.
- Rapid taps + network = **race conditions**: debounce, send absolute values, ignore stale replies, and check that screen, total and server agree.
- Going below the minimum (removing an item) needs **confirmation or undo**, and screen readers need **named buttons** and the **new value** announced.

</KeyTakeaways>
