You're booking a holiday apartment. Step 1, guests. Step 2, your address. Step 3, payment. You notice a typo in the address and press **Back**. The address form is empty. You type it all again, press **Continue**... and the payment step is empty too. Then the phone rings, you come back, refresh the page, and you're on step 1 with nothing filled in.

A wizard turns one long, scary form into a few short ones. It also adds **navigation and memory**: which step am I on, what did I already enter, where does Back go? Every one of those is a place where a user's work can quietly disappear.

## What is it?

A **wizard** (also called a multi-step form or stepper) splits a long task into an **ordered series of steps**, one screen or section at a time, with a **step indicator** showing where you are: *Contact › Shipping › Payment › Review*. You'll meet wizards in checkouts, bookings, sign-ups, loan and insurance applications, onboarding, and setup flows ("connect your smartwatch").

<Callout type="info" title="Not the + / − stepper">
"Stepper" means two different things. This page is about **steps in a process**. The small control with a minus button, a number and a plus button (quantity in a cart) is also called a <Term id="stepper" />; it has [its own page](/components/stepper).
</Callout>

### Linear or non-linear?

| Type | How it works | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Linear** | Steps in a fixed order. You can't open step 3 before step 2 is valid. Completed steps can be revisited. | Checkouts, payments, anything where later steps depend on earlier ones |
| **Non-linear** | Every step can be opened at any time, in any order. The final step checks that everything is complete. | Profiles, settings, long applications users fill in over several days |

The indicator must make the rule visible: in a linear wizard, upcoming steps look (and are) not clickable; in a non-linear one, every step looks clickable and shows whether it's done.

- **Material steppers:** Material Design (version 2) defined horizontal, vertical and mobile steppers. Material 3 no longer includes a stepper, but web libraries like MUI, Angular Material and Vuetify still ship one, so you'll see that look everywhere.
- **No native stepper on iOS or Android.** Apps usually show one screen per step with a progress bar or "Step 2 of 4", and use the [navigation bar](/components/navigation-bar) back button.
- **Optional steps** are labelled "Optional" and can be skipped. **Error states** mark a step with a red icon when it needs attention.
- **Progress without labels** (dots or a bar) is common in onboarding. It shows *how far*, but not *what's next*.

## When to use it

- The form is **long** (more than one screen of fields) and falls naturally into **groups**: who, where, how to pay.
- Later steps **depend** on earlier ones: shipping options depend on the address, payment options on the country.
- Users benefit from a **review** before committing: orders, bookings, applications, transfers.
- The task takes a while and users may **pause and come back**: then steps and drafts help a lot.

## How good wizards behave

### Validation happens per step

Each step checks its own fields when the user presses **Continue**, with clear <Term id="error-message">error messages</Term> next to the fields, and doesn't move on until they're fixed. Don't wait until the last step to say "ZIP code is invalid" without saying where. Also don't validate the whole wizard on step 1 ("Payment method is required" before the user has seen payment). See [text field](/components/text-field) for field-level <Term id="input-validation" />.

### Back keeps everything

**Back** goes to the previous step **with all its data still there**. That includes choices like radio buttons and checkboxes, not just text. Going back and forward several times must never lose, duplicate or reset anything. This is <Term id="state-persistence" />, and it's the first thing to test in any wizard.

### Progress is saved

Users get interrupted: a phone call, a lost connection, a closed tab. Good wizards save a **draft** (in the browser, on the server, or both) so a refresh or a return visit lands on the **same step with the same data**, sometimes with a message like "Welcome back, you were on step 3". Ask how long a draft lives, whether it follows the user to another device, and what's **not** saved on purpose (card numbers and passwords usually aren't).

On mobile, the same idea is <Term id="state-restoration" />: the OS can kill an app in the background, and the user should come back to the same step.

### Every step has an address (web)

On the web, each step should change the URL: `/checkout/shipping`, `/checkout/payment`. Then the **browser Back button** goes to the previous step (not out of the checkout), **Refresh** stays on the step, and support can send a <Term id="deep-link" /> to a specific step. Test what happens when you open a later step's URL **directly** in a linear wizard: you should land on the first incomplete step, not on a half-broken payment page.

On Android, the **system Back** gesture or button should go to the previous step too. On iOS, the **swipe from the left edge** does the same in a navigation stack. If Back closes the whole flow, the user at least deserves a "Leave checkout? Your progress is saved" message.

### Editing from the review page

The review step shows everything with **Edit** links. Edit opens that step, and saving should **return to Review** ("Save and return to review"), keeping every other step as it was. Walking the user through all later steps again, or worse, clearing them, is a common bug. One exception: if the change really affects later steps (a new country changes the shipping options), the wizard should **say so** and ask only for what changed.

### Errors send the user to the right step

When the server rejects the order at the end (address can't be delivered, card declined), the user should land **on the step that needs fixing**, with the error shown on the field, and their other data intact. A generic "Something went wrong" on the review page, or a restart from step 1, are both bugs.

### The indicator never lies

The highlighted step, the "Step 2 of 4" text, the page title and the form on screen must always agree: after Continue, Back, browser Back, Edit, refresh and deep links.

## Common bugs

| # | Bug | What you see | Typical severity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Back loses data** | The previous step's fields are empty after going back | High |
| 2 | **Browser/system Back leaves the flow** | One Back press and you're out of the checkout | High |
| 3 | **Indicator out of sync** | "Step 3" is highlighted while step 2 is shown | Medium |
| 4 | **Skipping required steps** | Clicking the indicator opens Payment without a valid address | High |
| 5 | **Edit from Review wipes later steps** | After editing Shipping, Payment is empty again | Medium–High |
| 6 | **No draft** | Refresh or app restart goes back to step 1, empty | Medium–High |
| 7 | **Errors on the wrong step** | Server error shows on Review, the field to fix isn't highlighted | Medium |
| 8 | **Double submit** | Pressing Place order twice creates two orders | Critical |
| 9 | **Stale later steps** | Changing the country keeps a shipping method that's no longer available | Medium |
| 10 | **Indicator doesn't fit** | Labels overlap or overflow on a 360 px screen | Low–Medium |

<Callout type="tip" title="The back-and-forth test">
Fill every step with **distinctive** data (not "test"), then go Back to step 1 and forward to Review using the in-app Back, the browser or system Back, the indicator and Edit links. Then refresh. Every value you typed must still be there, exactly once.
</Callout>

### Tools

- **The address bar and DevTools › Application** (see [DevTools for testers](/web/devtools-for-testers) and [cookies, sessions and storage](/web/cookies-sessions-and-storage)): watch the URL change per step, and find the draft in Local Storage, Session Storage or a server request. Check no card number or CVC is stored there.
- **DevTools › Network:** see when each step is saved, and that Place order sends **one** request even if you double-click.
- **Android:** *Developer options › Don't keep activities* to test the draft after the OS kills the app; test the system Back gesture on every step.
- **iOS:** swipe back from the left edge on every step; background the app for a while and return.
- **Screen readers:** VoiceOver, TalkBack or NVDA to hear the step changes.

## Accessibility

- **Announce the step.** When a new step opens, move focus to its heading ("Step 2 of 4: Shipping") or announce it in a live region. Otherwise a <Term id="screen-reader" /> user doesn't know anything changed after pressing Continue.
- **Mark the current step.** Say each step's state in text ("Contact, completed"). A check mark or color alone is not enough (WCAG 1.4.1 Use of Color, Level A).
  <DevNote>In the indicator use a list, with `aria-current="step"` on the current one.</DevNote>
- **Errors.** Link error messages to their fields, move focus to the first invalid field or an error summary, and don't rely on red alone. See <Term id="aria" /> and [text field](/components/text-field).
  <DevNote>Errors are linked with `aria-describedby`.</DevNote>
- **<Term id="keyboard-navigation">Keyboard</Term>.** Every field, the indicator links and Back / Continue reachable with <kbd>Tab</kbd> in a logical <Term id="focus-order" />. <kbd>Enter</kbd> in a field shouldn't accidentally submit the whole order.
- **Time limits.** If the wizard times out (common in banking and ticket sales), warn the user and let them extend it (WCAG 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable, Level A).
- **Don't make them type it twice.** WCAG 3.3.7 Redundant Entry (Level A) asks that information already entered in the same process is filled in or selectable, for example "Billing address same as shipping". A Back button that clears fields fails users here too.
- **<Term id="touch-target">Touch targets</Term>.** At least 44×44 pt on iOS and 48×48 dp on Android. On the web, WCAG 2.5.8 (AA) asks for at least 24×24 CSS px; 44×44 px (2.5.5, AAA) is the comfortable target. Small step circles in the indicator are a typical failure.

## Mobile vs web

<Tabs labels={['iOS', 'Android', 'Web']}>
<Tab>

- No stepper component: each step is usually a screen pushed onto a **navigation stack**, with "Step 2 of 4" or a progress bar. The back button and the **left-edge swipe** must go to the previous step with data kept.
- A modal flow (sheet) may have **Cancel** instead of Back: does it ask "Discard your changes?" before throwing away a half-filled booking?
- Background the app on step 3 and come back later, and test **Dynamic Type** at the largest size: does the indicator still fit?

</Tab>
<Tab>

- No native stepper either: steps are usually screens or fragments. The **system Back** gesture/button must go to the previous step; if it closes the flow, it should confirm first.
- Test **rotation** and **process death** (*Don't keep activities*): same step, same data afterwards.
- Check the **keyboard** (IME) doesn't cover the Continue button on small screens, and that **predictive back** shows the previous step, not the screen before the wizard.

</Tab>
<Tab>

- Each step should have its own **URL**: test Back, Forward, Refresh, bookmarks and opening a later step directly.
- Test the **draft**: refresh, close the tab, open the site in a second tab (do the two tabs fight?), log in on another device.
- Check the **responsive** layout: at 360 px the horizontal indicator should switch to a compact or vertical version.
- **Double submit**: double-click Place order and check only one order is created.

</Tab>

<KeyTakeaways>

- A wizard splits a long task into **ordered steps** with an indicator. It is not the **+/− stepper**.
- Use it for long forms with natural groups and dependencies; not for short forms or independent sections (use tabs).
- **Back keeps data**, always. Test in-app Back, browser or system Back, the indicator and Edit links.
- On the web, every step has a **URL**; a **draft** lets refresh and return visits continue where the user stopped.
- **Linear** wizards don't let users skip ahead; **Edit from Review** returns to Review; **server errors** open the right step.
- The **indicator never lies**, and each new step is **announced** to screen readers ("Step 2 of 4: Shipping").

</KeyTakeaways>
