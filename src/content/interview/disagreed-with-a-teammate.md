---
question: Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate. How did you handle it?
short: Use a short STAR story about a real work disagreement. Show that you listened, used facts instead of opinions, looked for a solution together, involved the right person if needed, and kept a good relationship afterwards.
category: behavioral
level: junior
lookingFor: A real, specific example, respectful and fact-based communication, willingness to listen and change your mind, a constructive outcome, and what you learned.
pitfalls:
  - Saying you never disagree with anyone.
  - Telling a story where you were completely right and the other person was simply wrong.
  - Picking a personal conflict with no work outcome.
  - Criticizing a former colleague or employer.
order: 5
---

Interviewers ask this to see **how** you disagree, not whether you win. Use **STAR** (Situation, Task, Action, Result) and keep it under two minutes.

**Example answer:**

> **Situation:** In a food delivery app, I found that the "Reorder" button added items even if they were no longer on the restaurant's menu. The developer said it was fine, because checkout would show an error later.
>
> **Task:** I thought users would be confused and lose trust, and I needed to raise that without turning it into an argument.
>
> **Action:** First I asked him to explain his reasoning, and I learned the menu API was slow, so checking availability on Reorder would add a delay. Then I showed him a short recording of the user experience: three items added, then an error at payment. I suggested we ask the product owner, since it was really a product decision. We brought both views to her in five minutes, with the trade-off clearly stated.
>
> **Result:** She chose a middle ground: check availability in the background and show a message on the cart. The developer and I worked well together after that, and I learned to ask about technical constraints *before* pushing my view.

**What makes this work**

- You **listened first** and understood the other side.
- You used **evidence**, not opinions.
- You moved the decision to the **right person**.
- You ended with a **result and a lesson**.

If you're new to testing, a disagreement from a school project, a previous job or volunteer work is fine. Just make it real.
