---
question: How do you learn a new product or domain quickly when you join a project?
short: Use the product like a real user, read whatever exists (docs, tickets, support questions), talk to the people who know it, build a simple map of features and risks, and start testing small things early. Asking good questions is part of the learning.
category: behavioral
level: junior
lookingFor: A proactive, structured learning approach, curiosity, comfort with asking questions, and the ability to become useful quickly rather than waiting to "know everything".
pitfalls:
  - Waiting for formal training before doing anything.
  - Being afraid to ask questions.
  - Reading documents for days without touching the product.
order: 8
---

I mix **using**, **reading** and **asking**, and I start contributing early.

**1. Use the product like a customer**

I sign up, complete the main flows, and try the main job of the app: order food, transfer money, book a trip. I note anything confusing; a fresh pair of eyes notices things the team stopped seeing.

**2. Read what exists**

- User stories and recent tickets, especially bugs: they show where the product breaks.
- Support tickets and app store reviews: they show what users struggle with.
- Any docs, designs, API documentation and the glossary of domain terms.

**3. Talk to people**

Short chats with a developer, the product owner and someone from support. Good questions: "What are the most important flows? What breaks most often? What would be the worst bug for us?"

**4. Build a simple map**

A list or mind map of features, user types, integrations and risky areas. It becomes my guide for testing and grows over time.

**5. Start testing small things**

I pick a small story or do a focused exploratory session in a low-risk area. Testing is one of the fastest ways to learn how a product really works.

**For a new domain** like banking or insurance, I also learn the key terms and rules (for example, what a pending transaction is), because many bugs only become visible once you know how things *should* work.
