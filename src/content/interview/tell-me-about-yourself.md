---
question: Tell me about yourself.
short: Give a 60 to 90 second answer in three parts. Present (who you are and what you're doing toward QA), past (one relevant strength with a concrete example) and future (what you want and why this role).
category: behavioral
level: junior
lookingFor: Clear, concise communication, a career story that makes sense, and a link between your background and testing. It also gives them threads to ask about next.
pitfalls:
  - Reciting your whole CV or life story in chronological order.
  - Talking for five minutes, or giving a two-sentence answer.
  - Opening with an apology such as "I don't really have experience, but...".
  - Sounding memorized word for word.
order: 0
---

Use **present, past, future**, and aim for about **60 to 90 seconds**.

- **Present:** who you are now and what you're doing toward testing.
- **Past:** one or two things from your background that make you a good tester, with a concrete example.
- **Future:** what you're looking for and why this role or company.

**Example answer (career changer):**

> I'm a customer support specialist at a travel booking company, and for the last six months I've been training to become a manual tester. I finished a testing course and a practice project where I tested a food delivery app end to end: a test plan, test cases, bug reports and a release recommendation.
>
> In support, a big part of my day is reproducing problems customers report and writing clear tickets for developers. Last year I noticed that several failed-booking complaints came from older Android phones. I collected the details, and the team found a bug in the payment screen. That's when I realized I enjoy finding the cause more than closing the ticket.
>
> Now I'm looking for a junior QA role in a product team. Your app is used by people on the go, so mobile quality really matters, and I like that your testers work inside the development teams.

**Tips**

- Practice from three bullet points, not a script, so it sounds natural.
- Choose the past example on purpose: it's the thread they're most likely to ask about next.
- End with the role, then stop. A short pause is fine.
