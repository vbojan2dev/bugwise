There's no universal right number: junior QA salaries vary a lot by country, city, company and industry. What matters is that you've **done your research** and can answer calmly.

**1. Prepare before any call**

- Look at job ads that publish salary ranges. In some countries and US states employers must share a range in the ad or before the interview.
- Check salary surveys or comparison sites for your country, and official statistics where available.
- Ask testers in local communities, or recruiters, who usually know the market.
- Decide your realistic range and your minimum, and whether you mean gross or net, monthly or yearly.

**2. Early in the process, ask for their range**

> I'd like to understand the role a bit better first. Could you share the range you've budgeted for this position?

**3. If they insist, give your researched range**

> Based on what I've seen for junior QA roles in [city], I'm looking at something between [X] and [Y], depending on the full package and the growth opportunities.

**4. Be honest**

- Never invent a current salary or a competing offer.
- In some places employers aren't allowed to ask about salary history. You can still politely decline: "I'd rather focus on what's fair for this role."
- If an offer is lower than your research suggests, you can ask once, politely, with reasons.
