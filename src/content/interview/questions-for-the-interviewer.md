---
question: Do you have any questions for us?
short: Always say yes and ask two or three genuine questions about the work, the team and the next steps, for example how testers and developers work together, what a great first three months look like, and how releases are tested.
category: behavioral
level: junior
lookingFor: Real interest in the role and in how quality works in the team, plus the judgment to ask about things that matter for doing the job well.
pitfalls:
  - Saying "No, I think you covered everything."
  - Asking something the job ad or website already answers.
  - Leading with vacation days or perks in the first technical interview.
order: 16
---

Always have **two or three questions** ready, and adapt them to who you're talking to. The answers also help you decide if you want the job.

**About the work**

- What does a typical week look like for a tester here?
- How do testers and developers work together? When does QA first see a feature?
- Is testing mostly manual, automated or both? Is there a path toward automation for someone interested?
- How are releases tested, and who decides whether something ships?

**About the team and growth**

- How many testers are on the team, and who would I learn from?
- What would a great first three months look like in this role?
- What's the biggest quality challenge the team has right now?

**About the process**

- What are the next steps, and when can I expect to hear back?

**Match the question to the person**

| Interviewer | Good topics |
| --- | --- |
| Recruiter | Process, timeline, team structure, salary range |
| Tester or QA lead | Test process, tools, releases, learning |
| Developer or product owner | Collaboration, how bugs are handled, what they value in a tester |
| Manager | Expectations, growth, how success is measured |

Benefits and vacation are fair questions, best asked with the recruiter or at the offer stage.
