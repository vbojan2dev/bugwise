---
question: Why should we hire you when you have no testing experience?
short: Agree calmly, then show evidence. Point to the testing work you've done (a practice project, bug reports, a portfolio), the transferable skills from your past roles, and how quickly you learn. No apologies.
category: behavioral
level: junior
lookingFor: Confidence without arrogance, honest self-assessment, concrete evidence of testing skill, and a sense that you'll learn fast and communicate well.
pitfalls:
  - Apologizing or saying you "only" did a course.
  - Arguing that you actually have lots of experience, or overstating practice work.
  - Answering with adjectives ("I'm a fast learner, very motivated") and no evidence.
order: 15
---

Use four steps:

1. **Agree honestly.** Don't argue with the facts.
2. **Reframe with real testing work** you've done, and offer to show it.
3. **Connect transferable experience** from past roles.
4. **Show how you learn,** with a fact rather than an adjective.

**Example answer:**

> That's right, I haven't worked as a tester yet. But I've done the work of testing on a complete practice project: I planned the release by risk, wrote test cases, filed bug reports with steps and evidence, and wrote a go/no-go recommendation. It's all in my portfolio, and I'd be happy to walk you through one bug report.
>
> In my five years in customer support I reproduced customer problems and wrote tickets developers could act on every day, so the communication side of testing isn't new to me.
>
> I learned all of this in six months while working full-time, and I'd bring the same approach to learning your product.

**Why it works**

- It's honest, so there's nothing to catch in follow-up questions.
- It replaces the missing job title with **evidence** they can check.
- It ends with an offer to show work, which moves the conversation to your strengths.
